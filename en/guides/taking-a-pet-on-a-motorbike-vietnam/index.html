Taking a pet on a motorbike in Vietnam: what the law says and how to do it safely

Can you carry a dog or cat on a motorbike in Vietnam? What the law says about leading animals while riding, bulky loads and load limits, the fines in Decree 168, and how to carry a pet safely.

In Vietnam you will see a dog standing on a scooter's floorboard, a cat in a basket strapped to the rack, even a rider walking a dog on a lead from the saddle. For expatriates and long-stay visitors with a pet, the question comes up quickly: may an animal ride on a motorbike at all, and how? The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) does not have a chapter on pets, but several of its motorbike rules apply directly, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines both rider and passenger for breaking them. This guide explains what is banned, what the law does not address, the fines, and how to carry a small animal as safely as possible. For the wider safety picture, see our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law bans

The key rule is in Article 33(3)(đ) of Law 36/2024/QH15. A motorbike or moped rider may not use the vehicle to tow or push other vehicles or objects, may not lead animals while riding, and may not carry bulky items or load the bike beyond the set limits. "Leading animals" (dẫn dắt vật nuôi) covers the picture of a rider holding a lead while a dog runs alongside: it is banned outright.

For the person on the back, Article 33(4)(a) bans carrying bulky items. A passenger who holds a large dog in their arms, or a big carrier on their lap, risks falling under that rule, and they cannot hold on properly at the same time.

What the law does not address

Article 33 does not mention carrying an animal in a container. What it does regulate is the size of any load. Under Article 33(5) of Law 36/2024/QH15, a load may not stick out more than 0.3 metres beyond either side of the luggage rack or more than 0.5 metres behind it, measured from the rack as designed by the manufacturer, and may not be higher than 2 metres from the road surface. A pet carrier strapped to the rack has to stay within those limits like any other load.

There is also no weight figure in the law. For weight, follow the motorbike's owner's manual and any limit marked on the rack. Our guide on luggage and load limits explains the rules for loads in general.

The fines

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines each person on the bike for their own conduct:

  • The rider: leading an animal, carrying bulky items, holding on to or towing another vehicle, or carrying a person standing on the seat or rack, is fined VND 600,000 to 800,000 under Article 7(3)(e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • The passenger: leading an animal or carrying bulky items, among other acts, is fined VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Article 12(5)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

These are the penalties that name the acts directly. Whether a particular carrier counts as bulky is a judgement made at the roadside, so keep the carrier small, secure and within the size limits of Article 33(5).

| Situation | Rule | Fine | |---|---|---| | Rider leads a dog on a lead while riding | Law 36, Article 33(3)(đ) | VND 600,000–800,000 for the rider (Decree 168, Article 7(3)(e)) | | Passenger leads an animal or holds a bulky carrier | Law 36, Article 33(4)(a) | VND 400,000–600,000 for the passenger (Decree 168, Article 12(5)(a)) | | Carrier sticks out beyond the rack limits | Law 36, Article 33(5) | Load limits apply to any load |

Carrying a small pet safely

The law sets the minimum. For the animal's sake and yours, these habits matter more:

  • Use a closed, ventilated carrier. A hard pet carrier with a secure door keeps the animal from jumping out at a junction. Line it with a towel so it does not slide around inside.
  • Strap it to the rack, not to a person. Fix the carrier upright with at least two crossed straps or bungee cords and check them after the first kilometres. Keep strap ends away from the rear wheel, chain or belt, brakes and exhaust, and leave the tail light and plate visible.
  • Keep the animal off your feet and the floorboard. A dog standing between the rider's feet can move suddenly, push against the rider's legs or slip off, and you will not be able to react in time.
  • Do not hold an animal in your arms. A frightened dog or cat can twist free. A passenger who holds an animal has no hand left to hold on with.
  • Mind the heat. A carrier in the sun heats up quickly. Ride early or late, give water before and after, and never leave the animal in a carrier on a parked bike in the sun. Our guide to riding in extreme heat covers the signs of heat stress in people; animals suffer sooner.
  • Keep trips short and smooth. Brake early and gently, take corners slowly and avoid rough roads. Extra weight high on the rack changes how the bike handles; our guide on how to brake on a scooter explains why.
  • Cover the carrier in rain. A light plastic cover that leaves the air holes open keeps the animal dry; see our guide to riding in heavy rain.

For longer distances, a car or taxi is usually kinder to the animal than a motorbike.

Carrying your own pet is a different question from dealing with dogs, buffalo and chickens on rural roads. That situation, including when the law requires you to slow down for animals, is explained in our guide to animals on the road.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to carry my dog on a scooter in Vietnam? The law does not address animals in carriers as such. It bans leading an animal while riding and carrying bulky items (Article 33(3)(đ) of Law 36/2024/QH15), and any load must stay within the size limits of Article 33(5). A small, secured carrier on the rack keeps you clearly away from those bans.

Can I walk my dog beside the scooter on a lead? No. Leading an animal while riding is banned (Article 33(3)(đ)) and fined for the rider under Article 7(3)(e) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Does a pet count as a passenger? No. The passenger rules in Article 33(1) concern people; an animal is carried as a load. How many people may ride is explained in our guide on how many people can ride one motorbike.

Can my passenger hold a small cat in a bag? A small bag that does not get in the way is common, but the passenger must still hold on, and bulky items are banned for passengers (Article 33(4)(a)). A carrier strapped to the rack is the safer choice.

Summary

Vietnamese law bans leading an animal while riding and carrying bulky items, for riders (Article 33(3)(đ) of Law 36/2024/QH15) and passengers (Article 33(4)(a)), and every load has to fit within 0.3 m each side of the rack, 0.5 m behind it and 2 m in height (Article 33(5)). The fines are VND 600,000 to 800,000 for the rider and VND 400,000 to 600,000 for the passenger (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Articles 7 and 12).

Use a closed carrier strapped to the rack, keep trips short and cool, and check the rack and straps before you set off with our rental bike checklist.

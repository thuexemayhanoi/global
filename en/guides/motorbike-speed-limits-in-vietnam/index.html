Motorbike speed limits in Vietnam: towns, rural roads and mopeds

Vietnam's default speed limits for motorbikes and mopeds under Circular 38/2024, how signs change them, the safe following distances and the Decree 168 fines for riding too fast.

Speed limit signs are not posted on every street in Vietnam, so riders need to know the default limits that apply when no sign says otherwise. Those defaults depend on three things: whether you are inside a built-up area, what kind of road you are on and whether your bike counts legally as a motorbike or a moped. This guide explains the limits in Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, the duty to ride slower than the limit when conditions call for it, the safe following distances and the fines in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For riding in the city generally, see Hanoi traffic for first-time riders and the hub Safer Riding and Breakdown Support in Hanoi.

Motorbike or moped: which one are you riding?

The limits differ by vehicle type, and the law draws the line clearly. Under Article 34 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, a moped is a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, for a combustion engine, a capacity of no more than 50 cm³, or for an electric motor, a power of no more than 4 kW. Anything above that is a motorbike. Most rental scooters in Hanoi, such as 110 cc or 125 cc automatics, are therefore motorbikes in the legal sense, while 50 cc scooters and low-power electric scooters are mopeds.

The default limits

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025, sets the maximum speeds that apply where no sign shows a different value:

  • Inside a built-up area, motorbikes: 60 km/h on dual carriageways and one-way roads with two or more motor-vehicle lanes; 50 km/h on two-way roads without a central divider and one-way roads with a single motor-vehicle lane (Article 6(1)).
  • Outside built-up areas, motorbikes: 70 km/h on dual carriageways and one-way roads with two or more lanes; 60 km/h on two-way roads and one-way roads with one lane (Article 6(2)).
  • Mopeds, wherever they ride: 40 km/h (Article 7).

A built-up area begins at the sign marking the start of a residential area and ends at the sign marking its end (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, Article 3). In practice, almost all of Hanoi's urban districts are built-up areas, so a motorbike's default ceiling in the city is 50 or 60 km/h, and often much less because of traffic.

Signs override the defaults

The defaults only apply where no speed sign is posted. Under Article 10 of the circular, road authorities can post lower limits for particular sections, for certain times of day and for particular vehicle types, for example on narrow urban streets with limited visibility or on mountain sections with tight bends and steep gradients. When a sign shows a lower number, the sign wins. Watch for the round signs with a red border and a black number, and keep to the lower figure until you are sure the restriction has ended. On rental bikes the speedometer can be slightly off, so leave yourself a small margin below the posted figure.

Expressways have their own limits of up to 120 km/h, but they are irrelevant for riders: Article 25(3) of the law bars motorbikes and mopeds from expressways altogether.

The limit is a ceiling, not a target

Article 12 of the law requires riders to keep a speed suited to the road, the traffic density, the terrain and the weather, and to slow down or stop where safety requires it. The list in Article 12(3) includes pedestrian crossings, junctions, bends, mountain passes and steep slopes, narrow bridges, areas around schools, hospitals and markets, built-up areas, road works, and rain, fog or slippery surfaces. Riding at the posted limit through a crowded market street can still be a violation of this duty.

Keeping a safe distance

Article 11 of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets minimum following distances on dry, straight, level roads with good visibility: 35 metres at 60 km/h and 55 metres between 60 and 80 km/h. Below 60 km/h, riders must keep a distance suited to the traffic, and in rain, fog, on slippery surfaces, winding roads or mountain passes they must leave more room than the table requires. Where a sign shows a minimum distance between vehicles, you keep at least that distance.

What speeding costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP grades fines for motorbike and moped riders in Article 7 by how far over the limit you were:

  • 5 to under 10 km/h over: VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Article 7(2)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • 10 to 20 km/h over: VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 under Article 7(4)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • More than 20 km/h over: VND 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 under Article 7(8)(a), plus 4 points off the licence under Article 7(13)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Riding in a group of two or more vehicles that exceed the limit carries VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 under Article 7(9)(b), and speeding that causes a traffic accident carries VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 under Article 7(10) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Practical speeds for visitors

In Hanoi traffic, the legal limit is rarely the constraint. Dense traffic, sudden lane changes and pedestrians crossing between vehicles mean that a comfortable speed is usually well below it. Out of the city, mountain roads in the north are steep and winding, and trucks and buses share the lanes. A sensible approach for visitors is to ride at a speed at which you could stop within the distance you can see clearly ahead, to slow down before every bend rather than in it, and to add margin in the rain.

Remember that a moped's 40 km/h limit applies on every road, including fast suburban ones, so a 50 cc scooter or a low-power electric scooter is not suited to longer trips. Our comparison of 50cc and electric scooters explains the trade-offs.

In short

Without a sign, a motorbike may ride at up to 50 or 60 km/h in built-up areas and 60 or 70 km/h outside them, while mopeds are limited to 40 km/h everywhere (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT). Signs can set lower limits, the law requires you to slow down whenever conditions demand it, and fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP rise from VND 400,000 to VND 8,000,000 and licence points.

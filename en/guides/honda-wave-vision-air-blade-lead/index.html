Honda Wave, Vision, Air Blade and Lead Explained

What separates the Honda Wave, Vision, Air Blade and Lead at rental shops in Vietnam: gears or scooter, engine size and licence class, storage, weight and features, from Honda's own data.

Ask for a motorbike in Hanoi and you will hear model names rather than categories: a Wave, a Vision, an Air Blade, a Lead. All four are Honda models built for the Vietnamese market, and they stand for quite different kinds of bike. This guide explains what each name means in practice, using the specifications Honda Vietnam publishes for its current models. It does not say which models a particular rental shop has, and rental fleets often include older model years, so features can differ from the bike in front of you. For the bigger picture, see our topic page on choosing a rental motorbike.

Two families: geared bikes and scooters

Honda Vietnam sorts its motorbikes into scooters ("xe tay ga"), geared underbones ("xe số"), manual bikes with a hand clutch ("xe côn tay"), large-capacity motorcycles and electric models (Honda Vietnam). Three of the names in this guide are scooters: you twist the throttle and go. The Wave sits in Honda's geared category, not among the hand-clutch bikes: you change gear yourself, but there is no clutch lever to work. If you are unsure which type suits you, our comparison of manual and automatic rentals explains the difference.

Honda Wave Alpha: the geared everyday bike

The Wave Alpha 110 is Honda Vietnam's basic geared model. According to Honda's specifications, it has a 109.2 cm³ single-cylinder, air-cooled four-stroke engine with 6.12 kW, a mechanical four-speed rotary gearbox and an electric starter (Honda Vietnam, Wave Alpha). It weighs 96 kg, the seat is 770 mm high, the fuel tank holds 3.7 litres and Honda gives a fuel consumption of 1.72 litres per 100 km. It runs on 17-inch wheels, the largest of the four models here. Further details: a three-in-one lock that combines the ignition, a steering lock and a magnetic lock, and a headlight that is always on while the engine runs.

What that means for a renter: the Wave is light and simple, but you have to shift gears with your foot. If you have only ridden scooters, practise in a quiet place before you join city traffic.

Honda Vision: the small, light scooter

The Vision has been part of Honda Vietnam's scooter range since 2011. The 2026 version has a 110 cc single-cylinder, air-cooled eSP engine with electronic fuel injection, an ACG starter and an idling stop system that switches the engine off briefly when you stand still (Honda Vietnam, Vision 2026). Honda lists a Smart Key system, a large storage compartment under the seat and a front pocket with a lid and a USB Type-C charging port; the headlight stays on while you ride. The Sport version has a 16-inch front wheel.

What that means for a renter: the Vision is the compact choice for short city trips. Ask the shop how the idling stop works on your bike, so a silent engine at a red light does not catch you out.

Honda Air Blade: sporty, and check the badge

The Air Blade comes in two engine sizes, 160 and 125, and Honda markets it as a mid-range sporty scooter (Honda Vietnam, 2026 models). For the Air Blade 160, Honda's specifications list a 156.9 cm³ liquid-cooled single-cylinder engine with 11.2 kW, a continuously variable belt transmission, a weight of 113 kg, a seat height of 775 mm, a 4.4-litre tank and 14-inch wheels (Honda Vietnam, Air Blade). The under-seat compartment holds 23.2 litres, enough for two half-face helmets, and has a light and a USB Type-C port under a waterproof cover. A SMART Key fob can lock and unlock the bike remotely, help you find it and trigger an alarm. ABS on the front wheel is listed on the Air Blade product page, and for 2026 Honda added it to the Sport version of the Air Blade 125 (Honda Vietnam, 2026 models).

What that means for a renter: check which version you are given. The engine size decides which licence you need, as the section on licences below explains.

Honda Lead: the practical scooter with the big boot

The Lead is Honda's practical family scooter. The current LEAD ABS has a 124.8 cm³ liquid-cooled eSP+ engine with 8.22 kW and a belt drive; it weighs 114 kg, its seat is 760 mm high and its tank holds 6.0 litres (Honda Vietnam, LEAD ABS). Its wheels are the smallest of the four: 12 inches at the front and 10 at the rear. The highlight is storage: Honda states more than 37 litres under the seat, enough for two large full-face helmets, with a light and a divider, plus a front pocket that takes a 500 ml bottle and has a USB Type-C port. The Special version has ABS on the front wheel, and every version has a Smart Key with remote locking and a locate function.

What that means for a renter: the Lead suits riders who carry bags or want to lock two helmets away, and its low seat helps shorter riders.

The four at a glance

  • Wave Alpha 110: geared, 109.2 cm³, 96 kg, 17-inch wheels, 3.7-litre tank (Honda Vietnam).
  • Vision: scooter, 110 cc, Smart Key, front pocket with USB-C (Honda Vietnam).
  • Air Blade 160: scooter, 156.9 cm³, 113 kg, 14-inch wheels, 23.2-litre storage (Honda Vietnam).
  • LEAD ABS: scooter, 124.8 cm³, 114 kg, 12- and 10-inch wheels, over 37 litres of storage (Honda Vietnam).

Which licence you need

Vietnamese law sets the licence class by engine size. Under Article 57(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, class A1 covers motorbikes up to 125 cm³ and class A anything larger. On Honda's figures, the Wave Alpha, the Vision and the LEAD are within the A1 limit, as is the Air Blade 125. The Air Blade 160, with 156.9 cm³, needs class A. Visitors riding on a foreign licence need the matching category on their documents; our guides to foreign driving licences and International Driving Permits explain the rules.

Which one suits your trip?

  • Short city trips, first time on a scooter: a Vision or a Lead, both twist-and-go.
  • Luggage or two helmets: the Lead, with the largest storage compartment of the four.
  • Longer rides out of town: an Air Blade; the 160 version has the largest engine of the four, but check that your licence covers it.
  • Rural roads and a geared bike you can handle: the Wave, with the largest wheels here.

Whatever you choose, check the bike at pickup: brakes, lights, horn, tyres and mirrors. Our rental pickup checklist lists what to look at, and our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down helps if something goes wrong.

In short

The Wave is a light geared bike with a four-speed rotary gearbox; the Vision, Air Blade and Lead are scooters (Honda Vietnam). The Vision is the compact one, the Lead has the most storage, and the Air Blade is the sporty choice, but the 160 version exceeds the 125 cm³ limit of licence class A1 under Article 57(1). Rental bikes are often older model years, so check the actual bike before you ride.

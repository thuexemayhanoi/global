Railway Crossings in Vietnam on a Motorbike

How to cross railway lines on a motorbike in Vietnam: warning signs, when to stop with and without barriers, what is banned nearby, the fines and what to do if you stall on the tracks.

The North–South railway and the lines around Hanoi cross countless roads, many of them in residential streets or on country roads that visitors ride on rental motorbikes. Some crossings have barriers, bells and flashing lights; others have only a warning sign. A train cannot swerve and needs a very long distance to stop, so railway crossings are places where care matters more than speed. This guide explains the rules in Article 24 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the warning signs in QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, what is banned around crossings and what to do if your bike stalls on the tracks. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The warning signs

Level crossings are announced by warning signs. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, sign W.210 "Giao nhau với đường sắt có rào chắn" stands before crossings with barriers and sign W.211 "Giao nhau với đường sắt không có rào chắn" before crossings without them. At crossings without barriers, sign W.242 is added, placed 10 metres before the outer rail. When you see any of them, ease off and get ready to stop.

Many crossings also have alternately flashing red lights. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, all vehicles must stop while they are lit and may continue only once they go out; a bell or an announcement that a train is coming often sounds as well.

Crossings with barriers

Article 24(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety requires you to stop on the right-hand side of your road, before the stop line, when:

  • the crossing keeper signals,
  • the red light flashes,
  • the bell rings, or
  • the barrier is moving or already closed.

A barrier that is coming down is not a signal to dash through. Wait until it is fully open and the lights have gone out.

Crossings without barriers

At crossings with no keeper, barrier, bell or light, Article 24(2) applies: stop on the right before the stop line, look both ways and cross only when no train is coming. Keep the engine running so you can cross briskly, lift your visor for a moment if you need to hear a train's horn, and look well along the line in both directions. Trains arrive faster than they seem to, and crossing the tracks on a motorbike takes longer than you expect.

Waiting at a closed barrier

Motorbikes quickly bunch up at a closed barrier. Wait on the right-hand side of your road before the stop line, as Article 24(1) requires. Do not pull out to the left past waiting vehicles: that would be overtaking at a level crossing, and anyone on the wrong side blocks the oncoming traffic when the barrier lifts. Never squeeze under a half-closed barrier. When it opens, move off calmly and leave enough space ahead that nobody has to stop on the tracks.

What is banned around crossings

Several rules apply around level crossings:

  • No overtaking: Article 14(6)(đ) bans overtaking at level crossings.
  • No U-turns: Article 15(4) bans turning round at level crossings.
  • No reversing: Article 16(2) bans reversing there.
  • No stopping or parking: Article 18(4)(m) bans stopping and parking within the railway safety zone.

Article 12(3)(d) also requires you to watch and slow down or stop at level crossings.

What it costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets these fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds, among others:

  • Stopping or parking against the rules at a level crossing or within the railway safety zone: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • U-turn in a prohibited place: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(k) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Overtaking where it is banned: 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND under Article 7(4)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

If your bike stalls on the tracks

For emergencies, Article 24(3) of the law sets a clear duty: if a vehicle breaks down or crashes on a crossing, or its load falls onto the tracks, and it cannot be moved out of the railway safety zone at once, the driver and everyone present must immediately signal to stop the train and take safety measures. On a motorbike that means:

1. Do not keep trying to restart the engine. Push the bike off the tracks quickly. 2. If you cannot, get yourself and any passenger to safety first, away from the rails. 3. Shout to people at the crossing and other road users so that they help warn the train. 4. Then call the rental shop; our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains the next steps.

A motorbike can be replaced; a person cannot. Never stay on the tracks to save the bike.

Practical tips

  • Slow and straight: cross the rails as close to a right angle as you can, without braking or accelerating hard, so your tyres do not slip into a groove.
  • Careful in the wet: wet rails are slippery, doubly so in the rainy season; see our guide to riding in heavy rain.
  • Never stop on the rails: if traffic backs up beyond the crossing, wait before the tracks until there is room on the other side.
  • No earphones: a train often announces itself by sound, and riders with music on notice too late.
  • Secure your luggage: loose bags can fall off as you cross the rails, and a load on the tracks brings the same warning duty as a breakdown under Article 24(3).

Frequently asked questions

The barrier is up but the lights are still flashing. May I cross? No. Under Article 24(1) a flashing red light or a ringing bell is a reason to stop on its own, whatever the barrier is doing. Wait until the lights go out and the bell stops.

Is it enough to look once before crossing an unguarded line? Look both ways before you move, and again just before the rails, as Article 24(2) requires you to cross only when no train is coming. Bends and buildings can hide a train until the last moment.

In short

When the red light flashes, the bell rings or the barrier moves, stop before the line (Article 24(1)). At crossings without barriers, stop, look both ways and cross only when no train is coming. Do not overtake, turn round, reverse or stop on the tracks. Fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP reach 1,000,000 VND, and in an emergency people come first and the train must be warned.

Taking the A1 Motorbike Test in Vietnam as a Foreigner

How foreigners living in Vietnam can sit the A1 motorbike test: who is eligible, the documents and how the theory test and riding course work.

Many foreigners who stay in Vietnam for longer want to ride legally, but not everyone has a licence that can simply be converted. For them the route is the same as for Vietnamese learners: a training course, a theory test and a riding test for class A1. The rules are set by the Ministry of Public Security in Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, which came into force on 1 July 2026, and some of its test content only applies from 2027 and 2028. This guide explains who may take the test, which documents you need, how the test works and what changes when. Our topic page on rental documents and contracts gives an overview of the paperwork around riding a rental bike.

Do you need the test at all?

Under Article 57(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, class A1 covers motorbikes up to 125 cm³ or electric motorbikes up to 11 kW, and class A covers larger machines. Before you sign up for a course, check whether you can avoid the test:

  • You hold a valid national licence and live in Vietnam. Foreigners who reside, work or study in Vietnam may be able to convert their licence without a test under Article 24 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA. Our guide to converting a foreign licence explains that route.
  • You are visiting for a short time. An International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, carried together with your national licence for the matching class, is the usual option under Article 57(8); see our guide to International Driving Permits for motorbikes.

The test route is for people who cannot use either option, for example because their home licence does not cover motorbikes.

Who can sit the A1 test

Article 15 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA sets two conditions for every candidate. You must meet the age and health requirements of Article 59 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, which sets the minimum age for classes A1 and A at 18 (Law 36/2024/QH15). And a driving training institution must confirm that you have completed a driving course, or you must hold a certificate of completion. In other words, you cannot simply book a test: you go through a registered driving school.

For foreigners, Article 16(2)(c) adds a residence requirement: the file must include a copy of a diplomatic or official identity card, a visa or temporary residence card that is valid for at least 90 more days, or a valid permanent residence card. This requirement is aimed at foreigners who stay longer; a short tourist visa with less than 90 days left does not meet it. Under Article 19(3), people who have not yet settled an open traffic violation case are not admitted to a test.

The documents

Your personal file under Article 16(2) of the circular contains:

1. the certificate or confirmation that you completed the training course; 2. a valid driver's health certificate, normally in electronic form, or on paper from a qualified medical facility if the system fails; 3. a copy or electronic copy of your diplomatic or official ID, visa, temporary residence card or permanent residence card; if you submit a copy that is not certified, you must show the original; 4. if you are retaking the test, the record of your earlier results.

The driving school submits the request for a test session together with the list of candidates and the personal files (Article 16(1)).

How the application works

Under Article 9(1), the electronic file is submitted online through the National Public Service Portal, the national e-ID app or police apps at least seven working days before the test session; if the system fails, it goes directly to the Traffic Police Division. At least two working days before the session, the Traffic Police Division informs candidates of the time and place through the portal, the e-ID account or another suitable channel (Article 9(5)).

What the test consists of

The motorbike test runs in a fixed order under Article 4(5)(a): the theory test, then riding test no. 1, then riding test no. 2 on the test course. The theory questions cover road traffic law, riding technique, rules of conduct and traffic culture, and preventing the harm caused by alcohol in traffic; the test is taken on a computer (Article 4(1)). Candidates who already hold a valid car licence are exempt from the theory test for A1 and A.

Each stage is a gate. If you fail the theory test, you may not take riding test no. 1; if you fail riding test no. 1, you may not take riding test no. 2 (Article 12(1)). A theory pass stays valid for one year, and failed candidates can register for another session. Taking documents, a mobile phone or other devices into the theory room or onto the test bike leads to suspension and cancellation of the result (Article 12(4)).

Which test format applies when

This is where timing matters. Under Article 41(2), the new test content in Appendix I applies from 1 March 2027, and riding test no. 2 in its new form only from 1 January 2028. Until 28 February 2027, parts of the previous Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA stay in force, among them its Article 4(1) to (3) and Appendix II (Article 41(6)), and candidates who fail a part tested under the old circular retake it under the old rules until then (Article 42(2)). If your test falls before March 2027, ask your driving school which format you will face.

From 1 March 2027, Appendix I of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA sets out the test like this:

  • Theory: 40 questions in 27 minutes, one point each; you pass with at least 36 correct answers.
  • Riding test no. 1: a figure of eight, a straight line, a lane with obstacle lines and a rough surface, in that order and within three minutes. You start with 100 points and need at least 80. Five points are deducted, for example, for not adjusting the mirrors before starting, for each missed indicator, for each time a wheel touches a boundary line, for each time a foot touches the ground and for each stall. Not looking back over both shoulders before you start, not wearing the helmet with the strap fastened, leaving the sequence, both wheels leaving the course or dropping the bike ends the test at once.
  • Riding test no. 2 (from 2028): eleven situations over two laps within five minutes, including joining a main road, turning, a red light, a U-turn, giving way to pedestrians, overtaking at more than 20 km/h, an emergency stop from at least 20 km/h within two metres of the line and a railway crossing. Elbow and knee protectors are compulsory, and the pass mark is again 80 of 100 points.

A note on language

The only special arrangement in the circular for candidates who cannot read Vietnamese is a paper theory test with an interpreter for members of ethnic minorities (Article 14). The text we checked does not describe tests in foreign languages, so ask the driving school before you enrol how the theory test works for you.

After you pass

The Traffic Police Department issues an electronic licence and adds it to the e-data system within two hours of the end of the session; if you want a printed PET card, it is printed and handed out within 3.5 working days (Article 19(1)). An A1 licence has no expiry date under Article 57(5)(a) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety.

Practical tips for the riding test

  • Practise slow control. The figure of eight and the narrow lanes reward low, steady speed with both hands on the bars and both feet on the footrests, as Appendix I requires (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA).
  • Make shoulder checks a habit. Looking back over both shoulders before moving off is not a formality on the test; forgetting it ends the attempt.
  • Signal every time. A missed indicator costs five points each time in both riding tests (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA).
  • Leave your phone outside. Devices in the test room or on the bike cost you the result.

While you prepare, our guides to road signs and speed limits cover topics that also come up in the theory test.

In short

Foreigners who live in Vietnam and cannot convert a licence can take the A1 test after a course at a driving school, with a health certificate and a visa or residence card valid for at least 90 more days (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA). The test has a theory part and riding tests on a closed course; the new format applies from 1 March 2027, with the second riding test from 2028. After a pass, the electronic licence is issued within two hours. Once you ride, our rental pickup checklist helps you check a rental bike before you set off.

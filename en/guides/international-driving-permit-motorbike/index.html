International Driving Permits for Motorbikes in Vietnam

What Vietnam requires of an international driving permit for a motorbike: the 1968 model, category A, validity and which countries' permits are refused.

Most travellers who plan to rent a scooter in Hanoi have heard that they need an "international driving permit", usually shortened to IDP. What fewer people realise is that Vietnam does not accept every booklet sold under that name. The model printed on the cover, the motorcycle category stamped inside and the dates on the first page all decide whether the permit actually lets you ride. This guide explains what Vietnamese law asks of an IDP, how to read your own permit, and where riders most often get it wrong. For the hand-over itself, use the Hanoi motorbike rental checklist.

What Vietnamese law says about international permits

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, Law 36/2024/QH15, has applied since 1 January 2025. Article 57(8), as summarised by a provincial government legal information portal, sets four rules that matter to a visiting rider:

  • An international driving permit is one issued by the competent authority of a country that is a member of the Vienna Convention, in the uniform model of that convention.
  • It is valid for no more than three years from the date of issue, and never beyond the validity of the national licence it accompanies.
  • A holder driving in Vietnam must carry both the international permit and the national licence, each covering the class of vehicle being driven and both still in date.
  • If the right to use the permit is suspended for a traffic offence, the suspension cannot run longer than the holder's permitted stay in Vietnam.

The phrase "the class of vehicle being driven" is the one that catches motorbike renters. A permit that only covers cars does not cover a scooter, however official it looks.

1949, 1968 and why the cover matters

There are several international permit models in circulation. The UK government lists three types of IDP and notes that the right one depends on the country you are visiting (GOV.UK). For Vietnam the relevant model is the one created by the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office tells British visitors that they need both the 1968 version of the IDP and their UK licence with them when driving a car or motorbike in Vietnam (FCDO).

Whether your home country can issue that model depends on whether it is a party to the convention. The UN Treaty Collection participant list, checked for this guide on 14 October 2026, shows Viet Nam acceding in 2014. It also lists, among others, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Italy, Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, Israel, the Philippines, Thailand and South Africa as parties. The United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, India, Japan and China do not appear on that list, and Spain and the Republic of Korea appear with a signature only.

Two government sources confirm what that means in practice. The U.S. Department of State says that U.S. driver's licences and International Driving Permits do not work in Vietnam and that renting a moped or motorbike without a Vietnamese licence is illegal. Australia's Smartraveller says you are not allowed to ride on an Australian licence or an IDP issued in Australia, including for motorcycles of 50 cc or more.

There is one documented exception to the convention rule. Viet Nam and the Republic of Korea signed an agreement on mutual recognition of international driving permits in June 2023. Under it, Korean citizens with a Korean-issued permit in category A may drive vehicles of that category for non-commercial purposes, for up to one year from entry, as long as the permit is valid and presented with the Korean licence. If you hold a passport from a country that is not a party, check for a bilateral arrangement of this kind with your own government before you travel rather than assuming one exists.

The motorcycle box: category A, A1 and B

An IDP is not a separate qualification. It is a translation of the categories already on your national licence, laid out in a standard format so that police abroad can read it. The 1968 model has a box for each category, and the issuing authority stamps the boxes that match your home licence.

For a motorbike rider the box that matters is A, which covers motorcycles, or its sub-category A1 for light motorcycles. Vietnam's own licence classes (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 57) draw the same line: class A1 covers two-wheelers up to 125 cm³ or an electric motor up to 11 kW, and class A covers anything larger. The models that appear most often in Hanoi rental listings, such as the Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade and Yamaha Sirius, all have engines above 50 cm³, so the A or A1 stamp is what a police officer will look for.

If your national licence only covers cars, your IDP will only show B, and B does not cover a scooter in Vietnam. That is true even if your home country lets car drivers ride a small motorcycle after a short course or on a code added to the car licence. Those national privileges are not categories in the convention sense, and they do not travel. Germany's foreign office, for example, warns its citizens that the extended B licence with code B196 is not recognised in Vietnam.

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, the only motorised two-wheelers in Vietnam that need no licence at all are mopeds below 50 cm³, which are built for low speeds. If you plan to rely on one, ask the shop to confirm the engine size in writing before you pay, because a scooter that looks small can still be a 110 cm³ model.

Validity, dates and how long you are staying

Check three dates on the first page before you leave home. The issue date starts the clock. The expiry date must not be later than three years after issue, and Vietnamese law also caps it at the expiry of your national licence. Finally, compare both with your travel dates: a permit that runs out halfway through a two-week trip leaves you riding without a valid document for the second week.

Your national licence has to stay valid too. If you renew it after getting the IDP, the permit is still tied to the old card, so it is safer to carry the licence whose details are written in the permit, or to apply for a new permit after renewing.

The international permit is designed for visitors. Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, the Ministry of Public Security's 2025 rules on licences, says that foreigners with a national licence who want to drive in Vietnam convert it into the matching Vietnamese class, unless a treaty provides otherwise. Smartraveller similarly notes that converting an Australian licence requires a Vietnamese residence permit valid for at least three months. If you are moving to Hanoi for work or study rather than visiting, conversion is the route to look at, not a fresh IDP every few years.

Counterfeit and "online" permits

Search results for "international driver's licence" are full of companies selling official-looking booklets or digital cards. Vietnamese law recognises only a permit issued by the competent authority of a convention country, and the home licence must still be shown with it. A privately printed document adds nothing: it is not issued by a government and it does not create a category your licence does not already have.

Smartraveller points out that the Australian Automobile Association and its member clubs are the only authorised distributors of IDPs for Australian licence holders and warns about fraudulent competitors. The UK government directs British drivers to get an IDP in person from certain shops with a PayPoint counter. Whatever your nationality, get the permit through the channel your own government names, and treat any website promising a "worldwide licence" without a national licence as a red flag.

What else to carry when you ride

The permit and licence are only part of the documents a rider needs. Article 56 of Law 36/2024, summarised on the government policy portal, also requires the vehicle registration certificate and the compulsory civil liability insurance certificate. With a rental, these belong to the owner, so ask the shop for the originals or the copies they provide and keep them with the bike.

The FCDO adds two warnings that apply to every nationality. It is illegal to drive without third-party insurance, and you should not hand over your passport as a deposit for a hired vehicle or as a guarantee for a fine. Check, too, that your travel insurance covers motorbike riding and the engine size you plan to rent. Smartraveller notes that insurers are likely to reject claims from riders who are unlicensed or hold the wrong class of licence.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ride a scooter in Vietnam on a car licence with a B196 code? No. An IDP only translates the categories on your national licence, so a car-only licence shows B, and B does not cover a scooter in Vietnam. National extras such as a short course or a code added to a car licence are not convention categories and do not travel; Germany's foreign office warns its citizens that the extended B licence with code B196 is not recognised in Vietnam.

Can Korean citizens ride in Vietnam with a Korean international driving permit? Yes, under a bilateral exception. Viet Nam and the Republic of Korea signed an agreement on mutual recognition of international driving permits in June 2023. Korean citizens with a Korean-issued permit in category A may ride vehicles of that category for non-commercial purposes for up to one year from entry, as long as the permit is valid and presented with the Korean licence.

Can I still use my IDP after renewing my national licence? Be careful, because the permit stays tied to the licence whose details are written in it. After a renewal, it is safer to carry that licence with the permit or to apply for a new permit. The national licence must also stay valid, since the permit's validity never runs beyond it, as the provincial government legal information portal summarises.

Before you book a bike

  • Confirm that your country can issue a 1968-model permit, or that a bilateral agreement covers you.
  • Check that your national licence includes motorcycles, and that the IDP has the A or A1 box stamped.
  • Make sure the permit and your licence are both valid for the whole trip.
  • Get the permit from the authority or organisation your government names, not from an online seller.
  • Pack the permit with the physical licence it refers to.
  • Ask the rental shop for the registration and insurance documents before you ride away.
  • If any of this does not fit your situation, choose a taxi, a ride-hailing service or a tour with a driver instead.

Drinking and riding in Vietnam: the alcohol rule for motorbike riders

Vietnam bans riding with any alcohol in your blood or breath. What the law says, how Decree 168 fines and licence suspensions scale with the reading, and safer ways home after a night out.

A cold beer with dinner is part of travelling in Vietnam, and so, for many visitors, is the rental motorbike parked outside. Legally the two do not mix at all: Vietnamese law sets no small permitted amount of alcohol for riders. This guide explains the rule, how the penalties scale with the measured level, what a checkpoint looks like from the saddle, how alcohol affects insurance after a crash and how to get home safely instead. For the wider picture of riding rules, start with Safer Riding and Breakdown Support in Hanoi.

The rule: no alcohol at all

Article 9(2) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety lists among the prohibited acts riding or driving on the road with any alcohol concentration in the blood or breath. The wording sets no threshold, so any measurable alcohol breaks the rule, whether you had one glass or five. Article 9(3) adds a parallel ban on riding with drugs or other prohibited stimulants in the body.

The UK government puts the practical consequence plainly: "Drink-driving is a serious offence in Vietnam. If you are tested and found to have any alcohol in your system, you may get a fine and possible imprisonment" (FCDO safety and security advice). The point to take away is simple: if you plan to drink, plan not to ride.

How the penalties scale with the reading

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which sets road traffic penalties, grades the sanctions for motorbike and moped riders in Article 7 according to the alcohol concentration found:

  • Up to 50 mg per 100 ml of blood or 0.25 mg per litre of breath: a fine of VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 under Article 7(6)(a), plus 4 points off the licence under Article 7(13)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Above 50 and up to 80 mg per 100 ml of blood, or above 0.25 and up to 0.4 mg per litre of breath: a fine of VND 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 under Article 7(8)(b), plus 10 points off under Article 7(13)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Above 80 mg per 100 ml of blood or 0.4 mg per litre of breath: a fine of VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 under Article 7(9)(d), and the right to use the licence is suspended for 22 to 24 months under Article 7(12)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Refusing to take the test does not help. Under Article 7(9)(đ) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, failing to comply with an alcohol check carries the same fine as the top band, VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000, and the same 22 to 24 month suspension.

What a checkpoint looks like

Alcohol checks usually happen at the roadside, where officers wave riders over and ask them to blow into a breath tester. Stop when signalled, switch off the engine, keep your hands visible and follow the instructions. Have your papers ready: the documents a rider should carry are listed in our guide to which papers to carry on a rental bike, and the wider etiquette of a stop is covered in stopped by traffic police on a rental bike. Arguing about how little you drank changes nothing, because the reading decides the band.

Why a foreign licence does not soften anything

The fines in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP are set per rider and per violation, and the decree contains no lighter scale for foreign visitors. A visitor who rides without a licence that is valid in Vietnam adds a second, separate problem on top of the alcohol reading; our guide to foreign licences and motorbike classes explains which licences count.

Alcohol, accidents and insurance

Alcohol changes who pays after a crash. Under Article 7 of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, the compulsory motor liability insurance excludes damage to property caused by a rider whose blood or breath alcohol exceeds the normal level set by the Ministry of Health guidance, so that part of a claim can land on you personally. The same article treats a rider whose licence has been suspended as a rider without a licence, and riding without a valid licence excludes the insurer's liability altogether.

If a crash injures someone, the stakes rise further. The FCDO warns that anyone involved in an accident "could face criminal charges and may need to pay compensation to injured people, even if the injuries are minor", and that foreign nationals have been stopped from leaving Vietnam until the police investigation is complete (FCDO). Our guide to what to do after a motorbike accident covers the first steps, and our guide to travel insurance for motorbike riding lists the questions to ask your insurer, including how it treats a rider who has been drinking.

Two common misunderstandings

"One beer is fine." Not under Vietnamese law. Article 9(2) prohibits riding with any alcohol in the blood or breath, and the lowest band in Decree 168 already starts above zero. Body weight, food or the type of drink change the reading, not the rule.

"My friend can ride us back instead." Only if that friend has not been drinking and holds a licence that is valid for the bike. The ban in Article 9(2) applies to whoever controls the vehicle, and passenger limits still apply: Article 33(1) allows a rider to carry one passenger, with two permitted only in a few listed cases such as a child under 12.

Getting home after a night out

The safest plan is decided before the first drink:

  • Leave the bike where it is. Park it somewhere secure, or at your accommodation, before you head out for the evening.
  • Use a ride-hailing app or a booked taxi. The FCDO recommends pre-arranged transport or official taxi-hailing apps such as Grab or XanhSM, which supply licensed cabs (FCDO).
  • Agree on a sober rider in advance, and make sure that person also holds a licence valid for the bike.
  • Wait until the next day. Alcohol can still show on a breath test the morning after a long evening, so give yourself time before riding again.

In short

Vietnam's rule for riders is zero alcohol. Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP grades the fines from VND 2,000,000 up to 10,000,000 depending on the reading, adds licence points or a suspension of up to 24 months, and treats refusing a test like the highest band. Alcohol can also leave you paying for damage yourself. Ride sober, or let an app take you home. Before your next ride, run through our pickup checklist.

Manual vs Automatic Scooter Rentals in Hanoi

Semi-automatic, automatic scooter or clutch motorbike: how each rides in Hanoi, the manufacturer figures that matter, which licence class you need and which type suits city streets or mountain roads.

Every rental shop in Hanoi asks the same first question: manual or automatic? The honest answer is that Vietnam has three kinds of transmission, not two, and the one the shop calls "manual" is usually not what a European or American rider expects. This guide explains how each type actually rides in Hanoi traffic, which manufacturer figures make a real difference, what licence class each one needs, and which type suits which kind of trip. The handover inspection itself is covered in our Hanoi motorbike rental pickup checklist.

The three transmissions you will actually find in Hanoi

Hanoi rental fleets are sorted by transmission rather than by brand. The published policy of MOTOGO, a Hanoi rental shop, separates its bikes into "xe số", "xe tay ga", "xe côn tay" and "xe điện", meaning semi-automatic, automatic scooter, clutch motorbike and electric scooter, and applies different terms to each category. The semi-automatic is the classic Vietnamese commuter: an underbone such as the Honda Wave Alpha with a foot-operated gear lever but no clutch lever, because a centrifugal clutch does that work for you. The automatic scooter, typically a Honda Vision or Air Blade, uses a belt-driven CVT: twist and go. The clutch motorbike, represented in Hanoi by the Honda Winner R and the Yamaha Exciter 155, is what most Western riders mean by "manual". When a shop says manual, always ask whether it means the semi-automatic or the real clutch bike.

How each type rides in Hanoi traffic

On a semi-automatic you shift with your left foot in a rotary pattern: neutral, first, second, third, fourth, and on many bikes back round to neutral. You cannot stall it because there is no clutch to release, but shifting under full throttle gives a noticeable jerk; easing off the throttle for a moment makes the change smooth. In return the semi-automatic offers two things scooters struggle with: strong engine braking on long descents and large 17-inch wheels that shrug off potholes and kerbs. The automatic scooter is unbeatable in the stop-and-go of the Old Quarter: both hands stay on the bars, the helmet or rain poncho goes under the seat, and the step-through frame makes getting on and off in a jam easy. Its drawbacks are smaller wheels that feel nervous in ruts and on wet cobbles, weaker engine braking downhill, and a belt drive whose wear a renter cannot see. The clutch motorbike needs a hand on the lever, six gears on the Exciter, and attention at every light; stalling at a Hanoi junction is unpleasant with fifty scooters behind you. The reward is the best control on mountain roads and when overtaking.

Manufacturer figures that make the difference

Numbers help more than impressions. Honda Vietnam lists the semi-automatic Wave Alpha 110 at a kerb weight of 96 kg, 109.2 cc, a rated fuel consumption of 1.72 l/100 km, a 3.7-litre tank, a 770 mm seat height and a mechanical four-speed gearbox. For the automatic Vision Honda gives 97 kg, 109.5 cc, 1.82 l/100 km, a 4.8-litre tank and a 785 mm seat. The clutch bike Winner R weighs 124 kg according to Honda, displaces 149.2 cc, is rated at 2.59 l/100 km, holds 4.5 litres and has a 795 mm seat. Yamaha Motor Vietnam lists the Exciter 155 VVA at 155.1 cc, 119 kg wet, a 5.4-litre tank, a 795 mm seat and a liquid-cooled six-speed gearbox. Two lessons follow. Semi-automatics and automatic scooters weigh almost the same and, on manufacturer figures, use roughly a quarter less fuel than the 150 cc class; and 25 mm of extra seat height decides whether a shorter rider gets both feet down at the lights. All consumption figures are factory ratings, and real use in Hanoi traffic is higher.

Your licence class decides too

In Vietnam the transmission question is also a legal one. Under Law 36/2024/QH15, summarised by the Nghệ An administrative service centre, class A1 covers two-wheelers up to 125 cc and class A covers everything above. The Wave Alpha, the Vision and most automatic scooters stay within A1; the Winner R and the Exciter 155 do not. For foreign renters that means the international driving permit must show category A1 or A, and A1 alone is not enough for a 150 cc clutch bike. Anyone stopped with an unsuitable licence pays, according to the Thư Viện Nhà Đất summary of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, 2,000,000 to 4,000,000 VND on a bike up to 125 cc and 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND above that. Choosing a clutch bike should therefore never be about the fun of the clutch alone.

Which type suits which trip

For riding inside Hanoi, between the Old Quarter, West Lake and the cafés of Tây Hồ, the automatic scooter is the sensible choice: easy in jams, room for the helmet, no gear changes at a hundred traffic lights a day. Couples with day bags benefit from the under-seat compartment and a comfortable dual seat, which is where larger scooters such as the Air Blade 125/160 or Lead come in. Anyone planning a multi-day mountain trip, the loops around Hà Giang or the passes near Mai Châu, should take a semi-automatic or a clutch bike: engine braking on long descents, big wheels on gravel and a gearbox that does not overheat on climbs count for more than comfort there. Complete beginners who have never ridden a two-wheeler should always take the automatic and stay in the city; Hanoi is not the place to learn a clutch. Experienced motorcyclists feel at home on an Exciter or Winner immediately, but must hold class A. In the rainy season, large wheels and more ground clearance favour the semi-automatic, because flooded streets disable low scooters first. Tall riders over 1.85 m sit far more comfortably on a Winner or Exciter than on a Vision.

What to check at the shop for each type

Ask for a short test ride in the yard whatever you choose. On a semi-automatic, check that all four gears engage cleanly and that the gear indicator works, because a worn linkage drops into neutral in traffic. On an automatic scooter, feel for vibration or shudder when pulling away, a sign of a tired belt or rollers, and check that it pulls from a standstill without hesitation. On a clutch bike, test the lever's bite point, whether the clutch slips under full throttle in a high gear, and whether neutral is easy to find at rest. On all three, brakes, tyre tread, lights, horn and a helmet that fits are part of the handover, and the contract should state the vehicle type, category and plate number. The checklist shows how to document all of that.

The decision in five questions

  • Riding only in the city and carrying a helmet or shopping? Automatic scooter.
  • Planning passes, gravel or several hundred kilometres? Semi-automatic or clutch bike.
  • Never ridden a two-wheeler before? Automatic scooter, and city traffic only.
  • Only class A1 on your international permit? Anything under 125 cc, so no Winner and no Exciter.
  • Is it the rainy season? Big wheels and ground clearance point to the semi-automatic.

Answer those five questions honestly and the transmission question is settled before the shop hands over the key.

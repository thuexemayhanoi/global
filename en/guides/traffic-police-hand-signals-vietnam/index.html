Traffic Police Hand Signals in Vietnam

What the three arm signals of Vietnamese traffic police mean, why an officer's signal overrides the traffic light, how to read your position at a junction and what ignoring a signal costs.

At rush hour, during a power cut or after a crash, police officers often direct Hanoi's junctions by hand. For visitors on a rental motorbike that is unfamiliar: the light may be green while the officer raises an arm. Riding on at that moment is one of the more expensive mistakes in Vietnamese traffic law. This guide explains the order of precedence set by the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, what the three arm signals mean, how to work out your own position and what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP charges for ignoring them. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Who may direct traffic

The law calls the people who direct traffic "người điều khiển giao thông". Under Article 2(10) of Law 36/2024/QH15, they are the traffic police and people assigned to guide traffic on the road. So you may meet them at roadworks or a ferry landing as well as at big city junctions. The details of the signals are set by the Minister of Public Security under Article 11(14).

Officer before traffic light: the order of precedence

Article 11(2) of the law sets the order in which road users must follow signals:

1. the instructions of the person directing traffic, 2. traffic lights, 3. road signs, 4. road markings and other markings on the road surface, 5. delineator posts, guard walls, barriers, reflectors, kilometre posts and similar devices, 6. audible warning devices.

The officer's instruction comes first. If the light is green and the officer signals stop, you stop. If the light is red and the officer waves your direction through, you may go. For signs, Article 11(12) adds that where a permanent and a temporary sign at the same place say different things, the temporary sign applies.

The three arm signals

Article 11(3) describes three basic signals. In each case what matters is where you are in relation to the officer: in front of them, behind them, or on their right or left side.

Right arm raised straight up

When the officer raises the right arm vertically, road users from every direction must stop (Article 11(3)(a)). You often see this when the junction is being cleared, for an accident or an emergency vehicle. If you are already in the middle of the junction, clear it carefully; if you are before it, stay put.

Arms held out to the side

When the officer holds both arms, or one arm, out horizontally, road users in front of and behind the officer must stop, and those on the officer's right and left may go (Article 11(3)(b)). A simple rule: if you are looking at the officer's chest or back, stop; if you see their shoulder and outstretched arm side-on, you may go.

Right arm pointing forward

The third signal is the most complex. When the officer points the right arm forward, Article 11(3)(c) says:

  • Road users behind the officer or on their right must stop.
  • Road users in front of the officer may turn right.
  • Road users on the officer's left may go in any direction.
  • Pedestrians crossing the road must pass behind the officer's back.

Reading the signals in practice

In theory it is simple; at a busy junction it is harder. These habits help:

  • Look early. As soon as you see an officer on the road or a podium at a junction, slow down and watch their arms rather than the light.
  • Work out your position. Ask yourself: am I in front of the officer, behind them or to the side? Only then does the signal make sense.
  • If in doubt, stop. If the signal is unclear, stop at the stop line and watch which direction starts moving. A few seconds' wait costs nothing.
  • Don't just follow the pack. Other riders moving off does not mean your direction is open; they may be heading another way.
  • Wait for changes. When the officer turns or gives a new signal, the new signal applies from that moment. Move off only when your direction is clearly open.
  • Keep your passenger informed. A quick word before you stop or move off helps a passenger hold on, because officers' signals can change abruptly.

Our guide to traffic light rules explains the lights when no officer is present, and our guide to right of way at junctions covers junctions without lights.

What ignoring a signal costs

A motorbike or moped rider who ignores the instructions of a person directing or controlling traffic faces 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and 4 points come off the licence under Article 7(13)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). That is the same amount as running a red light under Article 7(7)(c). Our guide to the licence points system explains how points work.

On top of that, Article 9(4) of the law bans insulting, threatening, obstructing or resisting officials on traffic duty, and failing to follow their instructions or inspection requests.

When an officer waves you over

Not every signal is about directing traffic. If an officer points at you and then at the roadside, they want to check you. Stop where indicated, switch off the engine and take your helmet off if asked. Our guide to traffic police stops explains what happens next and which papers to show.

Frequently asked questions

The light is green but the officer has raised an arm. Which applies? The officer. Under Article 11(2) their instructions take precedence over traffic lights.

I can't tell whether I'm in front of or beside the officer. What should I do? Stop and wait until the signal is clear or the officer waves you through. Better to stop once too often than to ride on wrongly.

Do the signals apply to pedestrians too? Yes. When the officer's right arm points forward, pedestrians cross behind the officer's back under Article 11(3)(c).

In short

Police instructions take priority over lights, signs and markings (Article 11(2)). Right arm up: everyone stops. Arms out sideways: front and back stop, the sides may go. Right arm forward: behind and right stop, in front may only turn right, the left may go. Ignoring the signals costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND and 4 points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

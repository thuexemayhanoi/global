Indicators on a motorbike in Vietnam: when the law says you must signal

When Vietnamese law requires a rider to signal: before turning and changing lanes, when overtaking or being overtaken, when stopping and the fines.

Watch a busy junction in Hanoi for a few minutes and you may conclude that indicators are optional. Plenty of riders turn, cut across lanes or pull over without a blink. The law says otherwise. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets out when a rider must signal, how long the signal has to stay on and what to do when someone wants to overtake, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines riders who skip it. Signalling is also simply good self-defence: in dense traffic, a rider whose next move is predictable is a rider others can avoid. This guide goes through each situation, then lists the fines and a few habits worth keeping. For the wider safety picture, see our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

Before every turn

Under Article 15(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15, turning means turning left, turning right or making a U-turn. Article 15(2) then sets the sequence. Before you turn, you must:

  • look around and keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind you;
  • slow down;
  • switch on the indicator for the direction you are taking (non-motorised vehicles without indicators use an arm signal instead);
  • move gradually into the lane nearest to the direction of the turn.

The indicator must stay on for the whole turn, and you may only turn once it is safe and you will not get in the way of other people or vehicles. While turning, Article 15(3) adds that you must give way to pedestrians, non-motorised vehicles and oncoming traffic.

Skipping any part of this costs money. Under Article 7(3)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a motorbike rider who turns without looking, without keeping a safe distance from the vehicle behind, without slowing down, without an indicator, or who switches the indicator off before the turn is finished, is fined VND 600,000 to 800,000. The same point exempts riders who are simply following a bend in the road where there is no junction. Where U-turns and turns are banned outright is covered in our guide to U-turns and left turns; who goes first at a junction is in our guide to right of way.

Changing lanes

On roads with several marked lanes in the same direction, Article 13(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 says you must stay in one lane and change only where changing is allowed, one adjacent lane at a time. You must signal in advance and check that you have a safe distance to the vehicles ahead, behind and on both sides before you move over.

The fine sits in Article 7(1)(e) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP: VND 200,000 to 400,000 for changing lanes where it is not allowed, without signalling first, or across more than one lane at a time. Since Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP, the same point also covers changing lanes without a safe distance to the vehicles ahead, behind or alongside. Which lane a motorbike should use in the first place is explained in our guide on which lane to use.

Overtaking and being overtaken

Overtaking has its own signals, and the law gives a job to both riders.

  • The rider who wants to overtake must first give a warning by flashing the headlight or using the horn, then use the direction indicator throughout the manoeuvre while keeping a safe distance ahead and behind. In towns and residential areas between 22:00 and 05:00, only the light signal may be used (Article 14(5)).
  • Overtaking is only allowed when there is no obstacle ahead, no oncoming vehicle in the stretch, the vehicle in front is not itself signalling to overtake, and it has signalled right and moved over (Article 14(3)).
  • The rider being overtaken must look ahead and, if it is safe, slow down, switch on the right indicator to show the rider behind that they may pass, and keep to the right until they are past. If there is an obstacle or it is not safe, the left indicator tells the rider behind that they may not overtake yet (Article 14(4)).

Overtaking without a signal, or switching it off halfway, is fined VND 200,000 to 400,000 under Article 7(1)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guides to overtaking rules and to using the horn explain the rest.

Stopping, pulling in and breakdowns

Signals matter when you leave the flow too. When you stop or park on the road, Article 18(3) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires you to signal to other road users as you move into and out of the stopping place, and not to get in the way of pedestrians or other vehicles.

If a breakdown or another emergency forces you to leave the bike where it takes up part of the carriageway, or where parking is not allowed, Article 18(7) requires hazard lights or a warning sign placed behind the vehicle so that others can see it. Our guide to what to do when your bike breaks down covers the next steps, and our guide on where to park covers stopping in town.

Headlights at junctions

One more light rule belongs here. Under Article 20(2)(d) of Law 36/2024/QH15, you must switch from high beam to dipped headlights when turning at a junction. Using the high beam when turning at a junction is one of the acts fined VND 200,000 to 400,000 under Article 7(1)(h) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. The other light rules are in our guide to riding at night.

The fines at a glance

| Situation | Rule | Fine for a motorbike rider | |---|---|---| | Turning without looking, slowing down or a continuous indicator | Article 15(2) | VND 600,000–800,000 (Decree 168, Article 7(3)(a)) | | Changing lanes without a signal, where not allowed, across two lanes or without a safe distance | Article 13(2) | VND 200,000–400,000 (Decree 168, Article 7(1)(e), as amended by Decree 238) | | Overtaking without a signal or switching it off midway | Article 14(5) | VND 200,000–400,000 (Decree 168, Article 7(1)(b)) | | High beam when turning at a junction | Article 20(2)(d) | VND 200,000–400,000 (Decree 168, Article 7(1)(h)) |

None of these points carries licence penalty points under Article 7(13) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, but they add up quickly when a checkpoint officer notices them.

Habits that make signalling work

The law sets the minimum. These habits make your signals useful to the people around you:

  • Signal early. Give the riders behind a few seconds to react before you slow down or move over.
  • Look before you move. An indicator announces your intention; it does not clear the space. Check the mirror and glance over your shoulder for scooters filtering past.
  • Cancel it afterwards. On most scooters the indicator does not switch itself off. A forgotten blinker tells everyone you are about to turn when you are not.
  • Read other riders, not only their lights. A blinking indicator may have been forgotten, and an unlit one means nothing. Watch the position of the bike and the direction of its front wheel.
  • Fix a broken indicator before riding. The law allows arm signals only for non-motorised vehicles without indicators (Article 15(2)). Which defects the rider is fined for is explained in our guide to rental bike defects.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to indicate when the road simply bends? No. The fine in Article 7(3)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP expressly excludes riders following the curve of the road where there is no junction.

Do I have to let someone overtake me? If it is safe, yes: slow down, indicate right and keep right until they are past. If it is not safe, indicate left to tell them to wait (Article 14(4)).

When are hazard lights required? When a breakdown or emergency forces you to leave the bike on part of the carriageway or where parking is not allowed; a warning sign behind the vehicle is the alternative (Article 18(7)).

Can I use hand signals instead of the indicator? On a bicycle without indicators, yes (Article 15(2)); our guide to cycling rules explains them. On a motorbike, the indicator is what the law expects.

Summary

Vietnamese law expects a signal before every turn, kept on until the turn is finished (Article 15(2)), before every lane change (Article 13(2)), throughout every overtake (Article 14(5)), when pulling in or out (Article 18(3)) and, after a breakdown on the carriageway, hazard lights (Article 18(7)). The fines range from VND 200,000 to 800,000 (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 7). Before you set off, test both indicators and the hazard switch, if the bike has one, with our rental bike checklist.

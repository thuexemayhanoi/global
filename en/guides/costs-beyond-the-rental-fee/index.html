What a motorbike rental in Hanoi can cost on top of the quoted fee

What a Hanoi bike rental can cost on top of the daily rate: fuel, the new Hanoi parking prices from 15 October 2026, delivery and extension charges, damage and the deposit, and what consumer law says.

The daily rate in the window or on the website is rarely the whole bill. Fuel, parking, delivery and collection, extensions, damage and deductions from the deposit can all be added before you hand the bike back. This guide is not a price list and quotes no shop's prices. It collects what official sources say about each type of cost and the questions worth asking before you pay. To compare base rental prices, see our section on rental costs and fees and the guide to daily, weekly and monthly rentals; for the deposit itself, read motorbike rental deposits and refund terms.

Fuel: the price per litre and the shop's refuelling rule

Petrol prices in Vietnam are adjusted in regular rounds. Under the joint decision of the Ministries of Industry and Trade and of Finance that applied from 15:00 on 15 October 2026, the retail price of E5 RON 92 petrol was capped at VND 26,971 per litre and E10 RON 95-III at VND 27,029 per litre, according to the newspaper Gia đình và Xã hội. Treat that as a dated example rather than a budget: the price moves with the next round, and each station posts its own.

For a renter, what shapes the bill is less the price per litre than the shop's refuelling rule. Do you collect the bike with a full tank and return it full, return it with the level you received, or pay for fuel by the gauge? Ask before you take the bike, photograph the fuel gauge at pick-up and again at return, and ask what the shop charges per litre if you bring the bike back low.

Parking: Hanoi's new prices from 15 October 2026

On 15 October 2026 the Hanoi People's Committee issued Decision 5794/QĐ-UBND on the price of parking services at state-funded points that temporarily use roadway or pavement; it takes effect on signature. For motorbikes it sets VND 10,000 for a daytime visit, VND 15,000 for a night-time visit and VND 25,000 for a day-and-night visit, with monthly tickets at VND 250,000 on the restricted streets of the core urban area and VND 200,000 on other streets, according to the Dân trí report and the decision text attached to it. Dân trí also reports that the previous per-visit prices were VND 5,000, 8,000 and 12,000, so older articles may quote lower numbers.

These prices cover state-funded points on roadways and pavements. A hotel car park, a shopping centre or a private lot may charge differently, and the decision asks the Department of Construction to review actual costs so that adjustments can be proposed later. Ask the price before you leave the bike and keep the ticket. If you plan to be in the Old Quarter at weekends, read our guide to the Ring Road 1 low-emission zone as well.

Delivery, collection, late return and extensions

None of the official sources used for this guide sets a tariff for delivering a bike to a hotel, collecting it at the airport, returning it out of hours, returning it late, extending the rental or dropping it off somewhere else: shops set those charges themselves. So ask about each one and ask the shop to put it in the contract or a confirmation message before pick-up day. Vietnam's Law on Protection of Consumers' Rights (19/2023/QH15, in force since 1 July 2024) supports that request. Article 21 requires traders to give accurate and complete information about the service, including price, fees and costs; Article 10 prohibits forcing a consumer to pay for a service already provided without prior agreement; and Article 25 does not allow terms that let the trader set or change the price at the time the service is provided, unless the law provides otherwise (full text).

Article 3 defines a consumer as a person who buys or uses services for personal, household or organisational consumption and not for commercial purposes. A visitor who rents a bike for their own travel fits that wording, though how it applies in a particular dispute is for the competent authorities to decide (full text).

Damage, repairs and the deposit

Compulsory motor liability insurance is not damage cover for the bike you rent. Under Article 7 of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, the insurer compensates damage to the health, life and property of third parties and to the health and life of passengers on the vehicle; damage to the rented bike itself is not on that list (full text). So who pays for a scratch, a snapped mirror or a lost key is a matter for the rental contract, and such sums may be taken from the deposit if the contract allows it.

Before you ride away, walk around the bike with a member of staff, photograph or film every side, existing scratches, tyres, mirrors, lights, the odometer and the fuel gauge, and ask the shop to write existing damage into the contract. Ask for the price of the usual repairs such as mirrors, brake levers, indicators, tyres and keys, whether a puncture repair is charged separately and whether the shop proves a deduction with an invoice or with photos. The UK Foreign Office also warns against giving your passport as a guarantee to a motorcycle rental shop, because the shop can hold it against claimed damage (FCDO travel advice). If the bike fails on the road, read what to do if your rental bike breaks down.

Contract language, reading time and unclear terms

Article 23 says the language of a consumer contract is Vietnamese, that the parties may add another language, and that if the two versions differ the one more favourable to the consumer prevails. Article 26 requires the trader to give a reasonable time to study a standard-form contract before it is concluded and to supply a copy within 07 working days if yours is lost; Article 24 says that terms open to different readings are interpreted in the consumer's favour (full text). Read the contract before you pay, ask for a version in a language you understand and photograph the signed copy.

Frequently asked questions

Can a rental shop make me pay a fee we never agreed on? Not for a service you never agreed to: Article 10 of the Law on Protection of Consumers' Rights (19/2023/QH15) bans forcing a consumer to pay for a service already provided without prior agreement, and Article 21 requires traders to give accurate and complete information on price, fees and costs (full text). A visitor renting a bike for their own travel fits the law's definition of a consumer, though how it applies in a particular dispute is for the competent authorities to decide.

Do Hanoi's new parking prices apply in hotel or private car parks? Not necessarily. Decision 5794/QĐ-UBND covers state-funded parking points that temporarily use the roadway or pavement, according to the Dân trí report and the decision text attached to it. Hotel, shopping-centre and private car parks can set their own prices, so ask before you leave the bike and keep the ticket.

Can I ask for my motorbike rental contract in English? You can agree to add it: under Article 23 of the consumer protection law, a consumer contract is in Vietnamese and the parties may add another language; if the two versions differ, the one more favourable to the consumer prevails (full text). Article 24 says terms open to different readings are interpreted in the consumer's favour, and Article 26 gives you reasonable time to study a standard-form contract. Ask for a version you understand and photograph the signed copy.

Questions to ask before you pay

  • What does the daily price include: helmet, rain poncho, phone holder, lock, the bike's papers?
  • What is the refuelling rule, and what is charged per litre if I return the bike low?
  • What do delivery, collection, out-of-hours return, late return and extension cost, and are they written into the contract?
  • Which items can be taken from the deposit, how are they proved, and how soon is the deposit returned?
  • Who pays for a puncture, a broken mirror, a lost key or a tow?
  • What happens if a traffic notice reaches the shop after I have returned the bike?
  • Do I get a copy of the signed contract and a receipt for every payment?

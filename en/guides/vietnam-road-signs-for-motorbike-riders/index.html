Vietnam road signs for motorbike riders

How to read Vietnamese road signs on a motorbike: the five sign groups in QCVN 41:2024, the shapes and colours, the signs that matter most for riders, traffic lights and the fines for ignoring them.

Vietnamese road signs follow a system that will look familiar to anyone who has driven in Europe: shapes and colours tell you what kind of message a sign carries before you can read the symbol. A handful of signs, however, matter far more to motorbike riders than to car drivers, starting with the one that bans motorbikes from a street altogether. This guide explains the five sign groups in the national road signalling regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, the order in which signals apply, the signs riders meet most often, how traffic lights work and what ignoring a sign costs. For the wider rules of riding in the city, see Hanoi traffic for first-time riders.

Five groups of signs

Article 11 of QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, issued on 15 November 2024 to replace the 2019 version, divides road signs into five basic groups:

  • Prohibitory signs show what road users must not do. They are mostly round, with a red border, a white background and black symbols, numbers or text.
  • Mandatory signs show orders that must be followed. They are round with a blue background and white symbols.
  • Danger and warning signs warn of hazards ahead. They are mostly equilateral triangles with a red border, a yellow background and a black symbol.
  • Information signs give directions and other useful information. They are mostly rectangular, square or arrow-shaped with a blue background.
  • Supplementary and text plates add detail to the other groups, for example a time window or a vehicle type, or are used on their own.

The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety describes the same groups in Article 11(5): prohibitory signs express bans, danger signs warn of situations that may become dangerous, mandatory signs give orders, information signs show directions or things worth knowing, and supplementary plates explain the others.

Which signal wins

When signals seem to conflict, Article 11(2) of the law sets a strict order: first the instructions of a traffic officer, then traffic lights, then road signs, then road markings and other surface markings, then delineators, barriers and similar devices, and finally audible signals. If a fixed sign and a temporary sign at the same spot say different things, the temporary sign applies under Article 11(12). In practice, a police officer directing a junction by hand overrides a green light.

Signs every rider should recognise

These signs from QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT come up constantly for motorbike riders:

  • P.104 No motorbikes and P.111a No mopeds. Some streets and bridges are closed to two-wheelers; the sign applies to you even if cars are allowed.
  • P.102 No entry, the round red sign with a white bar, marks the wrong end of a one-way street.
  • P.101 Road closed bans all vehicles.
  • P.124 No U-turn and P.127 Maximum speed, the round sign with a red border and a black number.
  • P.130 No stopping or parking and P.131 No parking, which often carry supplementary plates with times or odd and even days.
  • W.207 Junction with a non-priority road and W.208 Junction with a priority road, which tell you who gives way at the next junction.
  • W.224 Pedestrian crossing and W.225 Children, which warn you to slow down.
  • R.303 Roundabout, a blue round sign; where it applies, traffic coming from the left has priority (Article 22).
  • R.122 Stop, where you must stop completely before continuing.
  • I.408 Parking, an information sign marking where parking is allowed.
  • DP.134 End of maximum speed and DP.135 End of all prohibitions, which lift earlier restrictions.

Where the built-up area begins and ends

Speed limits and several other rules depend on whether you are in a built-up area, and two mandatory signs mark it. R.420 Start of built-up area applies to every road in the urban area until you pass R.421 End of built-up area, according to QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT. Within that zone, a posted P.127 speed sign still takes precedence over the default limit. Outside towns, the regulation treats a stretch of road as passing through a built-up area when, among other conditions, it is at least 500 metres long with houses close to the carriageway. The hub Safer Riding and Breakdown Support in Hanoi collects our guides on speed and the other riding rules that change inside these zones.

Traffic lights

Article 11(4) of the law gives the meaning of the three colours. Green means go, but you must slow down or stop for pedestrians and wheelchair users still crossing. Yellow means stop before the stop line; if you are already on or past the line, you may continue. Flashing yellow means you may proceed but must watch, slow down and give way to pedestrians and other vehicles. Red means you must not go.

What ignoring a sign costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines motorbike and moped riders according to the sign involved:

  • General failure to follow signs or road markings: VND 200,000 to 400,000 under Article 7(1)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Entering an area or road where a sign bans your type of vehicle, such as a no-motorbikes street: VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 under Article 7(6)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Riding the wrong way on a one-way street or against a No entry sign: VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 under Article 7(7)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Running a red light: VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 under Article 7(7)(c), plus 4 points off the licence (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Practical tips

Navigation apps do not always know which streets are closed to motorbikes or which way a one-way street runs, so look up from the screen at every junction. Supplementary plates under a sign often carry the detail that matters, such as hours or vehicle types, and they are usually in Vietnamese; it helps to learn a few words, for example "trừ" (except), "giờ" (hours) and "xe máy" (motorbike). When you are unsure what a sign means, follow the traffic that is clearly allowed through, or stop safely and check.

In short

Shape and colour tell you the type of message: red-bordered circles prohibit, blue circles order, red-bordered yellow triangles warn and blue rectangles inform (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). Police instructions beat lights, lights beat signs and temporary signs beat permanent ones (Article 11). Watch especially for the no-motorbike and no-entry signs, because the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP for ignoring them are high. Before your first ride, run through our pickup checklist.

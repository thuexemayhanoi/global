U-Turns and Turning on a Motorbike in Vietnam

How to turn and make U-turns on a motorbike in Vietnam: the steps before every turn, who goes first, where U-turns and reversing are banned, the signs to look for and the fines.

Miss your turning in Hanoi and the local solution is often to swing round wherever there is a gap in the traffic. Plenty of riders do it, and navigation apps often suggest it. Yet the law sets out how to turn and where turning round is off limits, and some of those places are exactly where visitors are tempted to try. This guide explains the turning rules in Article 15 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the places where U-turns and reversing are banned, the signs that matter and the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For riding in the city in general, see our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders.

Three manoeuvres, one set of duties

Article 15(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety groups three manoeuvres under "changing direction": turning left, turning right and making a U-turn. The same basic duties apply to all three, and U-turns come with an extra list of forbidden places.

Before every turn: look, slow down, signal

Under Article 15(2), before you change direction you must:

1. watch the traffic and keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind you; 2. slow down; 3. switch on your indicator and keep it on for the whole manoeuvre; 4. move gradually into the lane nearest to the direction you are turning.

You may turn only when it is safe and you will not get in anyone's way. On a wide road that means moving across early, not cutting over several lanes at the last moment.

Who goes first when you turn

Article 15(3) requires you to give way to pedestrians, non-motorised vehicles and oncoming traffic when you turn. That still applies on a green light: under Article 11(4)(a), green means go, but you must slow down or stop for pedestrians and wheelchair users who are still crossing. Our guide to right of way at junctions covers who has priority at different kinds of junction.

Where U-turns are banned

Article 15(4) lists the places where you must not turn round, unless a person directing traffic or a temporary sign allows it:

  • on pedestrian crossings;
  • on bridges, at bridge approaches and under overpasses;
  • at fords and submerged road crossings;
  • at level crossings with railways;
  • on narrow roads and slopes;
  • on bends with obstructed visibility;
  • on expressways and in road tunnels;
  • on one-way roads.

Signs add to the list. The prohibition sign P.124 "No U-turn" bans turning round, and the guide sign I.409 "U-turn place" marks a spot where turning round is provided for (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). On roads with a central reservation, those U-turn places are often the only legal option.

Reversing: even tighter limits

Rolling back a few metres can look like the easy answer, for example in a narrow lane. Article 16 requires you to look to both sides and behind, signal and reverse only when it is safe. Reversing is banned on one-way roads, in no-stopping zones, on pedestrian crossings, at junctions and level crossings, where visibility is obstructed, in tunnels and on expressways. In those places, get off and push the bike a short way instead.

What it costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets these fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds:

  • U-turn in a prohibited place: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(k) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Turning without watching, keeping your distance, slowing down or keeping the indicator on, or turning against a no-turn sign: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • U-turn in a road tunnel: 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND under Article 7(4)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Not giving way when turning to pedestrians on a crossing, oncoming traffic, or pedestrians and non-motorised vehicles crossing where there is no marked crossing: 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND under Article 7(5)(b) and (c), plus 2 licence points under Article 7(13)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Turning wrongly and causing an accident: 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Turning at night

After dark, lights come into it. Between 18:00 and 06:00, and in fog, smoke, dust or rain, your headlight must be on, and when you turn at a junction you must not use the high beam, which dazzles oncoming riders and pedestrians. Each mistake costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(g) and (h) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guide to riding at night explains the light rules in full.

Practical tips for Hanoi

Many Hanoi roads have a central reservation with only occasional gaps. Plan your route to keep U-turns to a minimum, and prefer a loop round the block with three right turns to a risky U-turn across several lanes. In the Old Quarter, watch out for one-way streets: if you are lost, push the bike a short way rather than riding against the traffic. Riding the wrong way down a one-way street costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Cutting across the central reservation is no short cut either: crossing a fixed median between carriageways costs 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Ride on to the next U-turn place or signal-controlled junction, even if the detour costs a few minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a U-turn at a junction? Junctions are not on the list in Article 15(4), so a U-turn there is not banned as such. It is banned where a sign such as P.124 forbids it or a person directing traffic says otherwise, and you still have to follow the steps in Article 15(2) and give way as Article 15(3) requires.

What if my navigation app tells me to turn round on a bridge? Ignore it. Bridges, bridge approaches and the space under overpasses are on the list of forbidden places in Article 15(4). Ride on to the end of the bridge and look for a legal place to turn.

Is pushing the bike back a few metres allowed? Getting off and wheeling the bike a short way is a sensible alternative to reversing where Article 16 forbids it, as long as you do not block pedestrians or traffic.

In short

Before every turn: look, slow down, signal and move into the right lane (Article 15(2)). Pedestrians and oncoming traffic go first, and you never turn round on bridges, pedestrian crossings, one-way roads, bends, slopes, in tunnels or at level crossings. Fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP range from 400,000 to 2,000,000 VND, and far more after an accident. Before your first ride, our rental pickup checklist helps you check the indicators.

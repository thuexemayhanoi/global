Traffic Camera Fines on a Rental Motorbike in Vietnam

How camera-detected traffic violations reach the owner of a rental motorbike in Vietnam, why the shop must identify the rider, what renters face and how to avoid deposit disputes.

Not every traffic offence in Vietnam ends with a police officer waving you over. Cameras and other devices record violations too, and the notice arrives later at the registered owner of the vehicle. Vietnamese people call this "phạt nguội", literally a "cold fine". On a rental motorbike the registered owner is not you but the rental shop, and the notice can turn up weeks after you have handed the bike back. This guide explains how the procedure in Article 47 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP works, why the shop has to help the authorities find the rider, what that means for you as a renter and how to avoid an argument over your deposit. For the paperwork side of renting, see our topic page on rental documents and contracts.

How a camera fine starts

When a violation is detected by technical equipment and the vehicle is not stopped on the spot, the authorities send a notice to the vehicle owner, and to other people involved where relevant, asking them to come to the responsible office and resolve the case. The notice is sent in writing or electronically through the Ministry of Public Security's traffic app, and the vehicle is also listed on the traffic police website. That is how the Hà Tĩnh provincial government describes the process, citing Article 47 of the decree (hatinh.gov.vn).

Who counts as the owner

Under Article 47(6) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, the vehicle owner is, in principle, the person or organisation in whose name the vehicle is registered. For a rental motorbike that is the rental business or its proprietor, which is why the notice lands at the shop and not in your inbox.

Why the shop will name you

The owner has a duty to work with the authorities to identify who was riding (hatinh.gov.vn). A private owner who cannot show that someone else was riding and does not cooperate is penalised for the recorded violation themselves. The same applies to businesses, unless the vehicle was stolen or used without permission.

That makes the rental contract the shop's most important piece of evidence. If the shop can show with the contract, a copy of your ID and your licence details that you were riding at the time, the shop is not penalised. Instead, the authorities summon you, the renter, to resolve the case (hatinh.gov.vn).

What happens to the rider

Once identified, the rider is expected to respond to the summons. If they do not, the authorities can still draw up a record of the violation and issue a penalty decision against them. If the offence carries licence points, they are deducted once the decision takes effect under Article 50 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP (hatinh.gov.vn); our guide to the licence points system explains how points work.

Ignoring the case has further consequences. If it is not settled by the deadline, or the fine is not paid, the authority informs the offices responsible for vehicle registration and driving licences, which then hold back the issue, exchange or reissue of that person's licence until the case is closed (hatinh.gov.vn, citing Article 47(11) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). A penalty decision can also be enforced, and late payment adds 0.05 percent of the unpaid amount for every day of delay, as the same article explains with reference to the Law on Handling Administrative Violations (hatinh.gov.vn).

What this means for travellers

For a holidaymaker the practical questions matter most. A notice may arrive after you have left Vietnam, and whether and how the shop contacts you depends on your contract and the contact details you left. Sort this out when you pick up the bike:

  • Ask what the contract says about later notices. Have the shop explain how it handles them and get the answer in writing.
  • Agree how charges are settled. Money should only come out of your deposit against clear evidence, such as a copy of the notice or decision.
  • Leave contact details that still work after the trip. An email address you check at home is better than a local SIM number.

Our guide to rental deposits and refund terms covers getting your deposit back, and our list of costs beyond the rental fee shows other charges to expect.

At drop-off: ask, don't hope

When you return the bike, ask whether any violations are recorded for it; the authorities list such vehicles on the traffic police website (hatinh.gov.vn). Get the shop to confirm whether anything was showing at the time of return, and write the return date and time on the contract so it is clear later whether a violation falls within your rental. Photos of the odometer and the bike at pickup and return help too. Our rental pickup checklist shows what to record.

Check a notice before you pay

If the shop shows you a notice after your trip, check it calmly before paying anything:

1. Plate number: does it match the bike you rented? 2. Date and time: does the violation fall inside your rental period as stated in the contract? 3. Place and type of violation: could you plausibly have been there at that time? 4. Decision number: with the number of the penalty decision or the violation record, the case can be looked up on the National Public Service Portal (LuatVietnam).

If any of these details is missing, ask the shop for a complete copy. A screenshot with no date or plate number is not proof.

Paying the fine

A confirmed fine is paid to the state, not to the shop, for example online through the National Public Service Portal using the decision number (LuatVietnam). Do not hand money to third parties without having seen a decision, and keep the payment receipt.

Frequently asked questions

Does this apply to electric scooters? Yes. The procedure in Article 47 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP is tied to the registered owner and applies to registered vehicles whatever powers them.

Can the shop simply keep money from my deposit? That depends on your contract. Either way, insist on proof that a decision exists and falls within your rental, and on a receipt showing payment to the state.

The easiest fix: don't get caught

Whether a police officer or a camera records a violation makes no difference to the outcome. Riders who follow the rules have nothing to fear from a camera notice. Our guide to traffic police stops explains what happens when you are stopped on the road.

In short

When a camera records a violation, the notice goes to the registered owner, which for a rental bike is the shop. Under Article 47 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP the shop must help identify the rider, and with the rental contract the authorities then summon the renter (hatinh.gov.vn). Agree how later notices are handled when you rent, ask about recorded violations when you return the bike, and pay only against a genuine decision.

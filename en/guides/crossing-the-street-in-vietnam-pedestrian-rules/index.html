Crossing the street in Vietnam on foot: what the law expects from pedestrians

How to cross the street in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City: where Vietnamese law lets pedestrians walk and cross, the hand-signal rule, crossing with children, the pedestrian fines and safe technique.

The first challenge many visitors face in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City is not riding a scooter but crossing the road in front of thousands of them. The traffic rarely stops, zebra crossings are often ignored, and the pavement may be full of parked bikes and street kitchens. Vietnamese law does give pedestrians clear rules, and they are worth knowing, because they explain both what you are expected to do and what you can expect from drivers. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets out where pedestrians walk and cross, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines pedestrians who break those rules. This guide covers the rules, the fines, crossing with children and a practical technique for dense motorbike traffic. For the rider's side of the same crossing, see our guide to pedestrian crossings for motorbike riders, and for the wider safety picture our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

Where pedestrians must walk

Under Article 30(1)(a) of Law 36/2024/QH15, pedestrians must walk on the pavement, the verge or a path reserved for pedestrians. Where there is none of these, they must walk close to the right-hand edge of the road in their direction of travel.

In Vietnamese cities the pavement is often blocked by parked motorbikes, shop displays or food stalls. The law does not make an exception for that. When you have to step into the road to get round an obstacle, keep as close to the edge as you can, look behind you before you step out and return to the pavement as soon as it is clear.

Where and how to cross

Article 30(1)(b) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets two situations:

  • Where there are traffic lights, road markings for pedestrians, a footbridge or an underpass, you may only cross there, and you must follow the signals and signs.
  • Where none of these exists, you must watch the oncoming vehicles, cross only when it is safe and give a hand signal while you cross.

The hand signal is a real legal requirement, not just a local habit: raising an arm makes you visible to riders who are scanning the road for gaps. Under Article 30(1)(c), pedestrians may also not climb over a central divider or hold on to a moving vehicle.

Traffic lights and traffic police

At signalled junctions, green means go and red means no entry (Article 11(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15). The same rule protects you once you are on the road: when the light turns green while pedestrians are still crossing, vehicles must slow down or stop to let them finish (Article 11(4)(a)). Riders also have to look, slow down or stop at markings for pedestrians and wherever pedestrians are crossing (Article 12(3)(a)).

Where a traffic officer directs a junction, follow the officer's signals rather than the lights. When the officer holds the right arm forward, pedestrians crossing must pass behind the officer (Article 11(3)(c) of Law 36/2024/QH15). What each signal means is explained in our guide to traffic police hand signals, and the lights in our guide to traffic light rules.

The fines for pedestrians

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP has its own article for pedestrians, Article 10:

  • VND 150,000 to 250,000 for not walking on the permitted part of the road, climbing over a divider, crossing at the wrong place or crossing without the required hand signal; for ignoring traffic lights, signs or road markings; and for ignoring the instructions of a traffic officer (Article 10(1) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • VND 400,000 to 600,000 for walking onto an expressway, carrying bulky items that obstruct traffic, or holding on to a moving vehicle (Article 10(2)).

The rules in Article 10 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP apply in the same way to locals and visitors. Expressways are closed to pedestrians as they are to motorbikes; our guide to motorbikes on expressways explains the signs that mark them.

Crossing with children

Under Article 30(2)(a) of Law 36/2024/QH15, children under seven must be accompanied by an adult when they cross the road, and Article 30(2)(đ) asks everyone to help young children, pregnant women, elderly and disabled people cross. In practice that means holding the child's hand on the side away from the traffic, crossing at the pace of the adult rather than letting the child run, and choosing a signalled crossing even if it means a detour. Families who ride as well will find the rules for children on bikes in our guide to riding with children.

A practical technique for dense traffic

The law sets the minimum. Locals cross through moving motorbike traffic in a way that looks alarming but follows a simple logic: riders steer around a pedestrian whose path they can predict.

  • Use crossings and lights where they exist. It is the legal choice and usually the safest.
  • Look before you step out, both ways, even on one-way streets. Motorbikes often ride the wrong way along the edge of the road.
  • Raise a hand and make eye contact with the riders coming towards you.
  • Walk at a steady, even pace. Do not stop suddenly, step back or run. Riders judge their line by your speed; a sudden change of pace is what causes collisions.
  • Watch for cars, buses and trucks. Motorbikes flow around you; larger vehicles cannot, and their drivers may not see you. Let them pass before you continue.
  • Be extra careful at night and in rain, when riders see less. Light-coloured clothing helps.

If something does go wrong, our guides to emergency numbers and to witnessing a road accident explain what to do. Riders who want to see the same street from the saddle can read our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders.

Frequently asked questions

Is jaywalking illegal in Vietnam? Crossing away from a crossing is only allowed where there are no lights, markings, footbridges or underpasses, and then only with a hand signal (Article 30(1)(b) of Law 36/2024/QH15); otherwise it is fined VND 150,000 to 250,000 (Article 10(1)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Do motorbikes have to stop for me at a zebra crossing? Riders must look, slow down or stop at pedestrian markings and where pedestrians are crossing (Article 12(3)(a)). Do not rely on it: wait until riders have clearly seen you.

Can I walk on the road if the pavement is blocked? Only as far as you need to get past, keeping to the edge; the rule is to walk on the pavement where there is one (Article 30(1)(a)).

Do the rules apply to tourists? Yes. The law applies to everyone using Vietnamese roads, and the pedestrian fines in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP make no exception for visitors.

Summary

Pedestrians in Vietnam must use the pavement, verge or pedestrian path, cross only at lights, markings, footbridges or underpasses where they exist, and elsewhere cross only when it is safe and with a hand signal (Article 30 of Law 36/2024/QH15). Breaking those rules costs VND 150,000 to 250,000, and walking onto an expressway or holding on to a moving vehicle VND 400,000 to 600,000 (Article 10 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Cross at a steady pace, raise a hand and watch the cars, and if you ride as well, check your bike before you set off with our motorbike checklist.

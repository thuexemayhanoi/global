Will your helmet and rain jacket fit in the seat box of a rented scooter in Hanoi?

Will a helmet and a rain jacket fit under the seat of a rental scooter in Hanoi? Manufacturer compartment sizes, helmet shapes, e-scooter batteries, wet gear and a pickup test.

Whether a helmet and a rain jacket fit under the seat of a rented scooter depends on three things: the model, the type of helmet, and, on an electric scooter, whether a spare battery already occupies the box. Honda says the Air Blade's compartment takes up to two half-head helmets and the LEAD's takes two large full-head helmets, while Honda's Vision page gives no litre figure at all. A thin jacket or poncho is the easy part, because it squashes flat. The helmet decides, so try yours in the bike before you ride away. For the wider choice of bike, start from our topic page on choosing a rental motorbike.

What the manufacturers actually state

The statements below come from the manufacturers' own Vietnamese pages, read on 19 October 2026. They describe current models, and rental fleets often include older versions, so treat them as a guide to what to ask rather than a promise about the bike you will be handed. Which models a given fleet runs is not something these pages can tell you.

  • Honda Air Blade: a 23.2-litre compartment that Honda says holds "up to two half-head helmets" plus other personal items. A divider can split it into two sections, there is a light, and a USB-C port sits inside under a waterproof cover (Honda Vietnam, Air Blade).
  • Honda LEAD: "more than 37 litres" with a divider, and Honda says it takes two large full-head helmets plus personal items. A light helps you find things, and the separate front pocket fits a 500 ml bottle (Honda Vietnam, LEAD).
  • Honda Vision: a "large" compartment that Honda says can take a helmet and many personal items. The page prints no litre figure and does not say which helmet type, so this is the model where a test matters most (Honda Vietnam, Vision).
  • Honda Wave Alpha: the owner's manual describes a storage box with a maximum load of 5 kg and warns against storing flammable or heat-sensitive items, valuables or fragile things in it (Honda Vietnam, Wave Alpha owner's manual). The passage gives a weight limit rather than a volume, so do not assume a helmet fits.
  • Yamaha Janus: Yamaha says the compartment is 1.1 litres larger than on the previous version and has an insulating layer against engine heat. Its specification table lists the storage compartment as 15.3 but prints no unit, so confirm the capacity on the bike (Yamaha Motor Vietnam, Janus).

For how these models differ otherwise, see our comparison of Honda Wave, Vision, Air Blade and Lead.

Why the helmet matters more than the litres

A litre figure measures volume, but a helmet is an awkward, rigid shape that wastes much of it. That is probably why Honda's wording separates half-head from full-head helmets instead of just quoting a number: a chin section adds depth that an open helmet does not have. That is an inference from the shapes, not something Honda spells out.

The main types are easy to tell apart. A full-face helmet covers the whole head including a section over the chin. A modular (flip-up) helmet looks the same when closed but has a chin bar that pivots upward. An open-face or three-quarter helmet covers the ears, cheeks and back of the head but has no chin bar, and a half helmet covers roughly half the head (Wikipedia, Motorcycle helmet). In a compartment that is sized for "half-head" helmets, the first two are the ones likely to cause trouble.

Three practical points follow from that.

  • Peaks and visors count. A long sun peak or a raised visor can stop the seat from closing, even when the shell itself would fit.
  • Two people, two helmets. If a passenger rides with you, you are storing two helmets, which is exactly the case Honda describes for the Air Blade and the LEAD.
  • Do not force the seat shut. If the lid will not latch without pressing down hard, the helmet does not fit. A lid that is merely resting on a helmet is not secured.

For the rules and standards that apply to helmets in Vietnam, and for fitting your own, read our guide to choosing a helmet in Vietnam instead of relying on this page.

Electric scooters: where the battery goes

Electric models can be the exception to everything above. VinFast's own FAQ lists the compartment volume of each model and says a helmet fits, then adds a second number for some models when an extra battery is installed (VinFast, electric scooter FAQ):

  • Feliz 2025 and Feliz Lite: 34 litres, or 15 litres with the extra battery.
  • Evo Grand and Vero X: 35 litres, or 16 litres with the extra battery.
  • Flazz: 14 litres, or 8 litres with the extra battery.
  • Evo Lite Neo and Zgoo: 17 and 14 litres respectively, with no extra-battery figure in the answers.

The same FAQ explains why. On the models it describes, the main battery is fixed under the footboard, while the extra battery is placed in the compartment and can be taken out. VinFast answers "can hold a helmet" for every model without naming a helmet type. With an extra battery fitted, treat that as unproven for a full-face helmet and test it. Hanoi's wet months, from the climate data

A climate-data page for Hanoi, based on 1991 to 2020 averages, puts the rainiest period from June to September, caused by the Southeast Asian monsoon. It gives roughly 1,600 mm of rain a year, more than 150 mm a month in May and October, and roughly 250 mm or more a month from June to September. Its table shows precipitation on 14 to 17 days a month in those four months. It describes summer as hot and muggy with frequent and sometimes heavy rain, and notes that typhoons typically pass from late May to early November, peaking from July to October (Climates to Travel, Hanoi). In winter the same page reports frequent drizzle.

For storage, the point is simple: in most months a rain set is part of the kit, not an extra. On the bike it belongs where you can reach it quickly, ideally before the sky has turned. That is general advice, but the climate figures explain why it matters. For riding itself in downpours, see riding a scooter in Hanoi during heavy rain, and for what to wear, our guide to gear beyond the helmet.

Packing wet gear without soaking everything else

None of the manufacturer pages read for this guide promises a watertight compartment. Honda's Air Blade page mentions a waterproof cover for the USB-C port only, and Yamaha's insulation is described as protection against engine heat. Assume that damp air and drips can reach whatever is in the box. The rest of this section is general advice, not sourced fact.

  • Bag the wet gear. Put a dripping jacket or poncho in its own plastic or dry bag before it goes under the seat, so it cannot soak a spare layer or a charger.
  • Keep papers and phone on you. Honda's Wave Alpha manual warns against keeping valuables in the box, and a compartment is the first place a thief opens. Carry your passport, cash and phone in a pouch you wear.
  • Do not charge in the box. Yamaha's Janus page notes that a phone or battery should not be left in the compartment while it is charging through the USB port.
  • Dry it at night. A damp jacket shut in a warm compartment stays damp. Hang it up when you stop for the day.

A two-minute test at pickup

Do this with the bike in front of you, before you sign anything off.

  • Open the seat yourself and find the release. If the bike has a Smart Key, learn which button opens the seat (our Smart Key guide explains the fob).
  • Put your own helmet inside and close the seat without pressing. It should latch cleanly.
  • Add the rain gear in its bag and a small pack, then close it again.
  • Look for the compartment light, a divider and a USB port, and check the box is clean and dry.
  • On an electric scooter, ask whether an extra battery is in the box.
  • If the bike has a hook for the helmet, use it only for parking.

If it does not fit: hook, top box, chest pack

First, ask for another bike or another helmet before you accept a compromise. If that is not possible, there are three fallbacks.

  • A helmet hook. Use it while parked and never ride with a helmet hanging from it. Do not leave the helmet there out of sight: it is easy to take, and the Swiss motoring club TCS notes that a helmet that falls from a shelf, seat or handlebar should be replaced even if no damage is visible, because the inner shell can be damaged (TCS, helmet comparison, in German). Our guide to preventing motorbike theft in Hanoi covers parking habits.
  • A top box. Only if the bike already has one. Do not drill or modify a rental bike, and see our guide to luggage and load limits before adding bags.
  • A small backpack worn on your chest. It keeps documents in sight and leaves the seat and rack free. Make sure nothing hangs near the handlebars or the front brake lever.

Frequently asked questions

Does a full-face helmet fit under a Honda Vision's seat? Honda says the Vision's compartment can take a helmet and personal items, but its page gives no litre figure and does not name the helmet type. Test your own helmet at pickup.

Do a helmet and a rain jacket fit together? Usually yes if the helmet fits at all, because a folded jacket or poncho is soft. The helmet is the limit. Honda's figures for the Air Blade (23.2 litres, two half-head helmets) and the LEAD (more than 37 litres, two large full-head helmets) show how much the model matters.

Is the compartment waterproof? None of the pages read for this guide say so. Honda mentions a waterproof cover only for the USB-C port. Bag wet gear and keep papers on you.

Which scooters have the biggest compartments? Among the pages checked, the Honda LEAD (more than 37 litres), the VinFast Evo Grand and Vero X (35 litres) and the Feliz (34 litres), though the VinFast figures drop to 15 or 16 litres with an extra battery.

Can I leave my helmet on the hook while I eat? It is better not to. A hanging helmet can be taken or knocked off, so lock it in the compartment if it fits.

Summary and next steps

Ask for the model first, then test your helmet and your rain set in the compartment with the seat closed. On electric scooters ask about the extra battery, and keep papers and phone on you rather than in the box.

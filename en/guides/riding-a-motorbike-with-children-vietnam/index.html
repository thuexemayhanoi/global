Riding a motorbike with children in Vietnam: what the law allows

What Vietnamese law says about carrying children on a motorbike: when two passengers are allowed, helmets for children, whether a small child may sit in front, the fines in Decree 168 and safety tips.

Anyone who spends a morning in Hanoi sees children on scooters everywhere: a toddler in front of a parent, two schoolchildren squeezed behind, a baby held by a passenger on the way to the market. For families who live in Vietnam or travel with children, the obvious question is what the law actually allows. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) has specific rules for children as passengers, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP attaches fines to them, with a few exceptions for small children that are often misunderstood. This guide explains how many children may ride, the helmet rule, where a small child may sit, which passenger bans apply to children too, and how to make short family rides as safe as possible. For the wider safety picture, see our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

How many children may ride

The starting point is Article 33(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15: a rider of a two-wheeled motorbike or moped may carry one passenger. In four cases, the rider may carry at most two people instead. One of them is carrying a child under 12 (point (c)); the others are taking a sick person to emergency care, escorting a person who has broken the law, and carrying elderly, frail or disabled people.

In everyday family terms, that means:

  • you and one child: allowed, as with any single passenger;
  • you, an adult passenger and one child under 12: allowed, because one of the two passengers is a child under 12;
  • you and two children under 12: allowed;
  • three passengers of any age, for example two adults and a child or one adult and two children: not allowed.

Under Article 7(2)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a rider who carries two people outside the permitted cases is fined VND 400,000 to 600,000. Carrying three or more people costs VND 600,000 to 800,000 under Article 7(3)(b), and Article 7(13)(a) also deducts 2 points from the licence. How many adults may ride together is explained in our guide on how many people can ride one motorbike.

Helmets: the rule and the exception

Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the rider and every passenger to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the chin strap properly fastened. The article sets no minimum age: the duty covers children as much as adults.

The exception sits in the penalty, not in the duty. Under Article 7(2)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, the rider is fined VND 400,000 to 600,000 for carrying a passenger without a fastened motorcycle helmet, except when the passenger is a sick person on the way to emergency care, a child under six, or a person being escorted for a violation. So a rider carrying a child under six without a helmet is not fined, but the law still expects that child to wear one. From the age of six, a child without a helmet costs the rider the same as an adult without one.

The rider's own helmet is covered separately by Article 7(2)(h), with the same fine band, and an adult passenger without a helmet can also be fined personally under Article 12(5)(b).

Can a small child sit in front?

Many families put a small child in front of the rider. The decree allows this for the youngest children only, through an exception in a much heavier penalty. Article 7(9)(h) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines a rider VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 for sitting behind another person and reaching round them to steer, unless the person in front is a child under six. The same act also leads to suspension of the right to use the driving licence for 10 to 12 months under Article 7(12)(b).

In practice:

  • a child under six may sit on the seat in front of the rider;
  • a child of six or older belongs behind the rider, never between the rider and the handlebars.

Weaving, group speeding and the other acts in the same group are explained in our guide to stunts, weaving and no-hands riding.

Bans that apply to children too

The passenger bans in Article 33(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15 apply to every passenger, whatever their age. A child on the bike may not:

  • carry bulky items;
  • hold an umbrella;
  • hold on to, pull or push another vehicle;
  • stand on the seat or the luggage rack, or sit on the handlebars.

The rider pays for some of these. Carrying a person who stands on the seat or the rack or sits on the handlebars is fined VND 600,000 to 800,000 under Article 7(3)(e) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and carrying a passenger who uses an umbrella is fined VND 200,000 to 400,000 under Article 7(1)(d). In the rainy season, a poncho that covers the child is the legal alternative to an umbrella; our guide to riding in heavy rain has more on rain gear.

Choosing a helmet for a child

A helmet only protects a child if it fits. An adult helmet on a small head slides over the eyes or comes off in a fall, however tightly the strap is pulled. A few habits help:

  • Buy with the child present. Helmet shops in Vietnam sell children's sizes; try several and choose one that sits level on the head and does not move when the child shakes their head.
  • Fasten the strap every time. The law asks for a properly fastened strap, and an open strap is the most common reason a helmet comes off.
  • Choose a light model for small children. A heavy helmet tires a young neck on longer rides.
  • Replace a helmet after a crash, even if it looks undamaged.

Practical safety on family rides

The law sets the minimum. These habits make short family rides safer:

  • Keep legs away from the exhaust. The exhaust pipe gets hot enough to burn bare skin. Make sure children keep their feet on the footrests, and dress them in long trousers and closed shoes; our guide on what to wear on a motorbike covers clothing.
  • Seat children where they can hold on. A child behind the rider holds the rider's waist; between two adults, the adult behind can keep a hand on the child. Our guide to riding pillion explains how passengers should sit and lean.
  • Ride slowly and brake early. Extra weight lengthens braking distances, and a child can move suddenly.
  • Watch for sleepiness. A child who nods off can slip sideways. Stop as soon as you notice it.
  • Avoid the hottest hours and heavy rain. Children overheat faster than adults; see our guide to riding in extreme heat.
  • Keep trips short. For long distances or very small children, a car is usually the safer choice.

Frequently asked questions

Do babies and toddlers need a helmet? The duty in Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 has no age limit. The rider is not fined for a child under six without a helmet (Article 7(2)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP), but a child too small for any helmet is better off not travelling by motorbike at all.

May I carry my partner and our child on one scooter? Yes, if the child is under 12: one of the two passengers is then a child under 12 (Article 33(1)(c)). Everyone wears a fastened helmet.

May an older child ride a scooter alone? Not before 16. Riders must be at least 16 for mopeds and 18 for motorbikes that need a licence (Article 59(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15); our guide to the minimum riding age explains the classes.

Does the under-12 rule mean three children may ride? No. The exception raises the limit from one passenger to two, not more (Article 33(1)).

Summary

A rider may carry one passenger, or two when one is a child under 12 (Article 33(1)(c) of Law 36/2024/QH15). Every passenger needs a fastened helmet (Article 33(2)); the rider's penalty spares only children under six (Article 7(2)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Only a child under six may sit in front of the rider (Article 7(9)(h)), and the passenger bans of Article 33(4) apply to children too.

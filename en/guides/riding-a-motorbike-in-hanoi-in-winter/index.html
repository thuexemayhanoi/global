Riding a Motorbike in Hanoi in Winter

What winter in Hanoi means on a rented motorbike: cold spells, drizzle and fog, the lighting and safe-distance rules, how to dress in layers and how to plan rides into the cold north.

Many visitors picture Vietnam as hot all year and are surprised by Hanoi in winter. The north has a real cool season, and on a motorbike the wind makes a mild grey day feel much colder. Add drizzle that leaves the roads slick and fog that cuts visibility, and winter riding needs a different plan from a sunny afternoon in October. This guide explains what winter weather in the Hanoi area looks like, which traffic rules matter most in cold, wet and foggy conditions, how to dress and how to plan rides out of the city. For the wider picture on safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What winter in the Hanoi area looks like

Winter in the north arrives in waves. Periods of mild, dry weather alternate with cold spells, when temperatures drop sharply for a few days. A study of ten weather stations in the Red River Delta, the lowland region that includes Hanoi, published in the Vietnam Journal of Hydrometeorology in 2022, counted on average about 6.2 severe cold spells and 3.2 damaging cold spells each year between 1981 and 2020, most of them lasting one to four days (Tạp chí Khí tượng Thủy văn, 2022). The same study found that the number of these spells has tended to fall over the decades, but they remain a regular part of the season.

For riders this means two things. First, check the forecast before each day out, because a ride that is pleasant on Monday can be miserable on Wednesday. Second, expect the cold to feel stronger on the bike than on the pavement: at 40 km/h the wind cools exposed skin far faster than standing still.

Drizzle and slippery roads

Hanoi's winter rain is usually not the heavy downpour of the summer but a light, persistent drizzle. It does not look dangerous, yet it wets the road just enough to lift dust and oil to the surface, and painted lines, manhole covers and smooth tiles near shopfronts become slippery. Brake earlier and more gently than usual, avoid sudden swerves, and stay off road markings when you lean into a turn. Our guide to riding a scooter in Hanoi during heavy rain covers braking and visibility in wet conditions in more detail.

Fog and the lighting rule

Fog is common on cold mornings, on the dykes along the Red River and above all in the mountains north of Hanoi. Under Article 20(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the headlight must be on not only between 18:00 and 06:00 but also in fog, smoke, dust, rain or any other bad weather that limits visibility, at any time of day. Riding without lights in these conditions costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. In practice: switch the headlight on whenever the air turns grey, and keep it on low beam so that it does not reflect back from the fog.

Speed and safe distance in bad weather

The law asks riders to adapt to the conditions, not just to the speed limit. Article 12(2) requires a speed that suits the road, the traffic density, the terrain and the weather, and Article 12(3) requires riders to watch out, slow down or stop in rain, wind, fog, smoke and dust and on slippery or muddy roads. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets minimum safe distances in Article 11, for example 35 metres at 60 km/h on a dry, straight road, and says the distance must be greater in rain, fog, on bends and on mountain passes. Our guide to motorbike speed limits in Vietnam lists the general limits; in winter weather, the safe speed is often well below them.

Dressing for a winter ride

Layers work better than one thick jacket, because the temperature can change a lot between a sunny midday and a foggy evening:

  • Base layer: a thin thermal top that keeps sweat away from the skin.
  • Middle layer: a fleece or a light down jacket.
  • Outer layer: a windproof jacket, ideally water-resistant, that closes at the neck and wrists.
  • Hands and neck: full-finger gloves and a scarf or buff; cold hands react more slowly on the brake levers.
  • Rain gear on top: when the drizzle starts, put on a rain suit before your clothes are wet, because damp layers lose their warmth quickly.

A helmet with a visor keeps the wind off your face. If the visor fogs up on cold mornings, open it slightly while riding slowly or wipe it at a stop; never ride blind behind a misted visor.

Short days and long breaks

Winter days are shorter than summer days, so the light fades earlier in the afternoon, and on grey days dusk seems to come even sooner. Plan city rides and day trips so that the last stretch is not in the dark. On longer rides, stop more often than you would in warm weather: every hour or so, get off the bike, move your fingers and toes, have a hot drink and check whether your hands are still warm enough to brake firmly. Numb fingers and a stiff neck are signs to take a longer break or to finish the day.

Riding into the mountains

Many visitors use the cool season for trips into the northern mountains. There, everything above is stronger: colder air at altitude, thicker fog in the passes, and wet, sometimes muddy roads on the climbs. Plan shorter stages, start later in the morning once fog has lifted, and make sure you arrive before dark, because fog and darkness together make mountain roads very difficult. Our guide to riding a motorbike at night in Vietnam explains why foreign ministries advise against riding after dark.

Check the bike for winter

Before a cold-weather ride, look at the parts that matter most in wet, dim conditions: tyre tread and pressure, both brakes, the headlight on high and low beam, the tail and brake lights and the indicators. Our pickup checklist lists what to inspect at handover. If the bike will not start on a cold morning or something fails on the road, our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains the next steps.

In short

Winter in the Hanoi area brings regular cold spells, fine drizzle and fog (Tạp chí Khí tượng Thủy văn). Switch on the headlight in fog and drizzle at any time of day (Article 20), slow down and keep more distance than the dry-road minimum (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT), dress in windproof layers with gloves, and plan mountain stages so that you finish before dark.

Riding Motorbikes in a Group in Vietnam

What Vietnamese law says about riding in a group: no riding abreast, heavy penalties for group speeding and convoys that block traffic, and how to organise a group motorbike trip safely.

Riding through Vietnam with friends or a tour group on rental motorbikes makes for some of the best memories of a trip. Groups also make typical mistakes: riding side by side to chat, following the bike in front with no gap, speeding up so as not to lose touch. Vietnamese law has specific rules and stiff penalties for several of these situations. This guide explains what the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP say about groups and gives practical tips for a trip on which everyone arrives safely. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

No riding abreast

Article 33(3)(a) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety forbids motorbike and moped riders to ride side by side in a row. That applies even when the road is wide and empty and you would like to talk. Riding three or more abreast costs 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(k) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For a group that means single file, each rider in their own line, with space between bikes.

Speeding together is especially expensive

Groups that egg each other on risk one of the heaviest penalties for motorbike riders. Under Article 7(9)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, two or more vehicles exceeding the speed limit as a group costs 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND each, and Article 7(12)(b) adds a licence suspension of 10 to 12 months. That is far more than a single speeding offence; our guide to motorbike speed limits gives the limits themselves.

Convoys that block traffic

A slow group can be a problem too. Under Article 7(9)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, riding in a convoy that obstructs traffic, without permission from the competent authority, costs 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND, again with a possible licence suspension of 10 to 12 months. A tour group should never fill the whole road as a block, stop others from overtaking or push through junctions together so that nobody gets left behind.

Other rules groups often break

  • Weaving and racing: Article 9(5) of the law bans racing, encouraging races, weaving and revving engines.
  • Constant honking: continuous honking or revving in residential areas costs 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND under Article 7(9)(k) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guide to horn rules explains when the horn is allowed.
  • Distance: Article 12(1) requires a safe minimum distance from the vehicle ahead. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets 35 metres at 60 km/h on a dry, straight road, and more in rain, fog, on bends and on passes.
  • Overtaking: each rider overtakes alone, with a signal and only where it is allowed.

Before you set off: organise the group

A good group ride starts in the car park, not on the road:

1. Agree the route and stops. Everyone knows the day's destination, fuel stops and breaks, and has the route on their own phone so nobody has to follow blindly. 2. Pick a leader and a sweep. The most experienced rider leads at a pace the least confident rider can manage; a second experienced rider rides at the back. 3. Meeting points, not keeping up. Agree fixed places where the group waits, for example at every major turn or every 30 to 45 minutes, so nobody has to race or run an amber light to stay in touch. 4. Swap numbers. Everyone saves the others' phone numbers and the rental shop's. 5. Agree signals. Simple hand signals for stop, fuel and hazards on the road help without anyone needing the horn.

Papers and passengers: for each rider

In a group it is easy to assume that "someone" has the papers. The law sees it differently: under Article 56(1), everyone riding a motorbike needs a valid licence for that vehicle and must carry the registration certificate and insurance certificate of their own bike. Our guide to which papers to carry lists them.

Passenger rules also apply bike by bike: under Article 33(1), one passenger is the rule, two only in exceptional cases, and everyone wears a helmet. Bikes with a passenger and luggage are slower and brake less well; do not put them at the back, where riders most often have to catch up.

On the road: space, not a pack

Ride in single file with enough space for everyone to brake and swerve on their own. Riders in the middle look well ahead, not just at the bike in front. At junctions and lights, each rider decides alone: if the light turns amber, the next rider stops even if that splits the group, and you regroup at the next meeting point. If a rider drops back, the sweep stays with them and the leader waits at the next meeting point. After a breakdown the whole group stops together at a safe spot beside the road, not on it; our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains the rest.

If something happens

If someone in the group falls or there is a collision with others, everyone involved stops. Anyone involved in an accident who does not stop at once, secure the scene or help the injured faces a fine of 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND under Article 7(5)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP; anyone who causes an accident and leaves faces 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND and loses 6 points under Article 7(9)(c) and 7(13)(c). Secure the scene, give first aid and call for help; our guide to what to do after a motorbike accident explains the steps.

Expressways and riding after dark

Two mistakes are especially common in groups. A group that follows the navigation app blindly can end up on a slip road to an expressway, which is closed to motorbikes; see our guide to motorbikes and expressways. And because groups move more slowly, stages drift into the dark. Plan shorter days and read our guide to riding at night.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to ride two abreast on motorbikes in Vietnam? No. Article 33(3)(a) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety forbids motorbike and moped riders to ride side by side in a row, even when the road is wide and empty. Riding three or more abreast is fined under Article 7(3)(k) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. A group should ride in single file, each rider in their own line, with space between bikes.

Can every rider in a group be fined for speeding together? Yes. Under Article 7(9)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, two or more vehicles exceeding the speed limit as a group are each fined, and Article 7(12)(b) adds a licence suspension of 10 to 12 months. That is far more than a single speeding offence, so never speed up just to keep in touch with the group.

How can a motorbike group stay together without racing? Agree fixed meeting points instead of trying to keep up, for example at every major turn or every 30 to 45 minutes. Pick an experienced leader who rides at a pace the least confident rider can manage, and a second experienced rider as sweep at the back. If a light turns amber and splits the group, the next rider stops and everyone regroups at the next meeting point.

In short

Riding abreast is banned (Article 33); three or more abreast costs 600,000 to 800,000 VND, and group speeding or a convoy that blocks traffic costs 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND plus a licence suspension under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Ride in single file with space, agree meeting points instead of keeping up at all costs, and let the least confident rider set the pace.

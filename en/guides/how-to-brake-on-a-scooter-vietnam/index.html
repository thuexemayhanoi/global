How to Brake on a Scooter

Why the front brake does most of the work on a scooter, how to use both brakes smoothly, how to brake in bends and in the wet, and what ABS on a rental scooter can and cannot do.

Plenty of visitors ride a scooter for the first time in Vietnam, and many bring a habit from their bicycle: when in doubt, use only the back brake, because the front one "sends you over the handlebars". On a scooter that habit is dangerous. This guide explains how the two brakes work together, how to apply them, what changes in a bend and in the wet, and what an anti-lock braking system on a rental scooter actually does. It draws on the motorcycle manual of the Oregon Driver and Motor Vehicle Services, Honda Vietnam's own information and Vietnamese rules. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Two brakes, two controls

On most automatic scooters both brake levers sit on the handlebars: the front brake on the right and the rear brake on the left. On geared bikes such as the Honda Wave, the rear brake is a pedal under your right foot instead. Ask the rental shop to show you the layout on your bike, and try both brakes before you set off. Our guide to the Honda Wave, Vision, Air Blade and Lead explains which common rental models are scooters and which are geared.

The front brake does most of the work

The Oregon manual is clear: a motorcycle has a brake on each wheel, and you should use both every time you slow or stop. Used properly, the front brake can supply 70 percent or more of the total stopping power, and using both brakes gives the shortest stopping distance (Oregon DMV, Section 4). The reason is physics. When you brake, weight shifts forward, pressing the front tyre into the road so that it can transmit more force, while the rear wheel goes light.

Squeeze, don't grab

How you brake matters as much as which brake you use. The manual advises applying the front brake lever and the rear brake smoothly and evenly until the bike stops. Grabbing the lever or stamping on the pedal can lock a wheel, and a locked wheel means lost control (Oregon DMV). In practice:

1. Start early. Roll off the throttle first, then begin gently with both brakes. 2. Build the pressure. Increase it progressively rather than clamping down at once. 3. Stop in a straight line. Keep the handlebars straight while you brake hard. 4. Ease off at the end. Release a little just before you stop so the scooter does not jerk.

Braking in a bend

In a bend, part of the tyres' grip is already busy holding the corner. If you have to stop while leaning, the manual advises applying both brakes lightly and gradually while reducing the lean. The more upright the bike becomes, the more grip is available for braking and the harder you can brake. Too much or too sudden pressure can start a skid (Oregon DMV). The answer is to brake before the bend, not in it. Our guide to narrow mountain roads covers bends where the road is tight.

Linked brakes and ABS

Some motorcycles have an integrated braking system that links the front and rear brakes to a single control; the owner's manual explains how it works on a particular model (Oregon DMV). Ask the shop whether your scooter has one.

Anti-lock brakes are fitted, for example, to the special edition of the Honda LEAD: Honda Vietnam lists ABS on the front wheel for that version (Honda Vietnam, LEAD ABS). The LEAD 125 owner's manual points out two limits: the ABS does not work below about 10 km/h, and the rear wheel has no ABS (LEAD 125 owner's manual). ABS helps stop the front wheel locking under hard braking, but it does not automatically shorten your stopping distance and it does not replace a safe gap.

In the wet and on loose surfaces

Grip drops on wet roads, painted markings, manhole covers and gravel. Vietnamese law expressly requires riders to watch out and slow down in rain and on slippery or muddy surfaces (Article 12(3)(m) of Law 36/2024/QH15), and Article 11 of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT requires a bigger following distance in the rain. Brake earlier and more gently in the wet, and avoid braking hard on painted lines. Our guides to riding in the rainy season and safe following distance have more.

With a passenger, luggage or a long descent

A passenger or luggage makes the scooter heavier, and stopping distances grow. Brake earlier, and tell your passenger to hold on when you need to slow down hard.

On long mountain descents, do not roll down with the brakes dragging all the way. Ride slowly and slow down in firm, deliberate stages with both brakes, so the brakes do not overheat, and take a break on very long descents. On passes and slopes, the law requires you to watch out and slow down anyway (Article 12(3)(d)).

Practise an emergency stop

An emergency stop is worth practising before you need one. That is why UK compulsory basic training includes a controlled stop from a straight line and an emergency stop, both using both brakes (GOV.UK, CBT element C). Find an empty, level space, ride off slowly and brake to a stop with both brakes from a low speed. Raise the speed only when that feels secure.

Check the brakes at pickup

Test both brakes before you ride away: each lever should bite firmly well before it touches the grip, and the brake light should come on with either lever. Missing or ineffective brakes are also an offence: under Article 14(1)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, the rider faces 400,000 to 600,000 VND.

Frequently asked questions

Isn't it safer to use only the rear brake? No. With the rear brake alone you give away most of your stopping power; the Oregon manual recommends using both brakes every time (Oregon DMV).

What if a brake lever suddenly feels soft? Stop somewhere safe and do not ride on until the shop or a mechanic has checked the brake. Under Article 14(1)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a bike with ineffective brakes may not be ridden in any case.

Do I need a scooter with ABS? ABS is a useful aid, especially in the wet. It replaces neither a suitable speed nor a safe gap, and depending on the model it does not work on the rear wheel or at very low speed (LEAD 125 owner's manual).

In short

Use both brakes every time; the front brake does most of the work (Oregon DMV). Squeeze progressively, brake before bends and earlier in the wet, and check the brakes when you collect the bike.

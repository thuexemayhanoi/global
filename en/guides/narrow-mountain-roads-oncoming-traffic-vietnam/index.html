Narrow Mountain Roads in Vietnam: Who Gives Way

Who must give way on narrow mountain roads in Vietnam, where overtaking, U-turns and stopping are banned, when to sound the horn before a bend and how to park a motorbike on a slope.

Ride a rental motorbike into northern Vietnam and sooner or later you will be on narrow mountain roads: steep ramps, tight bends, barely room for two vehicles, and trucks crawling uphill. For many visitors this is the best part of the trip and the most demanding. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety has clear rules on who gives way when you meet oncoming traffic, where overtaking and U-turns are banned and how to park on a slope. This guide sums them up, with the amounts set by Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Meeting oncoming traffic: slow down and keep right

On roads without separated carriageways, which describes most mountain roads, Article 17(1) of the law sets a simple rule: when two vehicles meet, both slow down and keep to the right in their direction of travel. On a narrow road that means moving to the right-hand edge early, rather than riding in the middle and swerving at the last moment.

Who gives way at narrow points

Where the road is so narrow that only one vehicle fits, Article 17(2) decides who waits:

  • Passing place: on a road only one vehicle wide with a passing place, the vehicle nearer the passing place pulls in and lets the other pass.
  • Gradient: the vehicle going downhill gives way to the one coming uphill.
  • Obstacle: the vehicle with an obstacle ahead lets the vehicle with a clear road go first.

The logic is practical: a vehicle climbing a hill is harder to get moving again than one rolling downhill, especially a heavily loaded truck. The road sign regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT also has mandatory sign P.132, which orders drivers at narrow points to give priority to oncoming traffic.

Getting this wrong, by passing incorrectly or failing to give way to oncoming vehicles at narrow points, on slopes or at obstacles, costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(h) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Slow down where the view ends

Article 12(3) of the law requires you to watch, slow down or stop in certain situations. In the mountains the relevant ones are narrow roads, bends, winding stretches, passes and slopes, animals on or beside the road, and rain, fog, slippery or muddy surfaces and stones or debris on the road. On a mountain route you can meet all of these within a few kilometres.

Your distance to the vehicle in front has to grow as well. Article 11 of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT requires a larger safe distance in rain and fog, on slippery roads and on winding stretches and passes than on a dry, straight road. Our guide to motorbike speed limits covers the speeds that go with it.

Sound the horn before blind bends

Before sharp bends and on passes with gradients above 10 percent and limited visibility, you will often find mandatory sign R.309 "Ấn còi": when you pass it, you must sound the horn (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). A short beep warns oncoming traffic you cannot yet see. Our guide to horn rules explains when else the horn is allowed.

Where overtaking and U-turns are banned

Mountain routes are full of places where overtaking is off limits. Article 14(6) of the law bans it, among other places, on narrow single-lane bridges, on bends with limited visibility, near the crest of a hill with limited visibility on two-way roads, and whenever weather or road conditions do not allow safe overtaking. Overtaking there anyway costs 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND under Article 7(4)(c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guide to overtaking rules has more.

U-turns are banned by Article 15(4) on narrow roads, on slopes and on bends with obstructed views, among other places. If you have taken a wrong turn, ride on to a wide stretch with a clear view.

When a truck wants to overtake you

On mountain roads you often end up in front of something faster. Article 14(4) of the law says what the vehicle in front must do: watch the road ahead and, when it is safe, slow down, signal right and keep to the right-hand edge until the other vehicle has passed, without obstructing it. If the road ahead is not clear, for example before a blind bend, signal left to show that it is not yet safe to overtake. For riders that means holding your line calmly rather than diving onto the verge, and making room as soon as the road allows.

Stopping and parking on a slope

Stopping calls for care in the mountains too. Article 18(4)(b) bans stopping or parking on bends or near the crest of a hill where the view is obstructed. For parking on a slope, Article 18(2) requires you to turn the steering towards the edge of the road and secure the wheels. On a motorbike, in practice, find a level spot with a clear view and off the roadway for photo stops. If the bike does have to stand on a slope, make sure it cannot roll or tip off its side stand.

If a breakdown leaves the bike on the roadway, Article 18(7) requires you to warn others with the hazard lights or a warning sign behind the vehicle. Our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down covers the next steps.

Practical tips for mountain trips

  • Start early. Plan mountain stages so you arrive before dark; our guide to riding at night explains why.
  • Spare your brakes. Take long descents slowly instead of braking all the way down, and stop for breaks.
  • Watch the weather. After rain, roads can be muddy with stones on the surface, which makes the duty of care in Article 12 of the law all the more pressing.
  • Check the bike. Brakes, tyres and lights should be in good order before a mountain trip.

Frequently asked questions

Does "uphill before downhill" apply to trucks too? Yes. Article 17(2)(b) speaks of vehicles in general: whoever goes downhill lets the vehicle going uphill pass, motorbike or truck. Even so, do not count on a heavy truck stopping when you have priority uphill; the motorbike is usually the vehicle that can stop more easily.

May I stop on a bend for a quick photo? Not if the view there is obstructed. Article 18(4)(b) bans stopping on bends and near crests with obstructed views. Ride on to a wide spot with a clear view.

In short

On narrow roads both vehicles slow down and keep right; at narrow points the vehicle nearer the passing place gives way, and downhill gives way to uphill (Article 17). Ignoring this costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND, and overtaking where it is banned 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Sound the horn at R.309 signs before blind bends, never stop on a blind bend and park the bike securely on slopes.

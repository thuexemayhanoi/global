Zebra Crossings in Vietnam: When Motorbikes Must Stop

Do motorbikes have to stop for pedestrians in Vietnam? When the law says to slow down or stop, why green does not mean go regardless, the rules when turning and what ignoring them costs.

Cross a street on foot in Hanoi and you soon learn to walk slowly and steadily through the stream of motorbikes. On the bike you see the other side: pedestrians appear between vehicles, in the middle of the road and on the zebra crossing. Many visitors wonder whether pedestrians have any priority in Vietnam at all. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety is clearer than everyday traffic suggests. This guide explains when you must slow down or stop, what applies when you turn and what breaking the rules costs under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

How crossings are marked

Besides the white stripes on the road, crossings have their own signs. The road sign regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT uses warning sign W.224 "Đường người đi bộ cắt ngang" ahead of a pedestrian crossing and information sign I.423 "Vị trí người đi bộ sang ngang" at the crossing itself. When you see either, expect people on the roadway.

What the law asks of pedestrians

Knowing pedestrians' duties helps you read their moves. Under Article 30(1) of the law, pedestrians walk on the pavement, the verge or a footpath, or at the right-hand edge of the road where there is none. They may cross only at lights, marked crossings, footbridges or underpasses; where none exists, they must watch approaching vehicles, cross only when it is safe and give a hand signal while crossing. So someone at the kerb with a raised hand wants to cross.

Children under seven may cross only with an adult under Article 30(2)(a), and point (đ) obliges everyone to help children under seven, pregnant women, older and frail people and people with disabilities to cross. On a motorbike that means at the very least stopping and giving them time.

The core duty: slow down or stop

Article 12(3)(a) of the law requires every rider to watch and slow down or stop at pedestrian crossings and other markings for pedestrians, and wherever pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing. The duty is not limited to the zebra crossing; it also applies where someone crosses away from one.

Green does not mean go regardless

Many visitors are surprised that a green light is not a licence to carry on at any cost. Under Article 11(4)(a) green means go, but if pedestrians or wheelchair users are still on the roadway you must slow down or stop to let them cross. On a flashing amber light you may proceed under point (b), but you must watch, slow down or stop to give way to pedestrians, wheelchair users and other vehicles. Our guide to traffic light rules explains the lights in full.

Turning: pedestrians first

Turning is where many dangerous moments happen. Article 15(3) requires you to give way to pedestrians, non-motorised vehicles and oncoming traffic when turning, and to turn only when you do not obstruct or endanger anyone. The decree distinguishes two cases:

  • At a marked crossing: failing to give way to pedestrians or wheelchair users on the crossing when turning costs 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND under Article 7(5)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Without markings: failing to give way to pedestrians crossing where there is no marked crossing costs the same under Article 7(5)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Both also cost 2 points under Article 7(13)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Our guide to U-turns and left turns covers turning more generally.

No overtaking, no stopping on the crossing

Two more rules concern the crossing itself. Overtaking is banned on pedestrian crossings and while pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing, under Article 14(6)(h) and (i); doing it anyway costs 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND under Article 7(4)(c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. And nobody stops or parks on a crossing: Article 18(4)(g) bans it, and Article 7(2)(e) of the decree charges 400,000 to 600,000 VND (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). That includes waiting at a red light: stop before the crossing, not on it.

High beam dazzles

At night, one more courtesy applies. Using high beam while pedestrians are crossing costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(h) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Dip your lights as soon as you see people ahead; our guide to riding at night has more.

If it ends in a collision

Failing to watch, slow down or stop where required, and causing an accident as a result, costs 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, with 10 points deducted under Article 7(13)(d). Our guide on what to do after a motorbike accident explains the next steps.

Practical tips

  • Look far ahead. Watch for people at the roadside waiting for a gap.
  • Ride steadily. Pedestrians in Hanoi expect traffic to flow evenly around them; sudden swerves or bursts of speed put them in danger.
  • Give the slow more time. Children, older people and anyone unsure on their feet need longer. If in doubt, stop.
  • The pavement is not a lane. Under Article 33(3)(b) motorbike riders may not ride into areas reserved for pedestrians.

When you are the pedestrian

Off the bike, the same law applies to you from the other side. Cross at lights, marked crossings, footbridges or underpasses where they exist; where there are none, watch the approaching traffic, cross only when it is safe and raise a hand as you go (Article 30(1)(b)). Walk at an even pace so riders can judge where you will be. Having crossed a busy Hanoi street on foot once, most riders become noticeably more patient at the crossing.

Frequently asked questions

Must I stop for someone just standing at the kerb? The duty in Article 12(3)(a) applies at crossings and wherever pedestrians are crossing. Someone at a crossing with a raised hand wants to go, so slow down and let them cross.

May I dodge a jam along the pavement? No. Riding on the pavement, except to cross it into a house or office entrance, costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, plus 2 points under Article 7(13)(a).

In short

At crossings and wherever people are crossing, slow down or stop, even on green (Articles 11 and 12). Pedestrians have priority when you turn; ignoring that costs 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND and 2 points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Never overtake on a crossing or stop on it.

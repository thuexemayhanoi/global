Rental Agreements, Changes and Cancellations

Review what to ask about rental date changes, cancellation fees, extensions, early returns and deposits before signing a Hanoi scooter contract.

Travel plans change: a flight slips, a typhoon crosses northern Vietnam, or after two days you realise you do not need the bike at all. What then happens to your booking deposit, your reservation and a rental already under way in Hanoi is governed by the Vietnamese Civil Code and, far more often, by the clauses you signed. This guide explains both: the legal logic of deposits, cancellation and termination, the cancellation deadlines Hanoi shops typically use, how to change dates, extend, return early or add a rider cleanly, and which evidence to keep. The handover of the bike itself is covered in our Hanoi motorbike rental pickup checklist.

Two deposits, two sets of rules

A motorbike rental often involves two payments that both get called a deposit: the booking deposit paid before you arrive and the security deposit paid at handover. The booking deposit is governed by Article 328 of the Civil Code of 2015, explained by the Supreme People's Court journal: a deposit secures the conclusion or performance of a contract; the party who paid it and then refuses to perform loses it, and the party who accepted it and then refuses must return it together with an equal amount, unless otherwise agreed. That last clause decides everything. Without a written cancellation rule you risk the whole booking deposit if you cancel; with a cancellation scale in the contract, the scale applies. So pay only a small booking deposit before arrival, pay in a traceable way, and ask for the cancellation terms in writing before you transfer anything.

Typical cancellation deadlines used by Hanoi shops

Deadlines are each shop's own business, but one published example shows the logic. The Hanoi shop MOTOGO describes in its article on cancellation and refunds from September 2026 a scale under which cancelling seven or more days before the rental date returns 100 percent of the deposit, three to six days before returns 70 percent, one to two days before returns 50 percent, and cancelling on the rental day itself is not refunded. The same article names two exceptions: in cases of force majeure such as natural disasters, epidemics or war the customer may be refunded or rescheduled without a fee, and if the bike cannot be used because of a technical problem the customer may get a refund or another bike. That is one shop's rule, not an industry norm; others use different tiers or ask for no deposit at all. Ask precisely: which deadline, which percentage, counted in days or hours, and what applies when a typhoon warning is issued?

Changing a booking instead of cancelling it

Most changes can be made without loss if you raise them early and in writing. A different pickup time, another model or a different delivery address is rarely a problem for a shop with a fleet, and a change is cheaper for both sides than a cancellation and refund. Keep the time logic in mind: under MOTOGO's policy a rental day is a 24-hour period from handover, lateness is charged by the hour once you are more than 30 minutes late, and more than eight hours late counts as a full day. Moving pickup from 9 am to 3 pm therefore moves the return time too. Extensions should be agreed by message before the deadline, so the shop does not allocate the bike elsewhere and so you slide into the right price tier. Early return is the opposite case: without a clause there is rarely money back, so have the rule written into the contract first.

Adding a rider is a special case. According to the Ministry of Public Security, when an offence is detected by camera, Article 47(8) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP obliges the vehicle owner to cooperate with the authorities and identify the actual rider; an owner who cannot is penalised instead, and under Article 47(11) unresolved cases block registration, inspection and licence procedures. Shops therefore rightly insist that every person who rides the bike is named in the contract with their licence. Register an additional rider before the first ride, not after.

When the shop cancels or no bike is ready

Shops cancel too: the reserved bike has been crashed, delivery to your district falls through, or only a lesser model is waiting at pickup. Under Article 328, the recipient of a deposit who fails to perform must return it together with an equal amount unless otherwise agreed; in practice shops offer an equivalent replacement or an immediate refund. Accept a replacement only if it matches your booking, and have any price difference written down. Keep the booking confirmation, payment proof and chat history; they are your only evidence if the shop later claims nothing firm was agreed.

Ending a contract while the rental is running

A rental already under way is governed by Articles 423 and 428 of the Civil Code, whose text THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT reproduces. A party may cancel or unilaterally terminate without paying compensation when the other party seriously breaches its obligations, when the parties have agreed so, or when the law provides for it; it must notify the other party immediately, and a party that terminates without such grounds is itself treated as in breach and liable. Translated into rental life: if the bike has a safety defect the shop will not fix, such as failing brakes, you may terminate and claim a refund for the unused days, but you must say so at once and in a provable way. If you simply hand the bike back early without a reason, whatever the contract says about early return stands. Document the defect with photos or video before returning the bike.

Evidence that ends most arguments

  • A written booking confirmation with model, period, price, deposit and cancellation terms.
  • Proof of payment for every deposit, with the plate number or booking reference in the reference field.
  • The chat history for every change: date, time, new terms and the shop's agreement.
  • Photos or video of the bike at handover and of every defect you report.
  • A receipt for every refund, with date and amount.

Five questions before paying a booking deposit

  • What are the cancellation deadlines, in days or hours, and what percentage comes back?
  • What happens on a typhoon warning, flooding or illness: refund, reschedule or loss?
  • Can I change the date, model or pickup place free of charge, and until when?
  • How do extension and early return work, and which price tier do I land in?
  • What does the shop offer if it cancels: a replacement of the same class or an immediate refund?

With those answers in writing, you can replan in Hanoi without your booking deposit becoming a loss.

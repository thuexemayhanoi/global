Safe Following Distance on a Motorbike in Vietnam

How much distance Vietnamese law requires between you and the vehicle ahead: the figures in metres from Circular 38/2024, when you need more space and what tailgating costs on a motorbike.

Arriving in Hanoi, most visitors are struck by how closely motorbikes ride, often barely an arm's length apart. It is easy to conclude that distance does not matter in Vietnam. It does. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety requires a safe distance, and a circular sets it in metres. This guide explains the distances that apply, when you must leave more room and what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP provides when it goes wrong. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law requires

Article 12(1) of the law obliges riders to keep to the speed rules and to a minimum safe distance from the vehicle ahead in the same lane or carriageway. Under Article 12(2) they must also adapt their speed to the condition of bridges and roads, traffic density, terrain, weather and other factors. Article 12(4) leaves the details to the responsible ministry, which set them out in Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025.

The distances in metres

For dry, straight and level roads without fog and with good visibility, Article 11(2)(a) of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets these minimum distances:

| Speed | Minimum distance | |---|---| | 60 km/h | 35 metres | | Over 60 to 80 km/h | 55 metres | | Over 80 to 100 km/h | 70 metres | | Over 100 to 120 km/h | 100 metres |

The first two rows matter most on a motorbike. Outside residential areas motorbikes may ride at no more than 70 km/h where signs do not say otherwise, and 60 km/h on roads without a central divider, while mopeds are limited to 40 km/h everywhere (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, Articles 6 and 7). For a sense of scale, 35 metres at 60 km/h is just over two seconds of travel. Our guide to motorbike speed limits covers the limits themselves.

Below 60 km/h the circular gives no fixed figure. Riders going slower must keep a reasonable distance from the vehicle ahead that suits the traffic density and the actual situation, under Article 11(2)(a) (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT). That is the rule in Hanoi's city traffic.

More distance in rain, fog and on passes

The table only applies in good conditions. In rain or fog, on slippery roads, on winding stretches and passes and whenever visibility is limited, Article 11(2)(b) requires a greater distance than the one on the sign or in the table. The law, too, requires extra attention and lower speed in rain, wind, fog, smoke, dust and on slippery or muddy roads (Article 12(3)(m)). Our guide to riding in heavy rain explains how to ride safely in the wet season.

The minimum distance sign

Where a sign sets a minimum distance, that figure applies: under Article 11(1) of the circular, your distance there may not be shorter than the number on the sign. The road sign regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT describes prohibitory sign P.121 "Cự ly tối thiểu giữa hai xe" as an instruction to cars, with the number giving the distance in metres. For motorbike riders, Article 7(3)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP also penalises failing to keep the distance shown on that sign, so respect it on two wheels as well.

Distance when overtaking, turning and to the side

Safe distance is not only about the vehicle in front. When overtaking, Article 14(5) of the law requires a safe distance to the vehicles ahead and behind throughout the manoeuvre. Before turning, Article 15(2) requires you to watch, keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down and signal. And sideways: under Article 33(3)(a), motorbike riders may not ride side by side. Riding with friends means single file with space between bikes; our guide to riding in a group has more.

What tailgating costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP targets the consequences:

  • Running into the vehicle ahead because the gap was too short, or not keeping the distance shown on a sign: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(i) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Causing an accident by not keeping the required distance: 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a), with 10 points deducted under Article 7(13)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

On top of that come the repair bills for both vehicles. Our guide on what to do after a motorbike accident explains the first steps.

Keeping space in dense city traffic

In Hanoi's crush, a large gap can seem unrealistic: open one up and another bike slips into it. Keeping as much room as you can still pays off:

1. Stay out of blind spots. Do not ride right beside or close behind buses and lorries whose drivers cannot see you. 2. Keep space ahead. When someone cuts in, let them and rebuild the gap. 3. Ride ready to brake. Keep your fingers on the brake levers when it gets tight. 4. Let speed follow space. When the gap shrinks, slow down rather than closing up.

Our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders has more tips for city riding.

On rural and national roads

Out of town, distance matters even more: speeds are higher, and lorries and buses can brake suddenly. At 60 km/h keep at least the 35 metres from the table, and much more in the rain. Overtake only where it is allowed and the road ahead is clear; our guide to overtaking rules explains where.

Frequently asked questions

Does the table apply to mopeds? Mopeds may not exceed 40 km/h under Article 7 of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, but the table starts at 60 km/h. At lower speeds the general duty applies: keep a distance that suits the traffic density and situation.

Is an arm's length legal in a jam? Below 60 km/h the circular sets no figure. A penalty under Article 7(3)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP applies if you run into the vehicle ahead because the gap was too short, or ignore a minimum distance sign. Such a small gap is still not safe; leave enough room to brake.

In short

The law requires a minimum distance to the vehicle ahead (Article 12). In good conditions Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets 35 metres at 60 km/h and 55 metres up to 80 km/h, with more in rain, fog and on passes (Article 11). Running into the vehicle ahead costs 600,000 to 800,000 VND, and an accident up to 14,000,000 VND plus 10 points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

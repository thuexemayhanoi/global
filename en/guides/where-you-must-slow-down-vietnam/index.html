Where You Must Slow Down in Vietnam

Schools, markets, roadworks, narrow bridges, rain: the places where Vietnamese law requires motorbike riders to slow down or stop, whatever the posted speed limit says.

A speed limit in Vietnam is a ceiling, not a target. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety names a long list of places and situations where riders must watch, slow down or stop, even when the sign allows a higher speed. For visitors on a rental motorbike this is one of the most useful rules to know, because these are exactly the places where crashes happen. This guide walks through the list in Article 12 of the law, explains the warning signs that go with it and shows what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP provides when ignoring it ends in a crash. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The principle: a speed that suits the conditions

Article 12(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires every road user to keep to a speed that suits the condition of the bridge and road, the traffic density, the terrain, the weather and anything else that affects safety. Article 12(1) adds the duty to follow the speed rules and keep a safe minimum distance from the vehicle ahead in the same lane; our guide to safe following distance explains how much room that means.

The numbers themselves come from Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT: mopeds, for example, are limited to 40 km/h everywhere under Article 7. Our guide to motorbike speed limits lists the limits for each type of road. Those figures tell you how fast you may ride at most, not how fast you should ride.

The list in Article 12(3)

Article 12(3) lists the situations in which you must watch, slow down or stop. We have grouped them to make them easier to remember.

Where people step into the road

  • at pedestrian crossings and other markings or signs for pedestrians, and anywhere pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing (point a),
  • at stops where passengers are getting on or off (point i),
  • when you meet a group of people walking along the road (point k).

Our guide to pedestrian crossings explains when you must stop for people on foot.

At hazards in the road itself

  • at danger warning signs or obstacles on the road (point b),
  • when turning, or when your view is restricted (point c),
  • at junctions, level crossings, narrow roads, bends, winding roads, mountain passes and slopes (point d),
  • at narrow bridges and culverts, fords and spillways, underpasses and road tunnels (point đ).

Where many people gather

Point (e) names schools, hospitals, bus stations, public buildings that draw crowds, residential areas, markets, roadworks and the scene of a road accident. In Vietnam, markets and schools often sit right on the main road, and in the morning and early afternoon the roadside fills with children on bicycles or on the back of their parents' scooters.

Animals

When domestic animals are walking on the road or grazing beside it, you must slow down too (point g).

Other vehicles

  • when passing oncoming traffic, when a vehicle behind is overtaking you, and when the vehicle ahead signals to turn or gives an emergency signal such as hazard lights (point h),
  • when you meet vehicles carrying oversized, overweight or dangerous loads (point k),
  • when you meet a priority vehicle (point l); our guide to giving way to emergency vehicles explains what to do then.

Weather and road surface

Point (m) covers rain, wind, fog, smoke and dust, and slippery or muddy surfaces or roads strewn with earth, stones or fallen material. In the rainy season and on mountain roads after a landslide, that is an everyday situation; our guide to riding in the rainy season has practical tips.

Traffic checks

Finally, you must slow down where a traffic control is being carried out (point n). Our guide to traffic police stops explains what happens at a check.

An everyday example

You are riding along a country road with a 60 km/h limit and come into a village. There is a market at the roadside, parents are waiting with their scooters outside the school, and a bus has stopped to let passengers off. No sign lowers the limit, yet three cases from Article 12(3) apply at once: a market and a school, a stop with passengers getting off, and people crossing the road. Ride through at 60 km/h and you keep to the speed limit while breaking the law. The right speed is one at which you can stop at any moment, including when a child steps out from behind the bus.

Signs that warn you

Many of these places are announced by a triangular warning sign. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT:

  • W.224 "pedestrian crossing": vehicles must slow down, give way to pedestrians and continue only when they will not endanger them.
  • W.225 "children": it is placed before stretches where children often cross or play, such as near kindergartens and schools. Ride slowly and carefully, because children may run into the road without warning.
  • W.227 "roadworks": it warns of stretches where people and machines are working on the road. You must slow down enough not to endanger them.
  • W.241 "congestion": it warns of stretches where traffic jams are frequent.

What it costs

Under Article 7(10)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a motorbike rider who fails to watch, slow down or stop as required and causes an accident faces 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND, and Article 7(13)(d) takes 10 points off the licence. Exceeding the speed limit by more than 20 km/h costs 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND under Article 7(8)(a), plus 4 points under Article 7(13)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Our guide to the licence points system explains how points work.

Slowing down in practice

Slowing down is more than easing off the throttle. These habits help:

  • Cover the brakes. Rest your fingers on the brake levers near schools, markets and junctions so you can react without delay.
  • Look far ahead. Watch for children at the roadside, balls, open gates and buses pulling in.
  • Brake earlier than at home. In Vietnam people often step into the road without eye contact, and vehicles turn out of narrow lanes without stopping.
  • Ride within your sight line. On bends and passes, ride slowly enough to stop within the distance you can see.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a fixed speed outside schools? Article 12(3) gives no number. Where a specific limit applies, a sign shows it; otherwise ride slowly enough to stop in time.

Do I have to stop at an accident scene if I was not involved? You must slow down when passing in any case, because accident scenes are named in point (e). The law goes further: under Article 80(4), drivers passing the scene of a road accident are responsible for taking injured people for emergency treatment, with exceptions such as priority vehicles. Call the emergency services in any case; our guide to emergency numbers in Vietnam lists them.

In short

The speed limit is a ceiling. At pedestrian crossings, schools, markets, roadworks, narrow bridges and bends, near animals, in rain and at traffic checks you must watch, slow down or stop under Article 12(3). Failing to do so and causing a crash costs 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND and 10 points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

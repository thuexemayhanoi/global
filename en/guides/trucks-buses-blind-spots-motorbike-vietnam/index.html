Trucks and Buses: Blind Spots and Safe Distance

How to deal with trucks and coaches on a motorbike in Vietnam: blind spots, following distance, the room they need to turn, bus stops, overtaking and what the law requires when you are overtaken.

On Vietnam's national highways, scooters share the road with container lorries, tipper trucks and long-distance coaches that often travel fast and sound their horns hard. For travellers on a rental motorbike these are the most intimidating encounters of a trip, and they really are dangerous: the driver of a large vehicle often cannot see a scooter beside or close behind them. This guide explains where the blind spots are, how much distance makes sense, what applies when large vehicles turn and at bus stops, and what the law requires when a lorry overtakes you. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The driver often cannot see you

The UK's Highway Code sums up the problem neatly: large vehicles may need extra road space to turn or to deal with a hazard that you are not able to see. If you are following a large vehicle such as a bus or an articulated lorry, be aware that the driver may not be able to see you in the mirrors. And be prepared to stop and wait if it needs room or time to turn (GOV.UK, Highway Code, rule 221).

For a rider that means staying out of the areas the driver cannot see: directly behind the vehicle, alongside the cab and on the vehicle's right-hand side, where in Vietnam you can be squeezed when it turns right. A simple rule of thumb: if you cannot see the driver's eyes in their mirror, they cannot see you.

Distance buys visibility

Large vehicles also block your own view. The Highway Code advises dropping back to increase your distance, which improves your view of the road ahead and lets you plan (GOV.UK, rule 222). In Vietnam, Article 11 of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT also sets minimum following distances, such as 35 metres at 60 km/h in good visibility, and more in rain and on mountain passes. Behind a lorry, leave more than the minimum, because otherwise you will see neither the potholes nor the obstacles in front of it. Our guide to safe following distance explains the general rules.

A rider who crashes into the vehicle ahead because of too short a gap faces 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

When a lorry turns

Long vehicles swing wide when they turn, and the rear cuts the corner. At a junction, never stop beside a lorry or bus that might turn, and do not squeeze between it and the kerb. Wait behind it until it has turned, as Highway Code rule 221 also advises (GOV.UK).

Being overtaken: make room

When a lorry or bus wants to overtake you, Article 14(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets out what you must do: watch the road ahead and, when it is safe, slow down, signal right and keep to the right until the vehicle has passed, without obstructing it. Article 12(3)(h) also requires you to watch out and slow down when meeting oncoming traffic and when being overtaken.

Expect the blast of air from a passing lorry, which can push a light scooter sideways for a moment. Hold the handlebars loosely but firmly, and do not ride right at the very edge, where gravel and potholes collect.

When a lorry sounds its horn behind you

If a lorry or bus sounds its horn behind you, it usually wants to overtake. Under Article 21(1) of the law, the horn is there to warn of danger or to announce overtaking. Do not panic or swerve; hold your line, ease off a little and make room as soon as the road allows, as Article 14(4) requires. Our guide to horn rules explains when you may use your own horn.

Positioning in traffic

At traffic lights and in jams, scooters like to gather beside large vehicles. Stop behind a lorry or bus instead, far enough back to see its mirrors. In moving traffic, do not ride alongside a lorry for long; either stay well back or pass briskly where overtaking is allowed.

Oversized and dangerous loads

Article 12(3)(k) of the law expressly requires you to slow down or stop when you meet vehicles carrying oversized or overweight loads or dangerous goods. Keep a particularly large distance from such vehicles, and overtake them only on long, clear stretches where overtaking is allowed.

Buses at stops

At bus stops, passengers get off and often cross the road in front of or behind the bus. The law requires you to watch out and slow down at stops where passengers are getting on or off (Article 12(3)(i)). The Highway Code also advises giving way to buses pulling away from stops when it is safe to do so (GOV.UK, rule 223). So do not shoot past a stationary bus at the last moment.

On mountain roads

In the mountains, loaded lorries often crawl uphill at little more than walking pace and need the whole width of the road on hairpins. Do not slip past on the inside of a bend, where a long trailer can sweep across your path, and do not sit right behind one on a climb, where you see nothing of the road ahead. On descents, give lorries even more room, because a heavy vehicle needs far longer to stop than a scooter. Our guide to narrow mountain roads explains who gives way where the road is too narrow for two.

Overtaking a lorry

Overtaking a lorry takes longer than you think. Overtake only where it is allowed and you can see a long way ahead, do it briskly and do not linger alongside the cab. On blind mountain roads, stay behind. Our guide to overtaking rules explains where overtaking is banned.

At night and in the rain

At night, the headlights of large vehicles dazzle, and in the rain they throw up spray that badly limits your view. Leave even more room then, and plan longer stretches on national highways for daylight. Our guide to riding at night has more.

Frequently asked question

May I filter between lorries in a jam? It is strongly discouraged. Between large vehicles, drivers can hardly see you, and weaving breaks Article 9(5) of the law. For that, Article 7(9)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP provides 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND, plus a riding ban of 10 to 12 months under Article 7(12)(b).

In short

Lorry and bus drivers often cannot see scooters beside or close behind them; keep your distance and stay out of their blind spots (GOV.UK, Highway Code, rules 221 and 222). When overtaken, keep right and slow down (Article 14(4)), take extra care near oversized loads and at bus stops (Article 12(3)), and never stop beside a turning lorry.

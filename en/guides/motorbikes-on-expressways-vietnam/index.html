Motorbikes and Expressways in Vietnam

Why motorbikes and scooters may not use Vietnam's expressways, how to spot an expressway entrance, the fine and licence points under Decree 168/2024 and how to plan trips on other roads.

Plan a day trip out of Hanoi in a map app and the fastest route often runs along one of the new expressways. On a rental motorbike that route is not an option. Vietnamese law keeps motorbikes and mopeds off expressways altogether, whatever their engine size. This guide explains what the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety says, how to recognise an expressway before you reach the slip road, what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP charges riders who enter one, what to do if you end up on one by mistake and how to plan trips on roads you may use. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law says

Article 25(3) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety lists who may not travel on an expressway: specialised machinery whose design speed is below the expressway minimum, motorised four-wheel passenger and goods vehicles, motorbikes, mopeds and similar vehicles, non-motorised vehicles and pedestrians. The only exception is for the people, vehicles and equipment that manage and maintain the expressway.

For a visitor the rule is simple. A 110 cc automatic scooter, a 150 cc manual bike and an electric scooter are all excluded. It does not matter how empty or wide the road looks, or that your navigation app suggests it.

How to recognise an expressway

The national road sign regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT marks the start of an expressway with the guide sign IE.452 and its end, or the distance to its end, with IE.453. Signs and maps also use the Vietnamese word "cao tốc", which means expressway. If you see it at a slip road, stay on the ordinary road.

Expressways are not the only roads closed to motorbikes. Where the prohibition sign P.104 "No motorcycles" stands, all motorbikes except priority vehicles are banned; the sign does not stop you from walking a motorbike along that road (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). The sign P.105 closes a road to both cars and motorbikes. Look out for both at bridges and elevated sections. More signs are explained in our guide to Vietnam road signs for motorbike riders.

Why motorbikes are kept off

Expressways are built for fast, continuous traffic. Under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, the maximum speed on an expressway can be up to 120 km/h and the minimum is 60 km/h (Article 9), while mopeds may never ride faster than 40 km/h (Article 7). A motorbike in a stream of coaches and lorries at those speeds would be a serious danger to its rider and to everyone else. The general speed limits for motorbikes on other roads are explained in our guide to motorbike speed limits in Vietnam.

What entering an expressway costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP treats riding a motorbike or moped onto an expressway as a serious offence:

  • Entering an expressway: a fine of 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(b), and 6 points are deducted from the driving licence under Article 7(13)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Causing an accident after entering: the fine rises to 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a), with 10 points deducted under Article 7(13)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

A crash on an expressway can also raise difficult questions with your travel insurance; our guide to travel insurance and motorbike riding explains what to check before your trip.

If you end up on an expressway by mistake

A slip road can be badly signed, or a navigation app can lead you astray. The law does not describe what a motorbike rider should do then, but it sets clear limits for everyone on an expressway. U-turns are banned on expressways under Article 15(4) and reversing under Article 16(2). Driving in the emergency lane or on the shoulder is not allowed under Article 25(1)(c); vehicles may stop there only after a technical fault or another emergency, with the hazard lights on, under Article 25(2).

In practice: stay calm, do not turn round or reverse, keep a steady speed to the next exit and leave there. If you cannot continue safely, stop in the emergency lane with your hazard lights on and call the traffic police on 113.

Planning trips from Hanoi without expressways

For most destinations in the north, ordinary national and provincial roads run alongside the expressways and are open to motorbikes. Plan like this:

1. Check the route before you leave. Look at the suggested route in the map and watch for sections marked "cao tốc" or numbered with the prefix CT. 2. Use a two-wheeler setting. Some map apps offer a motorbike mode or an option to avoid highways. Use it where available, and still check the result. 3. Allow more time. Ordinary roads pass through towns and villages and are slower. Plan breaks and aim to arrive before dark; our guide to riding at night explains why. 4. Choose a suitable bike. A moped limited to 40 km/h is too slow for long stretches of national road. Our comparison of manual and automatic rentals helps you choose. 5. Consider other transport. For distant destinations it can make more sense to take a bus or train and rent a motorbike locally.

Talk your plans through with the rental shop and save its phone number. If the bike lets you down on the way, our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains the next steps.

On national roads: speed and distance

Taking the national road instead of the expressway means sharing it with lorries, coaches and cars. Article 12 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety requires riders to adapt their speed to the road, the traffic and the weather and to keep a safe distance from the vehicle ahead. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets that distance in its Article 11, for example 35 metres at 60 km/h on a dry, straight road, and more in rain, fog, on bends and on mountain passes. On roads that run through one village after another, the posted maximum is rarely the right speed.

Common misunderstandings

"A wide ring road must be an expressway." Not every wide road is an expressway. What counts is the signage: the IE.452 sign marks where an expressway begins (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). At the same time, a P.104 sign can close an ordinary road or bridge to motorbikes, so read the signs at every slip road.

"The navigation app sent me there." That does not protect you from the fine under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. The person riding the vehicle is responsible for following the signs.

In short

Motorbikes and mopeds may not use expressways in Vietnam under Article 25(3). Entering one costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND and 6 licence points under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and much more if it ends in an accident. Watch for the IE.452 sign and the word "cao tốc", plan trips on national roads and allow extra time. Before your first ride, our rental pickup checklist helps you check the bike.

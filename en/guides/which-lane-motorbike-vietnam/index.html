Which Lane Should a Motorbike Use in Vietnam?

Which lane a motorbike belongs in on Vietnam's multi-lane roads, how to change lanes legally, what the lane signs and white and yellow lines mean, and the fines for getting it wrong.

On Hanoi's wide roads, motorbikes seem to be everywhere at once: on the far right, in the middle, between the cars. Yet the law says clearly which lane a motorbike or moped should use and how a lane change must be made. Riders who know the rules are more predictable and avoid fines. This guide explains the lane rules in Article 13 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the signs and markings that matter and the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For how city traffic works as a whole, see our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders.

The basic rule: keep right

Under Article 10(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, all road users keep to the right in their direction of travel, use the lane and part of the road intended for them and follow the signs. Article 13(1) adds that slower vehicles keep to the right. On a big road a motorbike is usually slower than the cars, so the right-hand part of the carriageway is its natural place. Riding slowly without keeping right, and holding up traffic as a result, costs 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Where motorbikes belong on multi-lane roads

Where the lanes in one direction are marked, Article 13(3) puts non-motorised vehicles such as bicycles in the outermost right-hand lane and motor vehicles in the lanes to its left. Motorbikes and mopeds count as motor vehicles. So if a separate lane for bicycles and other non-motorised vehicles is marked off on the right, your motorbike belongs to the left of it.

That separation is often marked with line 3.1. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, motor vehicles may cross this line when they need to, but must give way to the non-motorised vehicles when they do.

Reserved lanes and lane signs

Article 13(4) forbids riding in a lane reserved for another type of vehicle. Signs above the road show which lane is for whom (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT):

  • R.412d "Làn đường dành cho xe máy": a lane for motorbikes and mopeds only.
  • R.412g "Làn đường dành cho xe máy và xe đạp": a lane for motorbikes, mopeds and bicycles.
  • R.412b and R.412f: lanes for passenger cars and for all cars respectively; motorbikes do not belong there.
  • R.412e "Làn đường dành cho xe buýt": a bus lane. Where it is separated by a dashed line, other vehicles may use it but must make way for buses; where the line is solid, it is off limits to them.
  • R.411 "Hướng đi trên mỗi làn đường phải theo": arrows showing which direction each lane leads. Together with the arrows painted on the road, it obliges you to pick the lane that matches where you are going.

The regulation itself makes two exceptions: before a junction you may change lanes to follow your route, as long as you follow the lane-change rules; and lane signs do not stop you turning right or left into a lane, a driveway or a property.

Changing lanes: four duties

On roads with several marked lanes in the same direction, Article 13(2) requires you to:

1. ride within a lane, not on the line; 2. change lanes only where it is allowed; 3. move only to the next lane each time; 4. signal first, look and keep a safe distance ahead, behind and to both sides before you move over.

On such roads, passing a slower vehicle is not overtaking in the legal sense but lane use under Article 13, as Article 14(1) makes clear. If you are faster, move legally into the next lane on the left instead of slipping through gaps.

What the lines mean

The markings tell you where lane changes are allowed. According to QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT:

  • Line 2.1, white and dashed, separates lanes in the same direction; you may change lanes across it.
  • Line 2.2, white and solid, separates lanes between which changing is not allowed; you may neither cross it nor ride on it.
  • Line 2.3, a wide white line, marks a reserved lane when solid and a priority lane when dashed. Other vehicles may not enter a reserved lane except in emergencies; they may use a priority lane but must make way for the vehicles it serves.
  • Yellow centre lines separate the two directions of travel. A dashed yellow line may be crossed, a double solid one may not.

Where a sign and a marking conflict, the sign wins under the order of precedence in Article 11(2), and a temporary sign takes priority over a permanent one under Article 11(12).

What getting it wrong costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets these fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds:

  • Changing lanes where it is not allowed, without signalling or across more than one lane at once: 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Not keeping right, using the wrong part of the road or the wrong lane, or crossing a fixed central reservation: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Riding three or more abreast: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(k) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Weaving and constant lane-hopping ("lạng lách, đánh võng"), which Article 9(5) expressly bans: 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND under Article 7(9)(a) and a licence suspension of 10 to 12 months under Article 7(12)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • A wrong lane or lane change that causes an accident: 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a), plus 10 licence points under Article 7(13)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

The pavement is not a spare lane

When traffic backs up at a light, some riders escape onto the pavement. That is not allowed: under Article 33(3)(b), motorbike riders may not ride into the part of the road reserved for pedestrians and other vehicles. Riding on the pavement costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND and 2 points under Article 7(7)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP; crossing the pavement to enter a building is the only exception.

Practical tips for Hanoi's wide roads

  • Get in lane early. Decide well before a junction where you are going and move over one lane at a time. Cutting across several lanes at the last moment endangers you and others. Our guide to right of way at junctions explains who goes first.
  • Look up. Lane signs often hang above the road. At big junctions, watch for arrows and reserved lanes.
  • Mirror and shoulder check. Motorbikes in Hanoi ride close together and pass on both sides; look over your shoulder before every lane change, even if the mirror looks clear.
  • Stay right when you are slow. On multi-lane arterial roads, lorries and buses move fast; keep right and let faster traffic by.
  • Watch for prohibition signs. Where the P.104 "No motorcycles" sign stands, motorbikes may not continue (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT), which matters especially at slip roads and bridges.

After dark, lights come into play; our guide to riding at night covers them.

In short

Motorbikes keep right, but to the left of a marked bicycle lane (Article 13). Change lanes only across dashed lines, with your indicator on and one lane at a time. Car and bus lanes are off limits; lanes signed R.412d or R.412g are for motorbikes. Fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP range from 200,000 to 800,000 VND, and much more for weaving or accidents.

Using the Horn on a Motorbike in Vietnam

When Vietnamese law lets you use a motorbike horn, the night ban in residential and hospital areas, the fines and horn etiquette for riders.

The sound of Hanoi is the sound of horns. Visitors often conclude that the horn is simply part of riding in Vietnam and can be used at will. The law is much narrower than the street noise suggests. It allows the horn for two purposes only, bans continuous honking and sets a night-time ban in residential areas and around hospitals. This guide explains what the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety says, which fines Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP attaches to horn offences, which road signs concern the horn and how to use it sensibly on a rental bike. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The two situations in which the horn is allowed

Article 21(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety says the horn may be used only in two cases: to warn other road users when a situation arises that could make traffic unsafe, and to signal that you are about to overtake. That is the whole list. A short warning when a pedestrian steps out from between parked bikes, when a car starts reversing out of an alley you are passing or when you approach a blind corner fits the first purpose. Announcing that you are coming past a slower vehicle on an open road fits the second.

Honking out of impatience at a red light, to greet a friend or to make the traffic ahead move faster is not on the list in Article 21.

What the law does not allow

Article 21(2) adds three limits:

  • No continuous honking. The horn is a short signal, not a long blast.
  • No horn with an improper volume. The horn must have the volume set by the regulations.
  • No horn between 22:00 and 05:00 in densely populated areas and in areas around facilities for medical examination and treatment. Priority vehicles such as ambulances on duty are exempt (Article 21(2)).

The night rule also changes how you signal an overtake. Under Article 14(5), a vehicle that wants to overtake signals by flashing its headlight or sounding the horn, but in urban and densely populated areas between 22:00 and 05:00 it may signal only with the light. If you ride after dark, our guide to riding a motorbike at night explains the rules for lights.

When someone honks behind you

A horn from behind may be a signal that the vehicle wants to overtake, one of the two purposes the law allows. Article 14(4) tells the rider in front what to do: watch the road ahead and, if it is safe, slow down, signal right and keep to the right of the carriageway until the vehicle behind has passed, without obstructing it. If there is an obstacle or it is not safe to be overtaken, the rider in front signals left to show that overtaking is not yet possible. For a new rider the practical lesson is simple: a horn from behind is information, not an order to swerve. Hold your line, ease off and let the other vehicle pass.

The fines under Decree 168/2024

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the following fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds:

  • Horn between 22:00 and 05:00 in densely populated areas or around medical facilities, except priority vehicles on duty: 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.
  • Continuous honking or revving the engine in densely populated areas or around medical facilities: 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND under Article 7(9)(k), and the driving licence is suspended for 10 to 12 months under Article 7(12)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Fitting or using the signal device of a priority vehicle on a bike that has no priority right: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(đ), and the device is confiscated under Article 7(12)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Two further fines concern the condition of the bike itself. Riding a motorbike without a horn, or with a horn that does not work, costs 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 14(1)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Using a horn that does not meet the technical standard for that type of vehicle costs 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND under Article 14(2)(đ), and the rider must replace the device with one that meets the standard or restore its technical function under Article 14(6)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). The law itself also prohibits installing or using sound and light devices on motor vehicles in a way that disrupts traffic order and safety (Article 9(16)).

Road signs about the horn

The national technical regulation on road signs, QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, includes three signs that concern the horn:

  • P.128 "No horn". This sign prohibits all vehicles from using the horn. How far the ban applies is shown on an S.501 plate below the sign, or it runs until a DP.135 "End of all prohibitions" sign (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT).
  • R.309 "Sound horn". Vehicles reaching this sign must sound the horn. It is placed before sharp bends or before mountain pass sections with a gradient of more than 10 percent where visibility is limited (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT).
  • I.425 "Hospital". It indicates a medical facility near the road; road users should slow down, pay attention and not use the horn (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT).

The R.309 sign matters on mountain roads: where it stands, typically before a blind bend or a steep pass, a horn signal is not just allowed but required. More common signs are explained in our guide to Vietnam road signs for motorbike riders.

Sirens: when a priority vehicle comes through

Fire engines, ambulances on emergency duty, police and military vehicles on urgent duty and some other vehicles have priority under Article 27(1). When such a vehicle gives its priority signal, everyone else must slow down, move close to the right edge of the road or stop to give way, and must not obstruct it (Article 27(5)). In dense city traffic that can mean stopping where you are and waiting until the vehicle has passed.

Hearing matters as much as honking

The horn only works if people can hear it, and that includes you. Article 33(3)(c) prohibits motorbike riders from using umbrellas and audio devices, with an exception for hearing aids. Earphones that cut you off from horns and sirens are therefore a bad idea on a rental bike. Our guide to using a phone while riding covers navigation within the rules.

Horn etiquette on a rental bike

Beyond the legal limits, a few habits help:

1. Tap, do not lean. A short tap gets attention without startling anyone. 2. Brake first, honk second. The horn does not stop anybody. If a situation becomes dangerous, slowing down is what keeps you safe. 3. Honk where people cannot see you. Blind corners, narrow alleys and the exits of driveways are where a short warning makes sense. 4. Stay quiet near hospitals and at night. Use your light instead of the horn where the law requires it. 5. Do not answer a horn with a horn. If someone honks behind you, they may want to pass. Keep right and let them through when it is safe.

Check the horn before you ride

Test the horn when you pick up the bike, together with the lights, brakes and mirrors; our rental pickup checklist lists what to look at. A horn that does not work is a reason to ask for a different bike. If it fails during your trip, contact the rental shop; our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains the next steps.

Frequently asked questions

Is honking at night always banned? The ban in Article 21(2) covers 22:00 to 05:00 in densely populated areas and around medical facilities. Outside those areas the horn is still limited to the two purposes in Article 21(1).

Can I honk to tell someone to move? Article 21(1) allows the horn only to warn of a situation that could become unsafe or to signal an overtake. Impatience is neither.

In short

The horn is a warning signal, not background noise. Use it only to warn of a possible hazard or to signal an overtake, never continuously, and not at all between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas and around hospitals, as Article 21 requires. In those areas, night honking costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND, and continuous honking or revving costs 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND plus a licence suspension of 10 to 12 months under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Check the horn at pickup, give way to sirens and keep your ears free.

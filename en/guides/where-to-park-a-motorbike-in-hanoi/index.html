Where to Park a Motorbike in Hanoi

Where you may and may not park a motorbike in Hanoi, how guarded parking works, what street parking inside Ring Road 1 costs under Decision 5794/QĐ-UBND and the fines for wrong parking.

Finding a place to leave the bike is part of every ride in Hanoi. Pavements are packed with parked scooters, cafés wave you onto their forecourt and attendants with ticket books stand at every busy corner. Not every spot is legal, though, and the rules differ from what many visitors expect. This guide explains where the law allows you to stop and park, the signs to watch for, how guarded parking works, what street parking costs inside Ring Road 1 under the city's new price decision, and the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For the wider picture on safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Stopping and parking: the legal difference

Vietnamese law distinguishes two things. Under Article 18(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, stopping is a temporary halt just long enough for someone to get on or off, to load or unload or to check the vehicle. Parking, under Article 18(2), is a halt without a time limit. Most of the rules below apply to both, so a "quick stop" in the wrong place is no defence.

When you stop or park on a road, Article 18(3) requires you to signal as you pull in and out and not to obstruct pedestrians or other traffic. On city streets, Article 18(6) adds that you may only stop or park close to the kerb on the right-hand side in your direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from the kerb.

Where you must never stop or park

Article 18(4) lists the places where stopping and parking are prohibited. The ones that matter most for motorbike riders in Hanoi are:

  • on pedestrian crossings,
  • at intersections and within 5 metres of the edge of the crossing road,
  • at bus stops and passenger pick-up points,
  • on bridges, unless traffic management allows it, and under overpasses, except where parking is permitted,
  • on the left-hand side of a one-way street,
  • in front of the gate of an office or organisation with a driveway, and within 5 metres either side of it,
  • where the carriageway is only wide enough for one lane of motor traffic,
  • where the bike would hide a road sign or traffic light,
  • on bus-only lanes and over drain covers,
  • on the carriageway or pavement where the law does not allow it.

The last point is the one most visitors run into. A pavement is not automatically a car park: parking there is only legal where it has been designated or licensed for parking.

Signs to watch for

Three signs from the national standard QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT tell you most of what you need:

  • P.130 "Cấm dừng xe và đỗ xe" (no stopping and no parking),
  • P.131a, b and c "Cấm đỗ xe" (no parking, with variants for odd or even days),
  • I.408 "Nơi đỗ xe" (parking place) and I.408a (parking partly on the pavement).

According to the same standard, P.130 and P.131 apply up to the next intersection or to an end-of-restriction sign, and supplementary plates underneath can limit them to certain times or vehicles. Read the small plate before deciding that a sign does not apply to you.

How guarded parking works

Most Hanoians do not leave their bikes on the street but use a guarded parking point, known as "gửi xe" or "trông giữ xe". An attendant takes the bike, hands you a numbered ticket and often notes the number plate. When you return, you show the ticket and pay if you have not paid already. Guarded parking exists on licensed pavement and roadway spots, in courtyards, in the basements of shopping centres and office buildings, and at markets, hospitals and sights.

Keep the ticket somewhere safe and photograph it together with the bike, because a lost ticket can lead to a long discussion when you want the bike back. Ask about closing times, especially in the evening: a basement car park that closes at 22:00 is no use after a late dinner.

What street parking costs inside Ring Road 1

On 15 October 2026 Hanoi's People's Committee issued Decision 5794/QĐ-UBND on prices for guarded bicycle, motorbike and car parking that temporarily uses roadways and pavements and was set up with public funds, as Hànộimới reported on 15 October 2026. For bicycles and motorbikes on the restricted streets of the core urban area and on the other streets inside Ring Road 1, the decision sets 10,000 VND per daytime visit, 15,000 VND per night-time visit, 25,000 VND for day and night, and a monthly rate of 250,000 VND on the restricted streets and 200,000 VND on the other streets (Hànộimới, 15 October 2026).

The same report names the restricted streets of the core area, among them Hàng Đào, Hàng Ngang, Hàng Đường, Hàng Giấy, Lý Thái Tổ, Đinh Lễ, Trần Hưng Đạo, Lý Thường Kiệt, Hai Bà Trưng, Phủ Doãn, Quán Sứ and Nguyễn Xí. These prices apply only to street parking points of this kind. Private car parks, shopping centres and cafés set their own prices, so check the price board at the entrance or ask before you hand over the bike. If you are renting for several weeks, compare the monthly rates of the parking point near your accommodation; our guide to costs beyond the rental fee shows how parking fits into the overall budget.

Fines for parking in the wrong place

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the following fines for motorbike riders in Article 7:

  • Stopping or parking on the carriageway in a way that obstructs traffic, or parking on the carriageway or pavement where it is not allowed: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Stopping or parking at a pick-up point, an intersection or a pedestrian crossing, or ignoring a no-stopping or no-parking sign: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Stopping or parking on a bridge: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Riding along the pavement to reach a parking spot is a separate offence: under Article 7(7)(a) of the decree, riding on the pavement costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND; only crossing it to enter a building or property is allowed. Get off and push the bike the last few metres instead.

Practical tips for renters

  • Ask your accommodation first. Many guesthouses and hotels have a courtyard or a contract with a nearby parking point, which is often the simplest option for the night.
  • Use guarded parking at night. Leaving a rental bike unattended on the street overnight invites theft, and the contract you signed decides who pays if it disappears, so read it before you choose where to leave the bike.
  • Lock and empty the bike. Engage the steering lock, take the key and do not leave helmets, phones or bags under the seat or on the hooks.
  • Mind the low-emission zone. If you ride into the Ring Road 1 area, read our guide to Hanoi's Ring Road 1 low-emission zone before you choose a petrol bike.
  • Keep your papers on you. Do not leave the registration card in the bike; our guide on which papers to carry explains why.

In short

Park only close to the kerb on the right, never on crossings, at intersections, bus stops or bridges (Article 18), and on the pavement only where parking is designated. Guarded parking with a ticket is the norm, and inside Ring Road 1 street parking now costs 10,000 VND per daytime visit and 15,000 VND per night under Decision 5794/QĐ-UBND (Hànộimới). Parking in the wrong place costs 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Before you set off, our pickup checklist helps you check the bike's lock and keys.

Letting someone else ride your motorbike in Vietnam: who is fined

Can a friend or partner ride your motorbike in Vietnam? Who may ride, the owner's fine for handing a bike to an unqualified rider, the rider's own licence fines under Decree 168 and impoundment.

Couples and friends who travel together often share one motorbike. One rides into town, the other takes over on the way back, or a friend borrows the bike for an afternoon. It feels like a private arrangement, but Vietnamese law treats the moment you hand over the keys as a legal act. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) forbids handing a motor vehicle to someone who does not meet the conditions to ride it, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines both sides: the owner who hands the bike over and the person who rides it. This guide explains who may ride, what each side risks, what happens to the bike and what to check before you let someone else take the handlebars. For the licence basics, see our hub on rental documents and contracts.

The rule: no motor vehicle for an unqualified rider

Article 9(7) of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists among the prohibited acts handing a motor vehicle to a person who does not meet the legal conditions to ride it. Those conditions are in Article 56(1): the rider must be old enough and healthy enough, and hold a valid licence for the class of vehicle, with points left on it. While riding, the rider must also carry the vehicle's registration certificate, the licence and the compulsory civil liability insurance certificate.

Mopeds are the one exception to the licence requirement. A moped rider needs no licence but must know the traffic rules, be able to ride and meet the age and health conditions (Article 56(4)). Everything below concerns motorbikes that need a licence, which includes almost every scooter rented to visitors.

Which licence the other rider needs

The class depends on the engine. Under Article 57(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15, class A1 covers two-wheeled motorbikes up to 125 cm³ or 11 kW, and class A covers anything larger. Vietnamese A1 licences issued before the law took effect and not yet exchanged still cover motorbikes from 50 to under 175 cm³ (Article 89(2)(a)).

A visitor normally rides with an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention together with their national licence; our guides to foreign licences and International Driving Permits explain which permits are accepted. The age limits are in Article 59(1): 16 for mopeds and 18 for an A1 or A licence. Our guide to the minimum riding age covers them in detail.

What the owner risks

Article 32(10) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines the owner of a motorbike who hands the bike to, or lets it be ridden by, someone who does not meet the conditions of Article 56(1). The fine is VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 for an individual owner and VND 16,000,000 to 20,000,000 for an organisation. The clause expressly includes a rider who holds a licence but is in a period of suspension.

The clause addresses the owner of the vehicle. On a borrowed or hired motorbike, that is usually not you but the person or business named on the registration certificate, and the decree does not spell out how the clause applies to someone who passes on a bike they have only borrowed. What it does make clear is that handing a bike to an unqualified rider is an offence in its own right, not just a problem for the rider.

What the rider risks

The person you hand the bike to is fined under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, depending on the engine:

  • up to 125 cm³ or 11 kW: VND 2,000,000 to 4,000,000 for riding without a licence, with a licence for the wrong class, an invalid or expired licence or one with no points left, or with an International Driving Permit but without the matching national licence (Article 18(5));
  • above 125 cm³ or 11 kW: VND 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 for the same failures (Article 18(7)).

Age matters too. A rider aged 14 to under 16 on a motorbike or moped receives a warning (Article 18(1)), and since Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP a rider aged 16 to under 18 on any motorbike is fined VND 400,000 to 600,000 (Article 18(4)(a) as amended).

What happens to the motorbike

Under Article 48(1)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, the police may impound the motorbike before the penalty decision when the rider commits one of these licence or age violations, among them Article 18(1), 18(4)(a), 18(5) and 18(7). The bike then stays with the police until the case is settled, whoever owns it. Our guide on when police can impound a motorbike explains the procedure and the papers you will need.

The rules at a glance

| Situation | Who is fined | Penalty | |---|---|---| | Owner hands the bike to an unqualified rider | Owner | VND 8–10 million for an individual, VND 16–20 million for an organisation (Decree 168, Article 32(10)) | | Rider without a valid licence, up to 125 cm³ or 11 kW | Rider | VND 2–4 million and possible impoundment (Decree 168, Articles 18(5) and 48(1)(i)) | | Rider without a valid licence, above 125 cm³ or 11 kW | Rider | VND 6–8 million and possible impoundment (Decree 168, Articles 18(7) and 48(1)(i)) | | International Driving Permit without the national licence | Rider | Same bands as above (Decree 168, Article 18(5)(b) and 18(7)(c)) | | Rider aged 16 to under 18 on a motorbike | Rider | VND 400,000–600,000 (Decree 168, Article 18(4)(a), amended by Decree 238) |

Before you hand over the keys

The law sets the minimum. These checks keep a shared bike out of trouble:

  • Look at the other rider's licence, not their confidence. Check that the class covers the engine size and, for visitors, that both the permit and the national licence are with them.
  • Read your agreement. If the bike is hired, the agreement may name the riders allowed and say who covers damage and traffic violations. Ask the owner before anyone else rides; our guide to rental agreements explains what to look for.
  • Pass on the papers with the bike. The rider must carry the registration certificate and the insurance certificate (Article 56(1)). Our guide on which papers to carry lists them.
  • Think about insurance. A rider without a valid licence may also be outside a travel insurance policy; see our guide on whether travel insurance covers riding a motorbike.
  • Swap riders only when parked. Changing riders while the bike is moving is banned outright (Article 33(3)(e) of Law 36/2024/QH15).

Frequently asked questions

May my partner ride the scooter if they have a licence? Yes, if the licence is valid for the class and, for a foreign licence, they carry the matching International Driving Permit (Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15). Check what your agreement with the owner says.

Is a friend who has never ridden but holds a car licence allowed? No. A car licence is not a motorbike licence; the classes are separate under Article 57(1).

Can the bike be taken even though I was not riding? Yes. Impoundment under Article 48(1)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP depends on the rider's violation, not on who owns or hired the bike.

Does the owner lose licence points? Article 32(10) of the decree sets a fine for the owner; it does not deduct points.

Summary

Handing a motorbike to someone who does not meet the conditions to ride it is banned (Article 9(7) of Law 36/2024/QH15). The owner faces VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 as an individual (Article 32(10) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP), the rider VND 2,000,000 to 8,000,000 depending on the engine (Article 18(5) and (7)), and the bike can be impounded (Article 48(1)(i)).

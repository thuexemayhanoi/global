One-Way Streets and Pavements in Vietnam

Why riding against a one-way street or along the pavement is one of the costlier mistakes on a motorbike in Vietnam, how to recognise one-way streets and what the law allows.

In a jam before a red light, some riders hop onto the pavement, and in narrow streets the odd rider goes a short way against a one-way street because it is quicker. To visitors it can look like local custom. Legally it is one of the more expensive mistakes you can make on a rental motorbike. This guide explains how to recognise one-way streets, what the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety says about pavements and what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP charges for breaking the rules. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

How to recognise a one-way street

The road sign regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT has two signs you need to know:

  • P.102 "Cấm đi ngược chiều" (no entry): it bans all vehicles, motorised and non-motorised, from entering the street in the direction the sign faces, except priority vehicles. Pedestrians may still walk on the pavement or verge.
  • I.407 "Đường một chiều" (one-way street): this information sign shows the only permitted direction with an arrow. It allows travel only in that direction and bans turning round to go back, except for priority vehicles. Where the one-way section ends, sign W.204 "Đường hai chiều" announces two-way traffic.

So from one end you see the round no-entry sign P.102, and from the other end the information sign I.407.

Where the signs stand helps too. Sign I.407a is placed after the junction, inside the one-way street itself, while versions I.407b and I.407c stand before the junction, on the road leading into the one-way street (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, Appendix E.7). If you want to turn, you can tell in advance whether the side street only runs one way.

On roads with a central divider, each carriageway can have its own direction. There, signs R.302a or R.302b at the ends of the divider, combined with P.102, show which way each carriageway runs (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, Appendix D.2). Riding a short way along the wrong side of the divider to the next U-turn gap is therefore not a shortcut; it is riding against the flow.

What else is banned in a one-way street

Riding against the direction is not the only ban. U-turns in one-way streets are banned by Article 15(4) of the law, reversing by Article 16(2), and stopping or parking on the left-hand side of a one-way street by Article 18(4)(a). If you miss your turning, ride on to the end of the street and find another way round.

The pavement belongs to pedestrians

Article 77(1) of the law is clear: the roadway is for traffic, the pavement for pedestrians. For motorbike riders, Article 33(3)(b) adds that they may not ride into areas reserved for pedestrians and other vehicles. The pavement is not an escape lane, not even for a few metres before a red light.

In return, pedestrians must use the pavement, verge or a footpath under Article 30(1)(a); where there is none, they walk close to the right-hand edge in their direction of travel. In narrow lanes without a pavement you therefore share the roadway with people on foot, so ride slowly and expect someone to step out of a doorway.

The decree makes one exception: crossing the pavement only to ride into a house or the grounds of an office or business is not an offence (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 7(7)(a)). That covers the entrance to your accommodation, for example. Ride at walking pace and give way to pedestrians as you cross.

What it costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the same amount for both offences: riding against a one-way street, riding against a no-entry sign or riding along the pavement costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(a), and Article 7(13)(a) adds a 2-point deduction (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Only priority vehicles on emergency duty are exempt.

Other amounts that may apply:

  • U-turn in a prohibited place, such as a one-way street: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(k) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Parking on the roadway or pavement against the rules: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Entering a road against its signs and causing an accident: 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Why the rule makes sense

Nobody in a one-way street expects oncoming traffic. Pedestrians look only one way when crossing, drivers pull over briskly to the right, and at junctions nobody watches for a vehicle from the wrong direction. A motorbike coming the wrong way turns up exactly where no one is looking. The pavement is similar: children, older people and travellers looking at their phones for directions walk there.

Practical tips

  • Check the signs, not just the app. Navigation apps do not know every one-way rule. Look at the signs at the entrance, even if the app suggests otherwise.
  • Walk, don't ride, back. If you have strayed into a one-way street and need to go a short way back, get off and push the bike along the edge rather than riding against the flow.
  • Go round the block. If you cannot use a one-way street, ride round. It costs a minute and saves trouble.
  • Park with care. Our guide on where to park a motorbike in Hanoi explains where you may leave the bike.

Frequently asked questions

May I ride a few metres the wrong way if nothing is coming? No. The ban in Article 7(7)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP has no minimum distance. Push the bike instead.

Does the pavement ban apply to electric scooters? Yes. Article 33(3)(b) covers motorbikes and mopeds whatever powers them.

Does a one-way rule apply at night when the street is empty? Yes. A prohibition sign without a supplementary plate applies at all times. If a ban only applies at certain times, supplementary plate S.508 under the sign states them, for example hours of the day, odd or even dates or days of the week, with English added where needed (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT).

In short

Riding against a one-way street or along the pavement is banned and costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND plus 2 points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Watch for signs P.102 and I.407, do not turn round in one-way streets and only cross the pavement to reach an entrance.

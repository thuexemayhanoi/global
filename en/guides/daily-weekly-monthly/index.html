Daily, Weekly or Monthly Motorbike Rental?

How Hanoi rental shops count days, how tiered day-count pricing works, what weekly and monthly rentals include, and which deposit, maintenance and return questions to settle before paying.

Anyone staying in Hanoi longer than a weekend faces a small arithmetic problem: keep extending a daily rental, book a week, or commit to a month? The answer depends less on the length of your trip than on how Hanoi shops count time, how their tiered prices are built, and what the contract says once a rental runs past a few days. This guide explains that logic using published price lists and policies, without promising any prices of its own, because rates change quickly and every shop calculates differently. The handover inspection is in our Hanoi motorbike rental pickup checklist.

How Hanoi shops count days and lateness

The first difference from home: a rental day in Hanoi is almost always a 24-hour period from the handover, not a calendar day. The Hanoi shop MOTOGO states on its Hanoi rental page that one rental day is 24 hours counted from the moment of rental. Pick the bike up at 3 pm on Monday on a three-day rental and it is due at 3 pm on Thursday. Lateness is charged, under MOTOGO's published policy, by the hour and by vehicle class, only once you are more than 30 minutes late; more than eight hours late is rounded up to a full extra day. Other shops use other thresholds, but the structure is similar: the handover time rules, and any extension should be agreed by message before the deadline.

The second difference: "weekly" and "monthly" rentals in Hanoi are usually not separate products but tiers of a scale based on the number of rental days. MOTOGO's price list from September 2026 uses tiers of 1 to 5, 6 to 10, 11 to 15, 16 to 20, 21 to 25 and more than 25 days; a Honda Vision 110 is listed at 180,000 VND per day in the first tier and 100,000 VND per day in the last. A "week" is simply tier two, a "month" the final tier. So do not ask for the weekly price; ask for the daily price at your exact number of days.

What weekly and monthly rentals include

For short rentals the package in Hanoi is fairly standard. MOTOGO lists two helmets, two disposable rain ponchos, a phone holder and a rear rack with straps for its Hanoi bikes, and delivery is free or carries a small surcharge depending on the district, set out on the same Hanoi page. Monthly rentals raise questions nobody asks for three days: who pays for the oil change, new brake pads or a worn tyre? Is a replacement bike provided when the vehicle goes to the workshop? May the bike leave Hanoi or go into the mountains? Is a mid-rental check with photos scheduled after two weeks? Serious shops answer in writing; a shop that dodges these questions is the wrong partner for a month.

Compulsory insurance always belongs to the vehicle, not the rider, and it must cover the whole rental period. According to Gia đình và Xã hội, the limits under Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP are 150 million VND per person per accident for injury and 50 million VND per accident for property damage. Check the certificate's expiry date, especially on a monthly rental: if the insurance lapses in week three, you ride uninsured from that day. Under the Thư Viện Nhà Đất summary of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, riding without the registration or the compulsory insurance certificate on you costs 200,000 to 300,000 VND per offence.

Published price structures as a guide

Two published Hanoi price lists show how large the gap between tiers is. The list from XeThienTri quotes 100,000 to 150,000 VND per day for basic semi-automatics and 150,000 to 250,000 VND per day for basic automatic scooters, and explains that a weekly rental saves 10 to 15 percent and a monthly rental 20 to 30 percent compared with the sum of daily prices. MOTOGO's price list describes the same order of magnitude: weekly rentals roughly 10 to 15 percent and monthly rentals 20 to 30 percent cheaper than the daily rate. Both are statements by those shops at the time of publication, not market prices; they are quoted here only to show the logic. Work out your trip with the current daily price of the matching tier and compare two shops in writing.

Two traps appear in this calculation again and again. First, early return: pay for 30 days at the monthly rate and leave after 18, and you will rarely get the difference back; if you do, it is recalculated at the higher daily rate of the tier you actually used. Second, extension: book five days and then extend day by day, and you often stay in the most expensive tier instead of sliding into the next one. Have both rules explained before you pay and written into the contract.

Deposit, contract and papers on longer rentals

The longer the rental, the bigger the shop's risk, and the bigger the deposit. XeThienTri's price page describes the usual guarantees as a cash deposit of 1,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND depending on the bike and the shop, or an identity document held by the shop, often the passport in the case of foreigners. For a monthly rental, never hand over your passport: you need it for hotels, visa extensions and every police check. Offer a larger cash deposit with a receipt or a less critical document instead, and have the contract record what the shop holds and when it comes back.

Beyond the usual points, a weekly or monthly contract should state the price tier and total, the rule for early return and for extension, who pays maintenance and wear parts, whether and when a mid-rental check takes place, how camera fines booked to the plate during your rental are handled, and what applies in case of theft. MOTOGO's policy shows the hard edge of such clauses: a stolen or destroyed bike is compensated at its valued price at the start of the rental, and days off the road caused by the renter are still charged. Photograph the registration, insurance, odometer and condition at handover and at every mid-rental check.

Which duration fits which trip

For two to four days in Hanoi there is nothing to calculate: take the daily rate, return on time, done. For five to ten days, say Hanoi plus trips to Ninh Bình or the coast, the second tier pays off provided you actually use the bike every day; on train days or a Halong tour it sits unused outside the hotel. From two weeks the scale becomes interesting, but only if the contract and the insurance cover the whole period. Anyone living, working or studying in Hanoi for a month or more does best with a genuine monthly rental, but should settle maintenance, replacement and mid-rental checks in writing and treat the bike like their own: check tyre pressure regularly, have the oil checked and park in guarded parking at night.

Five questions before paying for a week or a month

  • From what time does the 24-hour day run, and after how many minutes late is an hour or a day charged?
  • Which price tier does my exact number of days fall into, and what happens on early return or extension?
  • Who pays for oil changes, wear parts and workshop costs, and is a replacement bike provided?
  • Does the compulsory insurance cover the entire period, and how are fines during the rental settled?
  • What deposit is required, in what form, and does the contract say when it is returned?

With those five answers in writing, you pay in Hanoi for the duration you actually need, not the duration the shop would like to sell.

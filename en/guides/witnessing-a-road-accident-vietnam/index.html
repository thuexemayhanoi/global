Witnessing a Road Accident in Vietnam

What you must do in Vietnam when you witness a road accident: help, call for help, protect the scene, take the injured to emergency care and give a statement, under Article 80 of the traffic law.

Ride a scooter around Vietnam for long enough and sooner or later you will pass an accident: a scooter down at a junction, a collision on a country road, people sitting dazed at the roadside. Many visitors are unsure what to do. Do I have to stop? Can I help if I do not speak the language? What if the police want a statement? The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety answers these questions fairly clearly, and it asks more of witnesses and passers-by than many people expect. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law asks of people at the scene

Article 80(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists the duties of everyone present where a road accident happens:

  • Help: assist the injured and get them timely care (point a),
  • Report: tell the police, a medical facility or the nearest People's Committee straight away (point b),
  • Protect the scene: take part in protecting the accident scene (point c),
  • Protect property: help protect the belongings of the people involved (point d),
  • Give information: provide information about the accident when the authorities ask for it (point đ).

On top of that comes a general duty to report: under Article 81(1), agencies, organisations and individuals who discover a road accident must report it immediately to the police, a medical facility or the nearest People's Committee.

Passing drivers: take the injured to emergency care

One rule surprises many visitors. Under Article 80(4) of the law, drivers of other vehicles passing the scene of a road accident are responsible for taking injured people for emergency treatment. Only priority vehicles and vehicles carrying people with diplomatic privileges are exempt. On a scooter, carrying a seriously injured person is often not a sensible option; then the fastest course is to call the ambulance and help until it arrives.

Vehicles involved in the crash only as a last resort

The vehicles involved in the accident are subject to a restriction: under Article 80(3) they may be used to take a victim for emergency treatment only if no other vehicle is available, and the positions of the vehicle and the victim must be recorded first, without changing or removing any traces. If someone has died, the scene must be left as it is and the body covered.

The numbers to call

According to THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT, these nationwide numbers apply:

  • 115 for an ambulance,
  • 113 for the police,
  • 114 for fire and rescue, for example if a vehicle is on fire or someone is trapped.

Emergency call centres generally work in Vietnamese. If you do not speak it, ask bystanders to make the call and use that time for what you can do without words. Our guide to emergency numbers in Vietnam explains what to say on the phone.

How to help in practice

1. Stop safely yourself. Park your scooter off the carriageway and switch on the hazard lights if it has them. A second crash helps nobody. 2. Warn other traffic. Alert approaching vehicles, for example by standing well before the scene and signalling. 3. Get the call made. Call 115 and 113, or ask someone to. 4. Stay with the injured. Talk to them and keep them calm and warm. Move an injured person only if they are in immediate danger, for example from fire or passing traffic. 5. Look after belongings. Keep the injured people's bags, phones and valuables together until the police or relatives arrive; under Article 80(2)(d) that is part of the duty too. 6. Leave the traces. Do not move vehicles or objects unnecessarily, so that the police can work out what happened.

Giving a statement as a witness

When the police arrive, they may ask you what you saw. Investigating an accident expressly includes taking statements from the drivers involved, the victims, witnesses and others concerned, under Article 83(2)(d) of the law. So stay until the police arrive if you can, and leave your name and phone number. If you do not speak Vietnamese, your accommodation or rental shop can help translate.

Write down what you saw straight after the accident: the time, place, directions of travel, the traffic light phase and number plates. Memories fade fast. Photos of the scene can help the police, but do not post them on social media and do not photograph injured people.

If you were involved yourself

If you were involved, your duties are stricter: stop at once, keep the scene unchanged, help, report and stay until the police arrive (Article 80(1)). A rider who causes an accident and leaves the scene faces 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND and 6 points off the licence under Article 7(9)(c) and 7(13)(c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guide on what to do after a motorbike accident explains the steps.

What happens at the hospital

Hospitals giving first aid to people injured in a road accident must inform the police immediately and test the blood of the drivers involved for alcohol, drugs and other stimulants under Article 81(3). If you bring an injured person to hospital, expect the police to be involved there too, and stay briefly until it is clear who is looking after the patient.

Be prepared

If you ride a lot, a little preparation goes a long way: save 113, 114 and 115 in your phone, know how to show and share your location in your map app, and keep a small first-aid kit with plasters, dressings and disposable gloves under the seat. A first-aid course before the trip refreshes the basics.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to stop if plenty of people are already helping? If there are enough helpers and the ambulance has been called, extra onlookers tend to get in the way. Ask briefly whether help is needed, and otherwise pass slowly. Accident scenes are places where you must watch out and slow down anyway under Article 12(3)(e).

May I leave if the police take a long time? For witnesses, the law sets a duty to give information when asked (Article 80(2)(đ)). If you cannot stay, leave your name and phone number with the helpers or the people involved so that the police can reach you.

In short

Anyone at an accident scene must help, report the accident, protect the scene and the victims' belongings and give information when asked (Article 80(2)). Passing drivers must take the injured for emergency treatment (Article 80(4)). Call 115 and 113 (THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT) and stay as a witness until the police arrive.

Choosing a motorbike helmet in Vietnam: the law, the fit and your own helmet

What Vietnamese law requires of a motorbike helmet, the fines for riders and passengers without a fastened one, how a helmet should fit, which type suits the ride and whether to bring your own.

The first thing most visitors are handed with a scooter in Vietnam is a helmet, often a light open-face shell with a thin strap and a scratched visor. It is easy to treat it as a formality. It is not. A helmet is the one piece of gear the law requires of everyone on the bike, and it is what decides how a fall at 30 km/h ends. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets the requirement, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines riders and passengers who ignore it. This guide explains what the law asks for, how to check that a helmet fits, which type to choose for the city and for mountain roads, and whether it is worth bringing your own from home. For the rest of the safety picture, see our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law requires

The rule is short. Under Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15, the rider and every passenger on a two-wheeled or three-wheeled motorbike or a moped must wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation and fasten the chin strap properly. Three points follow from it:

  • Everyone on the bike. The duty applies to the passenger exactly as to the rider, whatever the length of the trip.
  • A motorbike helmet. The decree describes the required item as a "helmet for motorcycle and moped riders" (mũ bảo hiểm cho người đi mô tô, xe máy). A bicycle helmet, a construction hard hat or a fashion cap is not that product.
  • A fastened strap. A helmet with the strap open counts as a helmet not worn properly.

The regulation itself is a Vietnamese national technical standard. This guide does not reproduce its technical content; if you want to check a specific helmet, ask the shop to show you the label and any conformity marking on it.

The fines

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines both the rider and, separately, the adult passenger:

  • Rider without a helmet, or with the strap not fastened properly: VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Article 7(2)(h) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Rider carrying a passenger without a helmet, or with the strap open: VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Article 7(2)(i), except when the passenger is a sick person on the way to emergency care, a child under six or a person being escorted for a violation (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Passenger without a helmet, or with the strap open: fined in their own right under Article 12(5)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

So a couple on one scooter with two open straps can face two fines at the same stop (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Articles 7(2) and 12(5)). What happens at a roadside check is explained in our guide to being stopped by the police, and the special rules for children in our guide to riding with children.

How a helmet should fit

A helmet that meets the standard still protects badly if it does not fit. A quick check takes a minute:

  • Level on the head. The front edge should sit about two fingers above the eyebrows, not tilted back on the crown.
  • Snug, not painful. Push the helmet side to side and up and down with both hands. The skin of your forehead should move with it; if the helmet slides on its own, it is too big.
  • Strap under the chin, not on it. Fasten the strap so that no more than two fingers fit between strap and chin. Then try to roll the helmet forward off your head from the back: it should not come off.
  • No pressure points. Wear it for a few minutes. A spot that hurts after five minutes will hurt more after an hour.
  • Visor and vision. Check that the visor, if there is one, closes and stays up when open, and that the shell does not block your view over the shoulder.

If the helmet you are offered does not pass these checks, ask for another size. Shops in Vietnam sell a wide range, including children's sizes.

Which type for which ride

There are three common types on Vietnamese roads, and each has its place:

  • Half helmets. The light shells that cover only the top of the head are the most common in town. They meet the minimum but leave the face and jaw exposed.
  • Open-face (three-quarter) helmets. They cover the back and sides of the head and usually have a visor. A good compromise for city riding in heat.
  • Full-face helmets. They protect the chin and face as well, and keep wind, rain and insects out on longer rides. They are warmer, which matters in the summer heat.

For short rides in town, a well-fitting open-face helmet with a visor is a sensible minimum. For mountain loops, long highways and rain, a full-face helmet is worth the extra warmth. Our guide to what to wear on a motorbike covers the rest of the kit, and our guides to riding in heavy rain and riding at night explain why a clear visor and a light-coloured helmet help.

Should you bring your own helmet?

There are good reasons to bring one: you know it fits, you know its history and it may offer more protection than a basic shell. There are also practical costs: it takes up a lot of luggage space and needs to be carried around when you are not riding.

The legal side is less clear-cut than many travellers expect. Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 refers to the Vietnamese national technical regulation and does not mention foreign certifications. We have not found an official statement that a helmet certified only under a foreign standard counts as compliant in Vietnam, nor one that says it does not. If you bring your own, make sure it is a proper motorbike helmet with a working chin strap; if you want certainty, a locally sold motorbike helmet removes the question.

Looking after a helmet

  • Replace it after any crash or heavy drop, even if the outside looks undamaged; the protective lining inside may already be crushed.
  • Keep it out of strong sun when parked if you can, and do not leave it hanging on a mirror, where it can fall and crack.
  • Ask for a clean helmet. If you are given a shared helmet, ask for a fresh one or a disposable liner, and check the buckle before you set off.
  • Check the strap every time. A buckle that does not click shut is a reason to swap the helmet, not to tie a knot.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bicycle helmet enough on an electric bike? E-bikes have their own rules, explained in our guide to e-bike rules. On a motorbike or a moped, the law asks for a motorbike helmet (Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15).

Do I really need to fasten the strap for a short ride? Yes. An open strap is treated like not wearing a helmet, for the rider and the passenger (Article 7(2)(h) and (i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Do helmet offences cost licence points? The decree sets a fine for them; they are not among the acts that cost points under Article 7(13) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

What if my passenger refuses to wear one? Then you are both in breach: you for carrying them (Article 7(2)(i)) and they for not wearing one (Article 12(5)(b)).

Summary

Everyone on a motorbike in Vietnam must wear a motorbike helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened (Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15). Riders are fined VND 400,000 to 600,000 for their own helmet and the same again for a passenger's, and adult passengers can be fined themselves (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Articles 7 and 12). Choose a helmet that fits snugly and suits the ride, fasten it every time, and check it along with the rest of the bike using our motorbike checklist.

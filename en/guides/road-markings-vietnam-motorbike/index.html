Road Markings in Vietnam: What the Lines Mean

What the yellow and white lines on Vietnam's roads mean, which ones a motorbike may cross, where to wait at the stop line and what ignoring road markings costs under Decree 168.

Riders from Britain and much of Europe are used to white centre lines. In Vietnam, as in North America, a yellow line separates the two directions of traffic, and there are other differences worth knowing before you set off on a rental motorbike. Cross a solid yellow line to get past a slow lorry, or wait at a red light on the far side of the stop line, and you risk more than a fine: both put you where other road users do not expect you. This guide explains the main markings in the national standard QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, how they rank against lights and signs and what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP provides when riders ignore them. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What markings do and where they rank

Under Article 11(6) of Law 36/2024/QH15, road markings show how the road is divided into lanes, the position or direction of travel and where to stop. In the order of precedence for traffic signals in Article 11(2), they come fourth: after the instructions of traffic police, traffic lights and road signs. If a sign and a marking say different things, follow the sign.

Yellow lines: the middle of the road

Yellow lines running along the road separate the two directions of traffic. Annex G of QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT describes four forms:

  • Line 1.1, broken yellow: it separates opposing traffic, and vehicles on either side may cross it to use the other half of the road, for example to overtake where overtaking is otherwise allowed and safe.
  • Line 1.2, solid yellow: it may not be crossed or ridden on. It is typically used where the view is too short for safe overtaking and the risk of head-on collisions is high, for example on two- or three-lane roads without a central divider.
  • Line 1.3, double solid yellow: it means the same as 1.2 and is used mainly on wider roads without a central divider, but also at dangerous spots on narrower roads.
  • Line 1.4, one solid and one broken line side by side: traffic on the side of the broken line may cross it when needed; traffic on the side of the solid line may not.

In practice, treat a solid yellow line as a wall. A slow lorry on a mountain climb is no reason to cross it. Our guide to overtaking rules explains when and where you may pass.

White lines: lanes going the same way

White lines along the road separate lanes for traffic travelling in the same direction (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, Annex G1.2 and G1.3):

  • Line 2.1, broken white: you may change lanes across it.
  • Line 2.2, solid white: it marks places where changing lanes is not allowed, and it may not be crossed or ridden on.
  • Line 2.3: it marks special lanes. As a solid line it borders a lane reserved for one type of vehicle, which others may enter only in an emergency; as a broken line it borders a priority lane that others may use as long as they make way for the vehicles it is meant for.
  • Line 3.1: it marks the outer edge of the carriageway or separates the lane for motor vehicles from the lane for non-motorised vehicles. You may cross it when necessary, but you must give way to bicycles and other non-motorised vehicles.

Our guide on which lane a motorbike should use explains where a motorbike belongs on a multi-lane road.

Hatched areas

Diagonally hatched areas, for example in front of traffic islands or where roads split, are line 4.1. They mark parts of the road that are not for driving on and channel traffic into the intended paths. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, Annex G1.4, vehicles may not ride on or across them except in an emergency. However tempting it is when scooters are queuing, the hatched area is not a shortcut.

Across the road: stop line, give-way line and zebra crossing

  • Line 7.1, the stop line: this solid white line across the lane shows where to stop at a red light or at the stop sign R.122. It is also used before level crossings, in left-turn lanes inside a junction and before pedestrian crossings (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, Annex G2.1). Stop before the line, not on it and not beyond it.
  • Line 7.2, the give-way line: a double broken line with a triangle pointing at you tells you to slow down or stop to give way. It goes with sign W.208, which warns of a junction with a priority road.
  • Line 7.3, the pedestrian crossing: it marks where people on foot cross the road. Our guide to pedestrian crossings explains when you must stop for them.

Our guide to traffic light rules explains what each light requires at the stop line.

When lines and signs disagree

At roadworks and after a junction has been rebuilt, old markings often no longer match the new layout. The order of precedence settles it: signs rank above markings under Article 11(2), and where a permanent and a temporary sign at the same spot say different things, the temporary sign applies under Article 11(12). Follow the signs and the instructions of roadworks staff, even if the lines on the tarmac say otherwise.

What it costs

For motorbike and moped riders, Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP provides, among others:

  • Ignoring signs or road markings in general: 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Not keeping to the right, riding on the wrong part of the road or in the wrong lane, or crossing a fixed central divider: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Overtaking where it is banned: 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND under Article 7(4)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Common mistakes by visitors

  • Creeping over the stop line. At red lights many riders edge forward over the stop line and onto the crossing. Stay behind the line, even when others move up.
  • Crossing a solid yellow line on a climb. On mountain passes it is tempting to cross the line behind a lorry. Wait for a stretch with a broken line.
  • Using a reserved lane. Where a lane is set aside for buses or cars, a motorbike does not belong in it.
  • Trusting faded paint. Not every line has been repainted recently. If a marking is hard to make out, ride as if it were solid and pay extra attention to the signs.

Frequently asked question

Do I have to stop at the stop line when the light turns amber? Yes, unless you are already on or past the line. Under Article 11(4)(b), riders must stop before the stop line when the light is amber; those already on or beyond it may carry on. The stop line is therefore the point that decides whether you may still go.

Can I cross a solid yellow line to overtake in Vietnam? No. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, line 1.2, solid yellow, may not be crossed or ridden on, and the double solid yellow line 1.3 means the same. Only the broken yellow line 1.1 may be crossed, for example to overtake where overtaking is otherwise allowed and safe. On a mountain climb, wait behind a slow lorry for a stretch with a broken line.

What does a solid line next to a broken yellow line mean? It is line 1.4 in QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT: traffic on the side of the broken line may cross it when needed, while traffic on the side of the solid line may not. Check which line is nearest to you before you move out to overtake, and treat the solid side as a wall.

Can I ride across the hatched area on the road in Vietnam? No, except in an emergency. Diagonally hatched areas, line 4.1, mark parts of the road that are not for driving on, for example in front of traffic islands or where roads split, and Annex G1.4 of QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT bars vehicles from riding on or across them. However tempting it is when scooters are queuing, the hatched area is not a shortcut.

In short

Yellow separates the directions of traffic, white separates lanes going the same way. Solid lines may not be crossed or ridden on, broken ones may (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). Stop before the white stop line 7.1 and keep the pedestrian crossing clear. Markings rank after police instructions, lights and signs (Article 11(2)), and ignoring them costs between 200,000 and 1,000,000 VND depending on the offence (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

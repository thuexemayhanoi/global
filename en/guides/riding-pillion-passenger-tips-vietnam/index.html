Riding Pillion on a Scooter in Vietnam

What a pillion passenger on a scooter in Vietnam must do and must not do: helmet, umbrella, bulky items, holding on, leaning in bends, and why the rider pays the fine for the passenger.

Plenty of couples and friends share one rental scooter in Vietnam: one rides, the other sits behind. A lot is said about the rider and very little about the passenger, yet the person on the back also decides whether a trip goes smoothly. A passenger who fidgets, leans against a bend or twists round suddenly can throw the scooter off balance. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety also has its own rules for passengers, and for some breaches the rider pays. This guide explains what passengers must do, what they must not do and how to arrive as a team. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Who may ride along

On a motorbike or moped, Article 33(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 allows the rider to carry one passenger as a rule. Two passengers are allowed only in exceptional cases, for example when one of them is a child under 12. Our guide to how many people may ride one motorbike explains the details.

A must for both: a helmet with the strap done up

Article 33(2) applies to rider and passenger alike: both wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the chin strap properly fastened. If the passenger rides without a helmet or with the strap undone, it is the rider who faces 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP; there are exceptions only in a few cases, such as children under six or a sick person being taken for emergency treatment. Check each other's straps before you set off.

What passengers must not do

Article 33(4) of the law lists what a passenger must not do while the bike is moving:

  • carry bulky items (point a),
  • use an umbrella (point b),
  • hold on to, tow or push other vehicles (point c),
  • stand on the seat or the luggage rack, or sit on the handlebars (point d).

The fines are aimed at the rider. Under Article 7(1)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, carrying a passenger who uses an umbrella costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND. Carrying bulky items, holding on to other vehicles, and passengers standing on the seat or rack or sitting on the handlebars cost 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Getting on and off

Climb on only once the rider is stable, with both feet on the ground and a brake applied. Say a quick word before you get on, and sit down smoothly without pulling the scooter sideways. Getting off works the same way in reverse. Watch out for the exhaust: it gets very hot on the move, and a burn on a bare calf happens faster than you would think.

Sitting and holding on

  • Feet on the footpegs. Fold out the passenger footpegs and keep your feet on them for the whole ride, including at traffic lights.
  • Hold on properly. Use the grab rail behind the seat or the rider's hips, not their shoulders or arms, so they can steer freely.
  • Sit still. Avoid sudden movements, turning round and shifting your weight, especially when pulling away and braking.
  • Brace when braking. When the rider brakes, you slide forward. Brace with your knees or a hand on the grab rail rather than thumping into the rider.

Talking on the move

Conversation is hard on a moving scooter. Agree on simple signals before you set off, for example two taps on the shoulder for "please stop". A passenger can be a second pair of eyes for signs and turnings, but should not distract the rider with shouts or pointing, especially at junctions. Longer discussions can wait for the next stop.

Leaning into bends

In bends, rider and scooter lean together. The passenger should follow the same lean and not resist it, even if the angle feels strange. It helps to look into the bend over the rider's shoulder. A passenger who braces against the lean forces the rider into corrections that can become dangerous in tight bends.

On long rides

Sitting on the back for hours is tiring too, often more than riding, because the passenger cannot see far ahead and has to react to every brake and bend without warning. Agree on regular breaks before you set off, and let the passenger call a stop as well as the rider. A drowsy passenger who nods off can slip sideways, so if either of you starts to flag, stop somewhere safe, get off and stretch. In hot weather, both of you should drink at every stop.

Children as passengers

Under Article 33(1)(c), a child under 12 may come along as a second passenger. A child must be able to reach the footpegs and hold on securely, and needs a properly fitting helmet. Sitting in front of the rider, held from behind, is only provided for small children: someone sitting behind who steers with their arms around a person sitting in front faces 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND under Article 7(9)(h) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, unless the person in front is a child under six.

Get off on the ferry

Ferries have a special rule: while boarding, crossing and leaving, only the rider may stay on the vehicle under Article 23(3)(a) of the law, with exceptions for groups such as children, older people and the sick. Adult passengers walk on board. Our guide to taking a motorbike on a ferry has more.

Clothing for the back seat

The passenger needs protection too: proper shoes instead of flip-flops, long trousers and, in strong sun, long sleeves. Keep loose scarves and bag straps well away from the wheel and chain. Wear a backpack close to your body rather than holding it, and put heavy bags in the storage space under the seat. Our guide on what to wear riding a motorbike has more.

Frequently asked questions

Can the passenger hold the phone for navigation? The ban on holding a phone while riding under Article 7(4)(đ) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP is aimed at the rider. A passenger navigating should still hold the phone securely and call out directions early and clearly. Our guide to using a phone while riding explains the rules.

Can we put up an umbrella against the sun? No. Article 33(4)(b) expressly forbids it, and the rider pays the fine.

In short

Passengers wear a helmet with the strap fastened, sit still with their feet on the pegs, hold the grab rail or the rider's hips and lean with the bike in bends. Umbrellas, bulky items, holding on to other vehicles and standing on the seat are banned (Article 33), and the rider pays the fines (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

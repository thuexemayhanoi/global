E-Bike Rules in Vietnam

How Vietnamese law treats pedal-assisted e-bikes: the 25 km/h definition, when it becomes a moped, the compulsory motorbike helmet, passengers, lights and the fines in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Electric bicycles are an everyday sight in Vietnamese towns, and some travellers use one for short trips instead of a scooter. Because an e-bike looks like a bicycle, many visitors assume it comes without rules. Vietnamese law sees it differently: an e-bike has its own legal definition, a compulsory motorbike helmet, a passenger limit and its own list of fines. This guide explains what counts as an e-bike under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), when a vehicle that looks like one is legally a moped, and what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP charges for the common mistakes. For choosing between vehicle types in general, see our page on choosing a rental bike.

What counts as an e-bike

Article 34(2)(b) of Law 36/2024/QH15 defines the xe đạp máy, a group that includes the xe đạp điện (electric bicycle): a bicycle with motor assistance whose motor power cuts out when the rider stops pedalling or when the bicycle reaches 25 km/h. The law lists it among the non-motorised vehicles (xe thô sơ), together with ordinary bicycles and cyclos.

The definition matters because it separates e-bikes from electric mopeds. Under Article 34(1)(g), a moped (xe gắn máy) is a two- or three-wheeled vehicle driven by a motor with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if the motor is electric, a power of no more than 4 kW; the same clause states that mopeds do not include e-bikes. A vehicle whose motor keeps driving it when you stop pedalling, or keeps pushing above 25 km/h, does not meet the e-bike definition. Depending on its design speed and motor power, it is then a moped or a motorcycle under Article 34(1), with the rules for those vehicles.

In practice, look at how the vehicle works rather than what the shop calls it. Pedals that actually drive the bike, assistance that stops when you stop pedalling and no twist-grip throttle are the signs of an e-bike. An electric scooter with a seat, footboard and throttle is a moped or motorcycle, however slow it feels. Our guide to 50cc and electric scooters covers those vehicles.

Licence and age

The law distinguishes between drivers of motor vehicles and riders of non-motorised vehicles. Article 2(9) defines a "driver" (người lái xe) as a person operating a motor vehicle, and lists people operating non-motorised vehicles separately. The licence duty in Article 56(1) applies to drivers, and the licence classes in Article 57 cover motorcycles, cars and other motor vehicles; none of them covers e-bikes. An e-bike that meets the definition therefore needs no driving licence.

Article 59(1)(a) sets a minimum age of 16 for mopeds; the article does not list e-bikes. If the vehicle you are offered turns out to be a moped or motorcycle under the definitions above, the age and licence rules for those vehicles apply, which our guide on the minimum age to ride a motorbike explains.

The helmet rule: a motorbike helmet, not a bicycle helmet

Article 31(3) requires both the rider and any passenger on an e-bike to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the strap correctly fastened. Ordinary bicycles do not carry this duty in the law; e-bikes do.

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP spells out which helmet is meant. Under Article 9(4)(d), riding an e-bike without a "helmet for people riding motorcycles and mopeds", or with its strap not correctly fastened, is fined 400,000 to 600,000 VND. Under Article 9(4)(đ), carrying a passenger who does not wear such a helmet costs the same, except when taking a patient to emergency care, carrying a child under six or escorting a person who has broken the law (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). A lightweight cycling helmet does not meet this wording, so ask for a motorbike helmet for yourself and any passenger.

Passengers and loads

Article 31(1) allows one passenger on an e-bike, or two when the extra passenger is a child under seven. Carrying more people than allowed is fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(o) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, except when carrying a sick person to emergency care.

Loads have their own limits. Under Article 31(5), goods on a non-motorised vehicle must not block traffic or the rider's view, may not exceed one third of the vehicle's length, may stick out no more than 1 metre in front and behind, and no more than 0.4 metres on each side of the wheels.

Riding rules shared with motorbikes

Article 31(2) applies most of the motorbike riding bans in Article 33(3) to e-bike riders, and the passenger bans in Article 33(4) to their passengers. On an e-bike you may not use an umbrella or audio devices other than hearing aids, enter parts of the road reserved for pedestrians and other vehicles, let go of the handlebars with both hands, tow or push other vehicles or carry bulky items.

The penalties in Article 9 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP are graded:

  • Riding three or more abreast, using an umbrella or holding and using a phone: 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(g) and (h) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Letting go with both hands, swerving suddenly in front of a moving motor vehicle or steering with your feet: 150,000 to 250,000 VND under Article 9(2)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Ignoring a traffic light or a traffic officer's signal: 150,000 to 250,000 VND under Article 9(2)(b) and (đ) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Weaving or racing, or riding into a one-way street against the traffic: 300,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 9(3)(a) and (c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Lights, lanes and where you may ride

Article 31(4) requires riders of non-motorised vehicles to ride in single file, to use the lane provided for non-motorised vehicles where there is one, and to use lights or reflective signals at the front and back between 18:00 and 06:00. Riding at night without them is fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

On roads with marked lanes, Article 13(3) sends non-motorised vehicles to the lane furthest to the right, with motor vehicles to the left. In road tunnels, Article 26(1) requires non-motorised vehicles to switch on lights or carry a light-emitting signal.

E-bikes may not use expressways: Article 25(3) bans non-motorised vehicles from them, and entering one is fined 800,000 to 1,200,000 VND under Article 9(5) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Provinces can also restrict where non-motorised vehicles may operate, because Article 35(6) leaves the operating area of these vehicles to the provincial People's Committee. Our guide to motorbikes on expressways explains how to recognise an expressway entrance.

Alcohol applies to e-bikes too

Article 9(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans operating any vehicle on the road with alcohol in the blood or breath. For e-bike riders, Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets fines of 100,000 to 200,000 VND for up to 50 mg per 100 ml of blood or 0.25 mg per litre of breath (Article 9(1)(p)), 300,000 to 400,000 VND above that up to 80 mg or 0.4 mg (Article 9(3)(d)), and 400,000 to 600,000 VND above 80 mg or 0.4 mg, or for refusing a test (Article 9(4)(b) and (c)). Our guide to drink-riding rules explains the zero limit in more detail.

Before you ride an e-bike

1. Test the cut-off. Ride a few metres, stop pedalling and check that the assistance stops. If it keeps pulling, you are probably on a moped. 2. Get the right helmets. One motorbike helmet for you and one for any passenger, straps fastened. 3. Check lights and reflectors. Front and rear, before it gets dark. 4. Check the battery and charger. Our guide to renting an electric scooter explains safe charging, which applies to e-bike batteries as well. 5. Keep right and in single file. Slower vehicles keep to the right of the carriageway, so expect scooters to pass you closely.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a helmet to ride an e-bike in Vietnam? Yes, a motorbike helmet, for both you and any passenger. Article 31(3) requires a helmet that meets the national technical standard with the strap correctly fastened, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP specifies a "helmet for people riding motorcycles and mopeds". A lightweight cycling helmet does not meet this wording.

How many passengers can I carry on an e-bike in Vietnam? One, or two when the extra passenger is a child under seven, under Article 31(1). Carrying more people than allowed is fined under Article 9(1)(o) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, except when carrying a sick person to emergency care. Each passenger must also wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard under Article 31(3).

Is an electric scooter with a throttle an e-bike under Vietnamese law? No. An e-bike's motor must cut out when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h (Article 34(2)(b) of Law 36/2024/QH15), so a vehicle with a twist-grip throttle that keeps driving itself is a moped or motorcycle under Article 34(1), depending on its design speed and motor power. The age and licence rules for those vehicles then apply, while an e-bike that meets the definition needs no driving licence.

Summary

An e-bike under Vietnamese law is a pedal-assisted bicycle whose motor cuts out when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h (Article 34(2)(b)); anything that drives itself beyond that is a moped or motorcycle. E-bikes need no driving licence, but rider and passenger must wear a motorbike helmet (Article 31(3)), only one passenger is allowed, or two with a child under seven, and the motorbike riding bans largely apply. Fines under Article 9 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP run from 100,000 VND for small mistakes to 1,200,000 VND for entering an expressway.

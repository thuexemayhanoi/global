Renting an Electric Scooter in Vietnam

What to know before renting an electric scooter in Vietnam: how realistic range figures are, how long charging takes, when you need a licence and how to charge safely, including at your hotel.

Electric scooters are an everyday sight in Vietnamese cities, and more rental shops now offer them to travellers. They are quiet and need no petrol. Before you rent one, though, you should know three things: how far you will really get, how long charging takes and how to charge the battery safely, including at your accommodation. This guide explains all three using a widespread model, the VinFast Evo200, and the advice of Vietnam's fire police. For an overview of choosing a bike, see our topic page on choosing a rental bike.

Electric scooters and the low-emission zone

In Hanoi, the type of engine may matter more in future. Under the city's plan for a low-emission zone inside Ring Road 1, motorbikes and mopeds that do not meet emission standard level 3 are to be barred there from 2028; there is currently no blanket ban on petrol bikes (Government portal). Our guide to the Ring Road 1 low-emission zone explains what that means for renters.

Range: where the official figure comes from

VinFast states a range of 203 km per charge for the Evo200, measured at 30 km/h with a 65 kg rider (VinFast, Evo200 specifications). That is a very steady, slow ride with a light load. In everyday use the range is lower when you ride faster, carry a passenger or luggage, climb a lot of hills or stop and start often. Plan with a generous margin and do not wait until the battery is empty before charging.

According to the specifications, the Evo200 has a 3.5 kWh LFP battery under the footboard weighing 28 kg; the whole scooter weighs 100 kg with the battery and has a seat height of 750 mm (VinFast). These figures come from a 2022 page, so ask the shop which version you are getting.

How long charging takes

With the 400 W charger, a full charge takes about 10 hours from 0 to 100 percent and about 8 hours from 20 to 100 percent, according to VinFast; with a 1,000 W charger it takes about 4 hours and about 3.5 hours respectively (VinFast). At pickup, ask which charger you get, how full the battery is and where you can charge on the road. VinFast lists charging at its own charging stations among the model's features (VinFast).

Do you need a licence for an electric scooter?

That depends on the model. Under Article 34(1)(g) of Law 36/2024/QH15, a vehicle counts as a moped only if its design speed is 50 km/h or less and, for an electric motor, its power is 4 kW or less. Mopeds need no licence (Article 56(1) and (4)). VinFast gives the Evo200 a top speed of up to 70 km/h (VinFast), so under that definition it is not a moped but a motorbike, and electric motorbikes up to 11 kW fall into licence class A1 under Article 57(1)(a). Ask the shop for your model's top speed before you ride without a licence. Our comparison of 50cc and electric scooters helps you choose between a small petrol scooter and an electric one.

Why safe charging matters

After several fires involving electric vehicles, Vietnam's Ministry of Public Security pointed out the most common causes in 2023: poor-quality or repaired batteries, unsuitable chargers, charging for too long or too often, charging straight after a ride while the battery is still hot, charging near heat sources or flammable materials, and damp sockets or damaged cables (Ministry of Public Security).

How to charge safely

The fire police recommend, among other things (Ministry of Public Security):

1. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, and do not charge if the charger or the vehicle shows a fault; swollen or cracked batteries must be replaced. 2. Let it cool down: after a ride, wait about 20 minutes for the battery to cool, and do not charge straight after riding. 3. Not too long: do not charge for more than 8 hours at a time, and switch the ignition off while charging. 4. The right place: dry and well ventilated, indoors not in cramped closed corners, at temperatures between 0 and 35 °C; keep the vehicle, battery and charger away from flammable materials, fire and heat sources. 5. Never unattended: check regularly while charging, stop charging before going to bed or leaving the house, and never charge overnight when no adult is at home. 6. No modifications: no other chargers or batteries, and no accessories that interfere with the vehicle's electrics.

Charging at your accommodation

Ask your hotel or guesthouse in advance whether and where you may charge. Do not charge in your room, a stairwell or a corridor, and not overnight. Plan charging for times when someone is nearby, for example after arriving in the afternoon once the battery has cooled for 20 minutes. Use only the charger that belongs to the scooter and plug it straight into a fixed socket rather than an overloaded multi-plug; the fire police advise a suitable, stable power source (Ministry of Public Security).

Planning your range

  • Know your route: for trips out of town, check in advance where you can charge.
  • Leave a margin: do not count on the official figure; assume well below it.
  • Hills and passengers: both reduce range, so plan shorter distances.
  • Record the charge level: note or photograph the battery level at pickup and return to avoid disputes.

Everything else still applies

An electric scooter is subject to the same rules as a petrol one, from the helmet duty in Article 33(2) of the law to lights, mirrors and brakes. The required equipment applies whatever the drive, as our guide to rental bike defects the rider pays for explains. Being almost silent has a downside too: pedestrians often do not hear you coming, so take extra care in narrow lanes.

Frequently asked question

What if the battery runs flat on the road? Call the rental shop and agree whether it will collect the scooter or bring a charged battery. Do not let another rider tow you or push you along with a foot; Article 33(3)(đ) of the law forbids it. For a short distance to the nearest charging point, walk the scooter. Our guide to towing or pushing a motorbike explains the rules.

In short

The 203 km range figure applies at 30 km/h with a 65 kg rider (VinFast); in everyday riding you get less. Whether you need a licence depends on top speed and power (Articles 34 and 57). Charge after the battery has cooled, for no more than 8 hours, somewhere dry and ventilated, and never unattended overnight (Ministry of Public Security).

Traffic Light Rules in Vietnam for Motorbike Riders

What green, amber, red, arrows and countdowns mean at Vietnamese traffic lights, whether you may turn right on red and what running a light costs.

At Hanoi's big junctions, countdown displays tick off the seconds, green arrows light up for single directions and some lights switch to flashing amber at night. Much of it will look familiar, but some rules differ from home, and running a red light in Vietnam is expensive. This guide explains what the signals mean under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety and the signalling regulation QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, the order of precedence between signals, the question of turning right on red and the penalties under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The three colours

Under Article 11(4) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, traffic lights have three colours, with or without a countdown:

  • Green means go. If pedestrians or wheelchair users are still in the roadway, you must slow down or stop and let them cross.
  • Amber means stop before the stop line. If you are already on or past the line, you may continue.
  • Flashing amber lets you proceed, but you must watch, slow down or stop and give way to pedestrians, wheelchair users and other vehicles.
  • Red means do not go.

QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT adds where to stop on red: before the stop line, or, if there is none, before the light in your direction of travel. On vertical lights red is at the top, amber in the middle and green at the bottom; on horizontal lights red is on the left and green on the right.

Arrows, vehicle symbols and the red cross

Many junctions have extra signals. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT:

  • Green arrows: where a light has green arrows, vehicles may go only in the direction of the lit arrow.
  • Left arrow: a green left arrow also allows a U-turn, unless a ban such as the P.124 "No U-turn" sign applies.
  • Vehicle symbols: a green light showing a type of vehicle lets only that type go.
  • Red cross: a red X means stop; anyone already in the junction should leave it promptly.
  • Countdown: the number shows how long the current colour will last.

The countdown is information, not permission. Do not move off while red still shows one second, and do not accelerate to catch the last second of green.

Can you turn right on red?

The law has no general right-turn-on-red rule. Red means do not go under Article 11(4)(c), and that applies in principle to every direction. You may turn right on red only where a signal or sign at that junction expressly allows it, such as a green right arrow. Other motorbikes turning right on red are not proof that it is allowed.

Pedestrians first, even on green

Green does not mean the junction is clear. Under Article 11(4)(a) you must slow down or stop on green while pedestrians or wheelchair users are still in the roadway, and Article 12(3)(a) requires you to watch and slow down at pedestrian crossings in general. Not giving way to pedestrians on a crossing when you turn costs 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND under Article 7(5)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Flashing red at railway crossings

Alternately flashing red lights are used at level crossings. Under QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, all vehicles must stop while they are lit and may continue only once they go out; a bell often sounds as well. Article 24(1) of the law requires you to stop on the right-hand side of your road before the stop line. Never dash across the tracks just as the lights come on.

Officer, light or sign: what counts most

Article 11(2) of the law sets a fixed order: first the signals of a person directing traffic, then the traffic lights, then road signs, then road markings. If a traffic officer is directing a junction, follow the officer's hand signals even if the light shows something else. Our guide to right of way at junctions describes the most important hand signals.

What running a light costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP treats signal offences as serious:

  • Running a red light: 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Ignoring a traffic officer's signals: also 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(d) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Points: both offences also cost 4 licence points under Article 7(13)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Offences can also be recorded by cameras; the notice then goes to the registered owner under Article 47 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which for a rental bike is the rental shop, and the owner must help identify the rider. Our guide to traffic police stops explains what happens at a roadside check.

Common mistakes at the lights

  • Rolling over the stop line: waiting on red means behind the line, not on the pedestrian crossing.
  • Going on the countdown: the display shows the time left; it is not a green light.
  • Following the crowd: if others ride on red, red is still red.
  • Reaching for the phone: do not pick up your phone even at a red light; our guide to using a phone while riding explains why.

Traffic lights in everyday Hanoi

In practice, dozens of motorbikes bunch up at every red light and many edge forward past the stop line. Stay behind the line even if others do not, because the stop line is part of the light rule. When the light turns green, everyone moves at once; pull away smoothly, look left and right for stragglers from the side road and expect pedestrians still crossing. At junctions without lights, the right-of-way rules apply.

At night some lights switch to flashing amber. That is not a free pass: you may go, but approach slowly and give way to anyone already crossing. Our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders describes how the traffic flows.

In short

Green means go, but pedestrians first; amber means stop before the line; flashing amber means proceed with care; red means stop (Article 11). Green arrows release single directions, and a left arrow also allows a U-turn (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). Turning right on red needs an express permission, and running a red light costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND and 4 points under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

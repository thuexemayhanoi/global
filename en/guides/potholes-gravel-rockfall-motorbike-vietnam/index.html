Potholes, Gravel and Rockfall on a Scooter

How to handle potholes, gravel, mud and rockfall on a scooter in Vietnam: the warning signs W.221, W.222 and W.228, what the law requires and the riding technique for loose or damaged surfaces.

In the Vietnamese countryside and mountains, the road surface often changes without warning. Smooth tarmac one moment, then a pothole, a stretch of gravel left by roadworks or a tongue of mud that has slid off the hillside after rain. In the rainy season, fallen rocks join the mix. For scooter riders on small wheels these are the trickiest moments of a trip. This guide explains the relevant warning signs, what the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety requires and how to get across loose or damaged surfaces safely. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Know the warning signs

The national road sign standard QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT has several warning signs for poor road surfaces:

  • W.221a "Đường lồi lõm" (uneven road): placed where a good road suddenly turns into a bumpy stretch with humps, potholes or ripples on which fast riding is dangerous.
  • W.221b "Đường có gồ giảm tốc": warns of speed bumps.
  • W.222a "Đường trơn": warns of a slippery road.
  • W.222b "Lề đường nguy hiểm": warns of a dangerous verge.
  • W.228a and W.228b "Đá lở": falling rocks.
  • W.228c "Sỏi đá bắn lên": flying gravel.
  • W.228d "Nền đường yếu": a weak road edge that may not bear weight.

When you see one of these signs, ease off at once. On mountain roads they are often repeated, because the hazard runs for many kilometres.

What the law requires

Article 12(3) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires riders to watch, slow down or stop at danger warning signs or obstacles on the road (point b) and in rain, wind, fog, smoke and dust, on slippery or muddy surfaces and on roads with a lot of earth, stones or fallen material (point m). Point (đ) adds fords and submerged stretches of road. These duties apply whatever the speed limit.

A rider who ignores them and causes a crash faces 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and loses 10 points. Our guide on where you must slow down in Vietnam lists the other places the law names.

Why two wheels are most at risk

The UK's Highway Code expressly reminds drivers that motorcyclists and cyclists may suddenly need to avoid uneven road surfaces and obstacles such as drain covers or oily, wet or icy patches, and tells them to give these riders plenty of room (GOV.UK, Highway Code, rule 213). For you as a rider, that works the other way round: expect cars and lorries not always to show that consideration, and plan any change of line early.

Riding over loose and wet surfaces

1. Brake beforehand. Lose speed while the surface is still firm, not once you are on the gravel. 2. Stay upright. Loose surfaces offer little grip for leaning. Ride the stretch as straight as you can. 3. Keep it smooth. No sharp acceleration and no hard braking; a light, steady throttle keeps the scooter stable. 4. Both brakes, gently. If you have to brake, use both brakes smoothly rather than grabbing a lever; abrupt braking can lock a wheel (Oregon DMV, Section 4). 5. Look well ahead. Look where you want to go, not at the pothole right in front of the wheel. 6. Cross edges squarely. Ride over ridges, rails and the edge of the tarmac as close to a right angle as possible so that the front wheel does not slide away.

Our guides on how to brake on a scooter and on riding bends and hairpins explain the technique in more detail.

Prepare the tyres and the scooter

Bad roads show up the state of the scooter. UK compulsory basic training makes simple checks before every ride part of the syllabus, including the tyres and their tread and the electrics (GOV.UK, CBT element B). Before a trip, check the tread and pressure of both tyres, and ask the shop to replace worn tyres before you head for the mountains.

With a passenger or luggage, the scooter is heavier and responds more sluggishly on loose ground. Ask your passenger to sit still on gravel; our guide to riding pillion has more.

Rockfall and landslides in the rainy season

In the rainy season, rocks and earth come loose from hillsides. Where sign W.228 is posted, ride through briskly but calmly and do not stop below the slopes, for example for photos. If material lies on the road, ride round it slowly, watching for oncoming traffic. After heavy rain, it is often wiser to postpone a mountain stage than to fight your way through muddy sections. Our guide to riding in the rainy season has tips for the wet months.

Flooded roads and temporary bridges

When water covers the road, you cannot see what lies beneath: a pothole, a ledge, an open drain. Do not ride through water of unknown depth. The road sign standard is clear about temporary bridges: at sign W.213 "Cầu tạm", take care, and in floods the bridge must not be used at all (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, Annex C.13).

Typical trouble spots in Vietnam

  • Roadworks: fresh gravel and sand, often without warning; sign W.227 announces roadworks.
  • Village centres: sand and soil from building sites, water from car washes and spilled oil outside workshops.
  • Road markings and steel plates: slippery when wet; do not brake or lean hard on them.
  • Bends on passes: sand and grit collect on the exit of the bend.

Frequently asked questions

Should I put my feet down on gravel? No. Keep your feet on the footboard while moving. Dragging your feet costs more balance than it gains. Ride more slowly and you will not need to dab.

Can I avoid a gravel stretch? Sometimes. Check your map app and ask locals whether there is a paved alternative. A half-hour detour is often safer than ten kilometres of loose gravel on a rental scooter.

What if I see a pothole too late? Do not brake hard or swerve at the last moment. Keep the handlebars straight and ride through on a steady throttle. A late, sharp swerve is usually more dangerous than the pothole itself.

In short

Signs W.221, W.222 and W.228 warn of uneven, slippery and rockfall-prone roads (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). The law requires you to watch out and slow down there (Article 12(3)). Brake beforehand, ride upright and smoothly over loose surfaces, and never ride through water of unknown depth.

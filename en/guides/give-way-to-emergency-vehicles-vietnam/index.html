Giving Way to Emergency Vehicles in Vietnam

Which vehicles have priority on Vietnamese roads, what red, blue-and-red and blue flashing lights mean, how to make way on a motorbike in heavy traffic and what obstructing them costs.

A siren in Hanoi's dense traffic, a flash of red or blue light, and every rider around you shifting at once: for visitors on a motorbike it is an uneasy moment. Where do you go when the road is packed with two-wheelers? And which vehicles actually have priority? Article 27 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety spells it out. This guide explains which vehicles have priority, what the colours of their lights mean, how to make way and what Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP charges for getting in their way. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

Which vehicles have priority

Article 27(1) of the law lists the priority vehicles:

  • fire engines of the fire and rescue police, and firefighting vehicles of other forces called to a fire,
  • military, police and procuracy vehicles on urgent duty, and convoys led by a traffic police vehicle,
  • ambulances on emergency duty,
  • vehicles protecting dykes, carrying out rescue and relief, or responding to natural disasters, epidemics or a state of emergency,
  • funeral processions.

When several of them meet at a junction, Article 27(2) sets the order: fire engines first, then military and police vehicles on duty and escorted convoys, then ambulances, then dyke-protection and rescue vehicles, and funeral processions last.

What the light colours mean

The first four groups must use a priority signal under Article 27(3), and the colour of the flashing light tells you who is coming:

| Light | Who uses it | |---|---| | Red | Fire engines on duty and ambulances on emergency calls | | Blue and red | Police, military and procuracy vehicles on urgent duty, and traffic police vehicles leading a convoy | | Blue | Dyke-protection, rescue and disaster-response vehicles, and vehicles on state-of-emergency duty |

Funeral processions have priority but use no such signal. The colours were reset by amending Law 118/2025/QH15, in force since 1 July 2026; the consolidated text on the government portal reflects that version (Law 36/2024/QH15).

What priority vehicles may do

Under Article 27(4), vehicles using a priority signal are not bound by speed limits. They may run red lights, drive against the flow and use other passable routes; on expressways they may only go against the flow on the emergency lane. They must still obey traffic police signals and temporary signs. For riders the key point is that an emergency vehicle can appear from any direction, including where you do not expect traffic.

How to make way on a motorbike

Everyone else's duty is in Article 27(5): when a priority vehicle signals, you must slow down, move to the right-hand edge or stop to let it pass, and must not obstruct it. Toll stations must let such vehicles through first in every case.

In practice, on a motorbike:

1. Stay calm. Check your mirrors and work out where the siren is coming from instead of braking hard. 2. Move right. Ease over to the right-hand edge and stop there if necessary. 3. Don't tailgate it. Never follow an emergency vehicle to get through a jam faster. 4. Keep watching. A second vehicle often follows. Only move off once the road is clear again. 5. Warn your passenger. A quick word before you brake and pull over lets them hold on.

The law also requires you to watch and slow down or stop when you meet a priority vehicle (Article 12(3)(l)) and not to overtake in that situation (Article 14(6)(g)).

At junctions and red lights

If an emergency vehicle arrives while you are waiting at a red light, do not just roll over the stop line; make room only as far as you can without endangering anyone. The traffic light rules still apply to you. In a queue, riders around you may scatter to both sides; aim for the right-hand side, as the law asks, rather than following the crowd into the opposite lane. Our guides to traffic lights and right of way at junctions explain how both normally work.

What obstructing them costs

Failing to give way to, or obstructing, a priority vehicle on duty with its signal on costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(đ) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and 4 points come off your licence under Article 7(13)(b).

The reverse is banned too: fitting or using emergency-style signal equipment on a vehicle without priority costs 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(đ), and the equipment is confiscated under Article 7(12)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). The law itself forbids fitting or using priority signals unlawfully in Article 9(24).

Sirens at night and your mirrors

The night-time horn ban between 10 p.m. and 5 a.m. in residential areas and around hospitals does not apply to priority vehicles under Article 21(2), so expect sirens and horns from emergency vehicles at night too.

You will usually spot an emergency vehicle behind you in your mirror first. A motorbike without a working left mirror may not be ridden; that costs 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 14(1)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Adjust the mirrors before you set off so you can see the traffic behind you; our rental pickup checklist reminds you to check mirrors and lights at handover.

Police escorts and funeral processions

Convoys led by traffic police appear in Hanoi now and then during official visits. Under Article 27(1) they count as priority vehicles. Follow the signals of officers at the roadside and do not tag on to the convoy. Funeral processions also have priority; let them pass calmly and do not push past.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to make way for an ambulance without a siren? The duty in Article 27(5) is tied to the priority signal. Without it, an ambulance is an ordinary vehicle in traffic. Still, make room if you can see it is in a hurry.

What if I cannot move right? Then slow down or stop, which Article 27(5) expressly allows. What matters is not obstructing the vehicle.

In short

Fire engines and ambulances use red lights, police and military on duty blue and red, and rescue vehicles blue (Article 27). When a priority signal is on, slow down, pull right or stop. Obstructing an emergency vehicle costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND and 4 points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

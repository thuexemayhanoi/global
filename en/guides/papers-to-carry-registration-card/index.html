Papers for a Vietnam Rental Bike: the Blue Card and Your Own Documents

What the blue card is, the four documents Vietnamese law says a rider carries, what a shop's photocopy does not settle, and a five-minute check of the papers against the bike before you ride off.

Ask a Hanoi rental shop whether the bike has its "blue card" and the answer is nearly always yes. The more useful questions come next: what the card is, who holds it, what you have to carry yourself and what happens if a police officer asks to see it. This guide separates the four documents Vietnamese law names from the paperwork shops hand out out of habit, and it ends with a short routine for the pickup counter. It covers the papers you need on the road. The contract itself is in our guide to rental agreements, changes and cancellations, and the bike-condition checks are in the pickup checklist.

What the "blue card" is

"Blue card" is how English-speaking riders and rental shops usually refer to the bike's vehicle registration certificate. In Vietnamese the document is the giấy đăng ký xe or chứng nhận đăng ký xe, and in everyday speech people say "cà vẹt", a word borrowed from the French carte verte, as LuatVietnam explains. The certificate is issued to the owner and records both the owner and the vehicle: make, model, plate number, frame number and engine number, according to the same LuatVietnam article.

Think of it as the bike's identity and ownership paper. It is not a licence for you, it is not insurance and it is not your rental contract. It belongs to the owner of the bike, which in a rental is the shop or the person behind it, so the original normally stays in the owner's hands unless you agree otherwise.

The four documents the law says a rider carries

Article 56 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, says that when you ride you must carry four things, as the Government portal summarises:

  • the vehicle registration certificate, or, where a bank holds the original because the bike is mortgaged, a certified copy together with the lender's confirmation;
  • a driving licence that matches the type of vehicle you ride;
  • the inspection certificate, for vehicle types that must be inspected;
  • the compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate for the vehicle.

The same article adds that when a document has been integrated into the owner's electronic identity account, it can be shown and checked through that account instead of on paper, according to the same Government portal page. Whether that helps a renter is a question for the shop, because the registration and the insurance belong to the owner and not to you.

Why the registration certificate is your problem too

Fines for the papers fall on whoever is riding. Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND for riding without a registration certificate (or, for a mortgaged bike, without a certified copy and the lender's receipt) or with one that has lapsed. The same range applies to using a certificate that has been altered, does not match the bike's frame and engine numbers or was not issued by the competent authority. Both rules are in Article 14 of the decree's full text. A missing plate, or a plate that does not match the certificate, is fined at 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND in the same article. Where the rider cannot show where the bike came from, the decree also provides for the vehicle to be confiscated.

The decree speaks of riding "without" a certificate and does not say how an officer must treat a plain photocopy, which is why the next section matters. Whether the shop takes any responsibility for a bike with bad papers is a contract question, so settle it in writing before you sign.

Original, copy or app: what to ask the shop

Under the law's wording the document you carry is the registration certificate itself, and the only copy it mentions is a certified copy of a mortgaged bike's certificate, backed by the lender's confirmation, as the Government portal's text of Article 56 shows. A shop's plain photocopy of the card is not mentioned. Many shops hand one out anyway and keep the original, and that may work at an ordinary check, but nothing in these texts promises that an officer will accept it. So ask before you leave:

  • Which document will I carry: the original, a copy or an electronic version?
  • What do you do if I am stopped and the officer wants to see the original?
  • Will someone from the shop come to me, and how quickly?
  • Who pays if the bike is held because of its papers, or if I have to pay at the roadside because of them?
  • Can you put the answers in the rental contract or in a message?

For a rental of several weeks, ask whether you can carry the original and leave a copy at the shop. Some owners agree and some will not, and either answer tells you something about the shop.

Check the card against the bike before you leave

Spend five minutes comparing the paper with the machine. Each check is quick, and each one catches a different kind of problem:

  • The owner's name on the card matches the shop or the person who rents you the bike, or the shop can explain the difference.
  • The plate number on the card is the plate on the bike.
  • The frame number and engine number on the card match the numbers stamped on the bike; ask the shop to show you where to find them.
  • Make, model and colour match what is in front of you.
  • No field looks erased, overwritten or glued over.
  • Photograph both sides of the card or copy, the plate and the numbers on the bike, and send the photos to yourself.

A bike whose card shows another plate or other numbers is the situation Article 14 of the decree addresses, as its full text shows, and it is the one you do not want to discover at a roadside check. If anything is off, ask for a different bike rather than for an explanation.

Your own licence and the international permit

The licence is the one document that does not depend on the shop. Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets a fine of 2,000,000 to 4,000,000 VND for riding a two-wheeled motorbike of up to 125 cc, or an electric one up to 11 kW, without a valid licence for it, and 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND above that size, according to the decree's full text. An international driving permit issued under the 1968 UN Convention on Road Traffic is not enough on its own: riding without the national licence that goes with it is fined in the same ranges, as the same text shows.

Which permits Vietnam accepts and which classes cover a rental scooter are explained in our guides to international driving permits and to foreign driving licences and motorbike classes. For the roadside check the point is simpler: carry both documents, and make sure the class on them matches the bike you are riding.

Compulsory insurance: ask for the certificate

Riding without a valid compulsory insurance certificate is fined at 200,000 to 300,000 VND under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. The policy is bought by the bike's owner, and its certificate is the document you carry. Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP accepts an electronic certificate, so a PDF or a photo sent by the shop can serve, and it is worth asking for one before you leave. The cover is compulsory civil liability: it pays for harm you cause to other people and their property, up to fixed limits. It does not replace your travel insurance, which is where your own injuries and medical costs belong, so ask your insurer whether riding a motorbike is covered and under which conditions.

If police ask for your papers

Stop in a safe place, switch off the engine and show the documents politely, with the registration first. If you carry a copy, say that the original is with the owner, offer the shop's phone number and call the shop while you wait. Do not hand over your passport to settle a check. If you are asked to sign a written record, photograph it first and do not sign what you cannot read; ask for a translation or call someone who can help.

Most checks end within minutes when the papers are in order. The point of the routine in this guide is that the bike's papers, rather than your negotiating skills, answer the officer's questions.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays the fine if a rental bike's registration papers are missing or wrong? The rider does, because fines for the papers fall on whoever is riding. Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines riding without a valid registration certificate or with one that does not match the bike, as the decree's full text shows. Whether the shop shares that risk is a contract question, so settle it in writing before you sign.

Can I carry an electronic insurance certificate for a rental bike in Vietnam? Yes. Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP accepts an electronic certificate, so a PDF or a photo sent by the shop can serve as the document you carry. Ask for it before you leave, because riding without a valid compulsory insurance certificate is fined under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

What should I do if the plate on the blue card does not match the rental bike? Ask for a different bike rather than for an explanation. A card that shows another plate or other frame and engine numbers is the situation Article 14 of the decree addresses, as its full text shows, and you do not want to discover it at a roadside check. Compare the card, plate, frame number and engine number before you leave, and photograph them.

A pickup routine in five minutes

  • Ask which document you will carry and get the answer in writing.
  • Compare the card, the plate, the frame number and the engine number with the bike.
  • Photograph the card, the plate and the numbers.
  • Ask for the insurance certificate, on paper or electronically.
  • Put your licence and permit where you can reach them without unpacking.

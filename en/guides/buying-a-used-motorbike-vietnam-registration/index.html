Buying a used motorbike in Vietnam: registration, papers and fines

Buying a second-hand motorbike in Vietnam? What the law requires when a bike changes hands, the owner and rider fines for missing transfers, papers and plates under Decree 168, and what to check first.

Every year thousands of travellers buy a second-hand motorbike in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, ride it the length of the country and sell it on to the next traveller at the other end. The deal is usually done in a café: some cash, a key and a small blue registration card, often in the name of a Vietnamese person neither buyer nor seller has ever met. It feels simple, but Vietnamese law attaches clear duties to a change of owner. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets the conditions for registering a vehicle after it changes hands, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines owners who skip the transfer and riders whose papers or plates are not in order. This guide explains what the law requires, who is fined for what, when a bike can be confiscated and what to check before you hand over any money. For papers in general, see our hub on documents and contracts.

What the law requires when a motorbike changes hands

Registration is tied to the owner, not just to the bike. Under Article 39(6)(a) of Law 36/2024/QH15, the registration certificate and plate are revoked when ownership of the vehicle is transferred. Under Article 39(2), registering the vehicle for a new owner requires three things:

  • the certificate showing that the old registration and plate have been revoked;
  • proof of lawful ownership;
  • proof that the financial obligations, such as registration fees, have been met.

The step-by-step procedure, including the documents each kind of owner must present, is set by the Minister of Public Security (Article 39(7)). This guide does not try to list those documents: they depend on the owner's status, residence and papers, and the local vehicle registration office of the police is the place that decides. What the law makes clear is that a sale is not finished, legally, until the registration has followed the bike.

What the owners risk

Article 32 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines the owner of a motorbike, not the rider, for registration failures. For an individual owner:

  • not completing the registration after a change of owner: VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 (Article 32(3)(a));
  • not completing the procedure to revoke the registration and plate as required: VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 (Article 32(8)(d));
  • putting a motorbike on the road without a registration certificate, or with one that has expired: VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 (Article 32(8)(đ));
  • putting a motorbike on the road without plates, with plates that do not match the certificate or with plates not issued by the authorities: VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 (Article 32(8)(h)), and the plates are confiscated (Article 32(18)(a)).

Organisations pay double: VND 1,600,000 to 2,000,000 under Article 32(3) and VND 8,000,000 to 12,000,000 under Article 32(8) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For the failures in Article 32(8)(d) and (đ), the owner is also ordered to complete the missing registration steps (Article 32(19)(g)).

What the rider risks

The person riding the bike is fined separately under Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, whoever owns it:

  • riding without a registration certificate, or with an expired one: VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 (Article 14(2)(a));
  • using a certificate that has been altered, that does not match the frame and engine numbers or that was not issued by the authorities: VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 (Article 14(2)(b)), and the certificate is withdrawn (Article 14(7));
  • riding without plates, with plates that do not match the certificate or with plates not issued by the authorities: VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 (Article 14(3)(a)), and the plates are confiscated (Article 14(5)(a)).

These offences also cost licence points: 2 points for Article 14(2)(a) to (c) and 6 points for Article 14(3) (Article 14(8)). How the points system works is explained in our guide to Vietnam's licence points system. While riding, you must carry the registration certificate in any case (Article 56(1)(a) of Law 36/2024/QH15); our guide on which papers to carry lists the rest.

When the bike itself is lost

Under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, two situations end with the bike being taken for good, and both are risks for a buyer.

  • Tampered frame or engine numbers. Cutting, welding, erasing or re-stamping frame or engine numbers, or putting a vehicle with such numbers on the road, leads to confiscation of the vehicle (Article 32(17)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • No valid papers and no proof of origin. If a rider has no registration certificate, or one that was not issued by the authorities, does not match the frame and engine numbers or has been altered, and cannot prove where the vehicle comes from or that it is lawfully owned, the vehicle is confiscated (Article 14(5)(b); for owners, Article 32(18)(b)).

Before a penalty decision, the police may also hold the bike in a number of cases (Article 48 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP); our guide on when police can impound a motorbike explains the procedure.

The rules at a glance

| Situation | Who is fined | Penalty | |---|---|---| | New owner does not register the bike after buying it | Owner | VND 800,000–1,000,000 for an individual (Decree 168, Article 32(3)(a)) | | Owner does not complete the revocation of registration and plate | Owner | VND 4–6 million for an individual (Decree 168, Article 32(8)(d)) | | Bike on the road without a valid registration certificate | Owner and rider | Owner VND 4–6 million (Article 32(8)(đ)); rider VND 2–3 million and 2 points (Article 14(2)(a)) | | Plates missing, not matching or not official | Owner and rider | Owner VND 4–6 million (Article 32(8)(h)); rider VND 4–6 million and 6 points (Article 14(3)(a)); plates confiscated | | Tampered frame or engine numbers | Owner | Confiscation of the bike (Decree 168, Article 32(17)(a)) | | No valid papers and no proof of origin | Rider | Confiscation of the bike (Decree 168, Article 14(5)(b)) |

Before you buy

The law sets the minimum. These checks keep a cheap bike from becoming an expensive problem:

  • Match the numbers. Compare the frame and engine numbers stamped on the bike with those on the registration certificate, and look for signs of grinding or re-stamping. A mismatch is a reason to walk away (Article 14(2)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Match the plate. The plate number must be the one on the certificate (Article 14(3)(a)).
  • Ask who is on the card. If the person selling is not the person named on the certificate, ask how the transfer will be done before you agree a price. A bike that has passed through many hands without a transfer is hard to register later, because registration for a new owner needs the revocation of the old registration (Article 39(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15).
  • Keep the paperwork. Keep any written sale agreement and receipts; proof of lawful ownership is one of the conditions for registration (Article 39(2)(b)).
  • Ask the registration office first. The documents a buyer must present are set by the Ministry of Public Security (Article 39(7)). Ask the local police registration office what applies to you before you buy, not after.
  • Sort out the rest of the legal kit. Owning a bike does not let you ride it: you still need a valid licence for its class (Article 56(1)), see our guides to foreign licences and getting a Vietnamese licence, and the bike needs compulsory insurance.
  • Check the city rules. Hanoi has started a low-emission zone inside Ring Road 1, with restrictions phased in step by step; read our guide to the Ring Road 1 low-emission zone before you plan to keep a petrol bike in the centre.

Frequently asked questions

Is it illegal to ride a bike whose blue card is in someone else's name? Riding a bike registered to someone else is not in itself an offence: borrowed bikes are ridden that way every day. What the law requires is that a new owner registers the vehicle after buying it, and the fine for not doing so falls on the owner (Article 32(3)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Lending rules are explained in our guide on letting someone else ride your motorbike.

What if the seller has lost the blue card? Then the bike has no registration certificate, and riding it is fined under Article 14(2)(a). A lost certificate can be reissued (Article 39(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15); ask for that to be done before you buy.

Can the police take the bike if the papers are missing? They can hold it in several cases, and it is confiscated when there are no valid papers and no proof of origin (Article 14(5)(b) of the decree).

Do I lose licence points for registration problems? The rider does: 2 or 6 points under Article 14(8). The owner's fines in Article 32 carry no points.

Summary

When a motorbike is sold, its registration and plate are revoked and the new owner must register it, with proof of revocation, ownership and fees paid (Article 39 of Law 36/2024/QH15). Owners who skip the steps are fined VND 800,000 to 6,000,000 as individuals (Article 32 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP), riders without valid papers or plates VND 2,000,000 to 6,000,000 plus points (Article 14), and a bike with tampered numbers or no proof of origin can be confiscated.

Check the numbers, the plate and the name on the card before you pay, and go through the bike itself with our motorbike checklist.

Hanoi's Ring Road 1 low-emission zone, explained for motorbike renters

Hanoi has not banned petrol motorbikes across Ring Road 1. What the low-emission zone restricts in 2026, what changes in 2027 and 2028, and how renters can plan around it.

Since mid-2026 many visitors have read that Hanoi "banned petrol motorbikes" from its centre. The real picture is narrower and phased. The Government portal's announcement of the scheme states that from 1 July 2026 the city did not ban all petrol and diesel motorcycles, mopeds and cars across the Ring Road 1 area. Instead it began with restrictions by vehicle type and time in two areas of Hoàn Kiếm ward, and it published a roadmap that tightens step by step until 2029. This guide explains what applies now, what changes next and how someone riding a rented scooter can plan around it. For the wider picture of riding in the city, start with our guide to riding safely in Hanoi, and before you take a bike, work through the Hanoi motorbike rental pickup checklist.

What the low-emission zone covers

Ring Road 1 is the innermost ring of Hanoi's road network, wrapped around the historic centre. For the purposes of the scheme it covers nine wards: Hoàn Kiếm, Cửa Nam, Ba Đình, Giảng Võ, Ngọc Hà, Tây Hồ, Ô Chợ Dừa, Hai Bà Trưng and Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám. Within that ring the plan defines smaller Areas 1, 2 and 3, and the official notice says their exact boundaries are drawn on the map attached to Decision No. 3273/QĐ-UBND of the Hanoi People's Committee. The Tuổi Trẻ report on the published scheme reproduces that map. The legal groundwork came earlier: according to a summary of the city's plans on the national news portal Vietnam.vn, the Hanoi People's Council adopted a resolution on low-emission zones in November 2025 that allows the city to restrict fossil-fuel vehicles by area, time and vehicle type, and to keep petrol motorbikes out of ride-hailing work inside such zones.

The point to remember is that the zone grows in stages. A rule that applies to two areas of one ward in 2026 is not automatically a rule for all of central Hanoi, and the reverse is also true: restrictions that sound distant today are already written into the timetable.

The rules from July to December 2026

The first phase, running from 1 July to 31 December 2026, is a pilot limited to Areas 1 and 2 of Hoàn Kiếm ward, the ward around Hoàn Kiếm Lake that takes in much of the Old Quarter. The only outright prohibition that affects a scooter rider is in Area 1: according to the official announcement, all road motor vehicles, including motorcycles, mopeds and cars, are barred there from 19:00 to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Tuổi Trẻ adds that priority vehicles defined by law, such as ambulances and fire engines, may still operate around the clock.

Everything else in phase 1 is either a recommendation or aimed at larger vehicles. Petrol motorbikes that carry passengers for ride-hailing apps are "encouraged" to limit their activity in the two areas, and residents are encouraged to stop using motorcycles made or imported before 2008 and mopeds made or imported before 2016. Coaches with more than 16 seats and small trucks may only run outside the scheme's peak hours, which are defined as 06:00–09:00 and 16:00–19:30 every day, while heavier trucks are limited to night-time hours.

In practice, a petrol rental scooter can still be ridden through central Hanoi in 2026. What changes is the weekend evening: if you plan to spend a Friday, Saturday or Sunday night around the lake, park outside Area 1 before 19:00 or arrive on foot. Riding past a sign that closes a road or area to your type of vehicle is a traffic violation penalised under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, so treat the signs at the zone boundary as binding even when the street looks quiet.

What changes in 2027

From 1 January 2027 the pilot expands to Areas 1, 2 and 3 across Hoàn Kiếm and Cửa Nam wards. The published roadmap turns several phase 1 recommendations into rules. Petrol motorcycles and mopeds may no longer operate for ride-hailing apps inside the zone, and the app companies are made responsible for routing their drivers around it. If you rely on motorbike taxis booked through an app, expect pickups at the zone edge or electric bikes inside it. For private motorbikes the city continues to encourage riders to retire older machines and switch to vehicles that meet emission level 3 or above, while cars that fail emission level 4 are not allowed in the zone at all.

The published text also lists the level 3 requirement for motorcycles among the 2027 measures, while the city's own summary presents it as the rule for the whole of Ring Road 1 from 2028. If you plan to ride an older bike into Hoàn Kiếm or Cửa Nam during 2027, check the current enforcement position first.

2028 to 2029: the whole of Ring Road 1

From 1 January 2028 the low-emission zone extends to the entire Ring Road 1 area. Motorcycles and mopeds that do not meet emission level 3 under the national technical regulation QCVN 99:2025/BNNMT will not be allowed to circulate there, petrol ride-hailing bikes remain excluded, and fossil-fuel cars must meet level 4. From 2030 the city intends to keep the phase 3 measures in force while it reviews the results and considers raising the requirements further.

For a renter this is the stage that matters most. A shop's older petrol scooters may simply not be allowed into the historic centre, so the age and emission standard of the bike become part of choosing it, alongside engine size and licence class.

What this means if you rent a motorbike

The practical consequences depend on when you travel:

  • During 2026: plan weekend evenings in the lake area on foot, and leave the bike in a guarded car park outside Area 1. The rest of central Hanoi works as before.
  • During 2027: expect ride-hailing motorbikes inside Hoàn Kiếm and Cửa Nam to be electric. If your rental bike is old, ask the shop whether it meets emission level 3 before you ride into the pilot areas.
  • From 2028: confirm in writing that the bike meets level 3 if you will ride inside Ring Road 1, and keep the registration papers with you, because they show the model and year.
  • Long-term rentals: if your contract runs across 1 January 2027 or 1 January 2028, agree in advance what happens if the bike can no longer enter the zone you need.

An electric scooter avoids the fossil-fuel rules altogether, but it brings its own planning: check the range, where you can charge or swap a battery, and how the shop handles a flat battery. The Hanoi Department of Construction told reporters in April 2026 that the city was preparing compact battery-swapping cabinets in public spaces, according to Vietnam.vn; their coverage is still growing, so ask the shop for the locations it uses.

Getting around the zone without a motorbike

Hanoi is also trying to make the centre easier to reach without a private motorbike. According to the same Vietnam.vn report, the Ring Road 1 area is served by two urban railway lines, 2A and 3.1, and by 45 subsidised bus routes, and the city had identified more than 210 possible parking locations at the gateways to the pilot zone so that people can leave a vehicle and continue on public transport. For a visitor, combining a rented scooter for trips outside the centre with walking, buses or the metro inside it is often the least stressful plan, especially at weekends.

Check the current rules before you ride

The scheme is designed to be adjusted between phases, and the signs on the street are what traffic police enforce. Before relying on any date in this guide, look at the official announcement and recent local reporting such as Tuổi Trẻ, particularly if you are staying for several weeks around 1 January 2027 or 1 January 2028.

What to do after a motorbike accident in Vietnam: the first steps, in order

What Vietnamese law requires at the scene of a road accident, which numbers to call, what to expect from police and hospital, what to tell the rental shop and the insurer, and what to keep.

A motorbike accident is the situation every renter hopes to avoid and few have thought through. This guide sets out what Vietnamese law and official travel advice say about the first hours: what you must do at the scene, who to call, what to expect from the police and the hospital, and what to tell the rental shop and the insurer. It is not legal or medical advice. If anyone is badly hurt, call for help first and read the rest later. For the wider picture on riding more safely, see our section on safer riding and support in Hanoi and the pick-up checklist.

The first minutes: stop, make the scene safe, call for help

Vietnam's road traffic law (Law 36/2024/QH15) is direct about what a driver involved in an accident must do: stop at once, warn others of the danger, leave the scene as it is, help anyone who is injured and report the accident to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee. Article 80 sets these duties out in the full text on the Government Portal. The US State Department's page on Vietnam gives the same list in plain English: stop immediately, help injured people, stay at the scene, keep everything as it is until the police arrive and report the accident (travel.state.gov).

The emergency numbers are 113 for the police, 114 for fire and rescue and 115 for an ambulance, according to this list of emergency numbers. People who happen to be at the scene also have duties under the same article: they must help the injured, report the accident, help protect the scene and the victims' belongings and give the authorities the information they ask for. You can therefore ask bystanders to call, to help and to keep traffic away, and you do not have to manage everything alone.

Two points catch people out. First, you are expected to stay until the police arrive, with one exception: you may leave to get emergency treatment, to take an injured person for emergency treatment, or because your own life or health is under threat, but you must then report to the nearest police or People's Committee straight away. Second, the vehicles involved may be used to take a victim to hospital only if no other vehicle is available, and then you must be able to show where the vehicles and the victim were and must not alter any traces of the accident; if someone has died, the scene stays as it is and the body is covered (Article 80, full text). When it is safe, photograph the position of the bikes, the road and any damage before anything moves.

Police, statements and the investigation

You must give the authorities your identity details, those of the other people involved and the information you have about the accident. Under Article 83, every road accident is investigated. Where there is no sign of a crime, the investigation covers alcohol and drug tests for the drivers, examination of the scene and the vehicles, temporary holding of vehicles, objects, documents and the drivers' licences, statements from drivers, victims and witnesses, and checks of documents; where there are signs of a crime, the criminal procedure rules apply instead. Depending on the nature and degree of the violation, the person who caused the accident may face an administrative sanction or criminal liability, and must compensate any damage under the law (full text).

Expect the process to take longer than the accident did. The UK Foreign Office warns that a person involved in an accident could face criminal charges and may need to pay compensation to injured people even when the injuries are minor, that a travel ban is possible while the investigation is carried out, and that foreign nationals have been stopped from leaving Vietnam until the police finished and have been asked to agree to out-of-court financial settlements. It advises getting professional legal advice and representation in that situation and tells British nationals to inform the British Embassy in Hanoi or the Consulate General in Ho Chi Minh City (FCDO travel advice for Vietnam). Whatever your nationality, contact your own embassy or consulate early.

In practice: be courteous and co-operative, say clearly if you do not understand a question or a document and do not sign anything you cannot read. Ask someone you trust who reads Vietnamese, such as the shop or the hotel staff, to help you, and photograph any paper you are handed. Do not agree to a payment or a settlement on the spot without taking advice first.

Medical care and who pays

Under Article 81, a medical facility that gives first emergency care to a road-accident victim must report to the nearest police and test the blood of the drivers involved for alcohol, drugs and other stimulants; a facility that cannot run the test must take a blood sample, keep it properly and send it on. If you were riding, expect that test and expect the police to hear of your admission. Article 82 adds that medical facilities must support and treat accident victims in all cases (full text).

None of that settles the bill. The US State Department notes that if you are in an accident without a Vietnamese driver's licence, your travel insurance may not pay your medical bills (travel.state.gov), and the UK Foreign Office asks you to make sure your travel insurance covers the activity you plan (FCDO). Keep every receipt and medical record, and read our guide on travel insurance and motorbike riding in Vietnam before you need it.

Tell the rental shop and the insurer

The compulsory insurance every registered motorbike must carry is a third-party liability policy governed by Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP. Article 7 says it compensates damage to the health, life and property of third parties and the health and life of passengers on the vehicle; the rider is not on that list. Article 6 caps compensation at VND 150 million per person for injury or death, and at VND 50 million per accident for property damage caused by a two-wheeler (Decree 67/2023).

Article 12 sets out what the policyholder and the insured person must do when an accident happens. They must notify the insurer at once through its hotline, do what they can to limit injury and damage, protect the scene and not move, dismantle or repair the property before the insurer agrees, unless that is needed for safety or required by the authorities. The insurer must explain the safety measures and the claim procedure within one hour of the notice and organise the loss assessment within 24 hours. A written or electronic notice must follow within five working days of the accident, and the insurer may reduce property compensation by up to 5% if it does not (Decree 67/2023).

As a renter you will usually not hold the policy, so phone the shop straight away. Ask for the insurer's hotline and the policy details, tell them what happened and ask what the rental contract says about damage to the bike before anyone repairs it or takes it away. Know the exclusions in Article 7: among other cases, the insurer does not pay when the driver had no licence or an invalid, expired or unsuitable one, when the driver deliberately fled without meeting the civil liability, or for property damage when the driver's blood or breath alcohol was above the normal level or drugs were used (Decree 67/2023). Our guides on which papers to carry on a rental bike and on foreign driving licences in Vietnam explain why the licence matters so much here.

What to keep

Article 13 lists what an insurer asks for in a claim: a written claim, copies of the registration certificate, the driving licence, an identity document or passport of the driver and the insurance certificate, medical evidence of injury such as an injury certificate or medical record, bills and receipts for repairs and for any costs spent to limit the loss and, in some cases, police documents on the investigation. The policyholder and the insured collect most of these, so start early (Decree 67/2023).

  • Photos of the scene, the vehicles, the plates, the road and any injuries, taken before anything is moved when it is safe.
  • Names, phone numbers and plate numbers of everyone involved and of any witnesses.
  • The names of the officers and the station that dealt with the accident, and a photo of every document you are given.
  • Medical records, receipts and prescriptions, kept together.
  • A written note of the times: when it happened and when you called the shop, the insurer, the police and your embassy.
  • Copies or photos of your passport, your licence and the rental agreement, kept apart from the originals.

Right of Way at Junctions and Roundabouts in Vietnam

Who has right of way at junctions in Vietnam: main road before side road, traffic from the right at unsigned junctions, from the left at signed roundabouts, plus fines under Decree 168/2024.

At a busy Hanoi junction it can look as if everyone simply goes at once. In fact the law sets out clear right-of-way rules, and one of them surprises many visitors: at a junction signed as a roundabout, traffic coming from the left has priority. This guide explains the rules in Article 22 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, your duties when turning, the order in which police signals, lights and signs apply, and the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For general advice on city riding, see our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders.

The starting point: look and slow down

Article 22 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety opens with a duty that comes before any question of priority. Anyone approaching a junction must observe the situation, reduce speed and give way according to the rules that follow. Riding into a junction without slowing down already breaks this principle, even if you believe you have priority.

Three rules for three kinds of junction

Article 22 distinguishes three situations (Law 36/2024/QH15):

1. Main road and side road. Where a non-priority road meets a priority road, or a branch road meets a main road, vehicles coming from the non-priority or branch road must give way to vehicles on the priority or main road, whichever direction they come from. 2. Junction without a roundabout sign. Give way to vehicles coming from your right. 3. Junction signed as a roundabout. Give way to vehicles coming from your left. When you enter a signed roundabout, you let through the traffic already approaching from the left.

The third rule is the one to remember. Before every large junction, look for the mandatory sign R.303, which tells all vehicles that want to change direction to go round the central island in the direction of the arrows (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). Where you see it, expect traffic from the left to have priority.

When you turn: pedestrians and oncoming traffic first

Turning comes with extra duties. Before you turn, Article 15(2) requires you to look around, keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down, signal and move gradually into the lane nearest to the direction you are turning; the indicator stays on throughout the turn. Article 15(3) then requires you to give way to pedestrians, non-motorised vehicles and oncoming traffic, and to turn only when you will not obstruct or endanger anyone.

Turning left across a big junction

For many visitors the hardest move is a left turn across a wide junction. Move into the left-hand lane of your direction early, signal well before the junction and let oncoming traffic through, as Article 15(3) requires. If there is a separate signal for left turns, wait for it. If the turn feels too much, there is a calmer option: ride straight across, make a U-turn where it is allowed and then turn right. It costs a minute and takes a lot of pressure off.

Police officer, traffic light, sign: what comes first

The right-of-way rules in Article 22 apply only when nothing else controls the junction. Article 11(2) sets a fixed order: first the signals of a traffic officer, then traffic lights, then road signs, then road markings and other guidance on the road. If an officer is directing traffic by hand, the officer's signals override even a green light. Under Article 11(3)(a), an officer raising the right arm straight up means that traffic from all directions must stop.

The lights themselves have their own rules. Under Article 11(4), green means go, but you must still slow down or stop for pedestrians and wheelchair users who are crossing; a steady amber means stop before the stop line unless you are already on or past it; a flashing amber means you may go, but you must watch out, slow down or stop to give way to pedestrians and other vehicles; red means stop.

Priority vehicles and railway crossings

Priority vehicles such as fire engines and ambulances that are sending their priority signals must never be obstructed. Failing to give way to them, or obstructing them, costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(đ) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, plus 4 licence points under Article 7(13)(b). Pull towards the right edge and let them pass.

A railway level crossing is a junction where the train always wins. When a crossing keeper signals, a red light flashes, a bell rings or a barrier moves or is closed, you must stop on the right-hand side of your road before the stop line (Article 24(1)). At crossings without a keeper, barrier, bell or light, you also stop before the stop line, look both ways and cross only when no train is coming (Article 24(2)).

What breaking the rules costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the following fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds:

  • Not slowing down and giving way when entering a main road from a side road or non-priority road: 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND under Article 7(6)(c), with 2 licence points under Article 7(13)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Not giving way to traffic from the right at a junction without a roundabout sign, or to traffic from the left at a signed roundabout: 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND under Article 7(6)(d), with 2 licence points (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Other breaches of the right-of-way rules at junctions: 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(đ) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Not giving way when turning to pedestrians on a marked crossing, oncoming traffic, or pedestrians and non-motorised vehicles crossing where there is no marked crossing: 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 VND under Article 7(5)(b) and (c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Ignoring a traffic light: 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 7(7)(c), with 4 licence points under Article 7(13)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Right of way in everyday Hanoi traffic

In dense traffic, priority is rarely negotiated with gestures. It works through predictable riding. If you roll into a junction slowly and steadily, other riders can read you; if you brake or accelerate suddenly, you throw everyone off. Hold your line, slow down early and expect that not everyone knows or follows the rules. Being in the right is little comfort after a collision.

Large roundabouts reward a plan. Enter slowly, keep to the side of your exit direction and signal early before you leave the circle. If you miss your exit, go round again instead of cutting across. If you are unsure, stop briefly before entering, watch the flow for a few seconds and then join a gap instead of forcing your way in. Frequently asked questions

Who has priority at a roundabout in Vietnam? At a junction signed as a roundabout, traffic coming from your left has priority, so when you enter you let through the vehicles already approaching from the left (Law 36/2024/QH15). Look for the mandatory sign R.303 before large junctions (QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT). Not giving way to traffic from the left at a signed roundabout is fined under Article 7(6)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, with 2 licence points.

Who has right of way at a junction without a roundabout in Vietnam? Give way to vehicles coming from your right, unless one road is a priority or main road (Law 36/2024/QH15). In that case, vehicles coming from the non-priority or branch road give way to vehicles on the main road, whichever direction they come from. Whatever the junction, observe the situation and reduce speed as you approach, as Article 22 requires before any question of priority.

Do I have to give way to oncoming traffic when turning left in Vietnam? Yes. Article 15(3) requires you to give way to pedestrians, non-motorised vehicles and oncoming traffic when you turn, and to turn only when you will not obstruct or endanger anyone. Move into the left-hand lane of your direction early and signal well before the junction. Not giving way to oncoming traffic when turning is fined under Article 7(5)(b) and (c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

In short

Before every junction: look and slow down. Then the main road goes before the side road; without a roundabout sign, traffic from the right has priority; at a signed roundabout, traffic from the left does (Article 22). When turning, pedestrians and oncoming traffic go first, and priority vehicles always do. Breaches cost between 200,000 and 6,000,000 VND under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, often with licence points.

Vietnam's Driving Licence Points System

How Vietnam's 12-point licence system works: which motorbike offences cost 2, 4, 6 or 10 points, how points are deducted and restored, and what it means for expats and visitors.

Since the 2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, every Vietnamese driving licence carries a points balance. For holidaymakers riding on an International Driving Permit it rarely matters, but for long-stay visitors and expats with a converted licence or one obtained in Vietnam it matters a great deal. This guide explains how the 12-point system in Article 58 of the law works, which common motorbike offences cost how many points, how points are deducted and restored under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP and what happens when all points are gone. For the paperwork side of renting, see our topic page on rental documents and contracts.

Twelve points per licence

Under Article 58(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, every licence has 12 points, held in the national road traffic order and safety database. How many points an offence costs depends on its nature and seriousness. The record is updated once the penalty decision takes effect, and the holder is notified.

The system applies to licences issued in Vietnam, including those converted from a foreign licence; our guide to converting a foreign licence explains that process, and our guide to taking the A1 test covers the other route. For International Driving Permits, Article 5(1)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP provides a different sanction: the right to use the permit can be withdrawn for a period.

Which motorbike offences cost points

Article 7(13) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the points deducted from motorbike and moped riders on top of the fine. Some examples:

  • 2 points: carrying three or more people, not giving way when turning, riding the wrong way down a one-way street or on the pavement, entering a road you are banned from (Article 7(13)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • 4 points: holding a phone, using an umbrella or earphones while riding, running a red light, ignoring a traffic officer's signals, speeding by more than 20 km/h, or alcohol at the first level (Article 7(13)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • 6 points: riding onto an expressway, or leaving the scene after an accident (Article 7(13)(c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • 10 points: alcohol at the second level, or offences that cause an accident under Article 7(10) (Article 7(13)(d) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Our guides to using a phone while riding, motorbikes and expressways and drink-riding rules give the amounts that apply to these offences.

How deductions work

Article 50 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets out the details:

1. Points are deducted once the decision on the offence takes effect. 2. If several offences are dealt with in one case, only the offence with the highest deduction counts. 3. If the licence has fewer points left than the deduction, all remaining points are deducted. 4. On a combined licence for motorbike and car, motorbike offences come off the motorbike part and car offences off the car part. 5. While a licence is suspended, no points are deducted.

Holders are notified in writing or electronically, for example through their account on the National Public Service Portal or the traffic app for citizens, and the information also appears on the traffic police website (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 50(3)).

A worked example

Here is how fast twelve points can shrink. Caught holding a phone in spring, you lose 4 points; running a red light in summer costs another 4 (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 7(13)(b)). Four points remain. One more 4-point offence in autumn, such as speeding by more than 20 km/h, empties the balance, because twelve months without a new deduction never passed in between. If two offences are dealt with in one and the same case, only the higher deduction counts under Article 50.

When points come back

The law offers two ways back to 12 points:

  • Automatically after 12 months: if you still have points left and 12 months pass after the last deduction without another one, your balance returns to 12 under Article 58(2). The database restores the points automatically and notifies you electronically under Article 51(2) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.
  • After a test, when all points are gone: if you lose all your points, you may no longer drive or ride on that licence under Article 58(3). No earlier than 6 months after losing them, you may sit a test of road traffic law organised by the traffic police; if you pass, your 12 points are restored.

One detail matters for expats: under Article 58(4), your points balance carries over when a licence is exchanged, reissued or upgraded to a higher class. A new card is not a fresh start.

Visitors on an International Driving Permit

If you ride only on an International Driving Permit, you have no Vietnamese points balance, but you are not free of sanctions. Besides fines, the right to use the permit can be withdrawn under Article 5(1)(g) of the decree. An International Driving Permit is also valid only together with your national licence: carrying it without the matching national licence costs 2,000,000 to 4,000,000 VND for motorbikes up to 125 cm³ under Article 18(5)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guide to International Driving Permits for motorbikes lists the documents you need.

Riding with no points left

Riding on a licence with no points left is treated like riding without a licence: Article 18(5)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP expressly covers riding a motorbike of up to 125 cm³ on a licence whose points have all been deducted, with a fine of 2,000,000 to 4,000,000 VND; above 125 cm³ it is 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND under Article 18(7)(b).

Keeping track

If you hold a Vietnamese licence, do not ignore penalty decisions, even when they arrive later by post or app; the deduction is booked once the decision takes effect under Article 50 of the decree. Pay fines on time, check your balance after each case and ride with extra care when few points remain. Our guide to traffic police stops explains what happens at a check, and on an electronic licence the balance is updated in the national e-ID app.

Frequently asked questions

Do my licence points reset when I renew or upgrade my Vietnamese licence? No. Under Article 58(4), your points balance carries over when a licence is exchanged, reissued or upgraded to a higher class, so a new card is not a fresh start. The law offers two ways back to 12 points: 12 months without a new deduction while points remain, or passing a road traffic law test after losing them all.

Does a licence converted from a foreign licence carry points in Vietnam? Yes. The points system applies to licences issued in Vietnam, including those converted from a foreign licence, and each has 12 points under Article 58(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety. Riders on an International Driving Permit have no Vietnamese points balance, but under Article 5(1)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP the right to use the permit can be withdrawn for a period.

How can I check how many licence points I have left? You are notified in writing or electronically, for example through your account on the National Public Service Portal or the traffic app for citizens, and the information also appears on the traffic police website (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 50(3)). On an electronic licence, the balance is updated in the national e-ID app, so check it after each case.

In short

Every Vietnamese licence has 12 points (Article 58). Typical motorbike offences cost 2, 4, 6 or 10 points; after 12 months without a deduction all points return, and if you lose them all you must wait at least 6 months and pass a test. The details are in Articles 7, 50 and 51 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Before your first ride, our rental pickup checklist helps you start on the right foot.

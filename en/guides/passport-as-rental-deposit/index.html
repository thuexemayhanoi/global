Leaving your passport as a rental deposit: risks and alternatives

What Vietnam's 2025 penalty decree says about pledging a passport, why UK travel advice warns against leaving it with rental shops, and safer alternatives to offer instead.

Some rental counters in Vietnam end the paperwork with the same request: leave your passport with us until the bike comes back. It sounds like a formality, but it hands a business control of your most important document for the whole rental. This guide explains what Vietnamese law says about pledging a passport, what the UK government advises, which alternatives to propose instead and what to do if a shop already has yours. For deposit amounts and refund terms, read our guide to rental deposits; for the full cost picture, see the rental costs and fees hub.

What Vietnamese law says about pledging a passport

Vietnam's penalty decree for security and public order, Decree 282/2025/NĐ-CP, has applied since 15 December 2025 and replaced Decree 144/2021. Its Article 21(3)(b) lists giving, renting, hiring out, pledging or accepting in pledge a passport or other entry and exit documents among the acts fined VND 3,000,000 to 5,000,000. Under Article 5(2), an organisation that commits the same act pays double the amount set for an individual (signed text on the Government portal).

Two things follow from that wording. It names both sides of the arrangement: the person who pledges the document and the person who accepts it. And it does not say in so many words whether a passport left with a rental shop as security counts as a pledge, while nothing in the decree carves out an exception for rentals either. If a shop insists on keeping your passport, you are being asked to join an arrangement the decree treats as an administrative violation, and declining is the sensible answer.

You are expected to have it with you

Handing the passport over also clashes with your own duties. Article 44 of the Law on Entry, Exit, Transit and Residence of Foreigners requires foreigners to carry their passport or international travel document, together with their residence papers, when travelling around Vietnam, and to show them when the authorities ask. Article 21(1) of Decree 282/2025/NĐ-CP punishes a foreigner who travels without these documents with a warning or a fine of VND 300,000 to 500,000.

Travel advice takes a more practical line. The UK government tells visitors: "You must always carry photographic ID. The authorities will accept a printed copy of the photo page of your passport and visa" (FCDO safety and security advice). That is the UK's reading of how checks work in practice, not a change to Vietnamese law. Either way, a passport sitting in a shop drawer is neither on you nor in your hotel safe.

What the UK government advises

The FCDO's Vietnam page is unusually direct on this point. Under road travel it says: "Do not use your passport as a deposit for hiring vehicles or as a guarantee you will pay a fine for a traffic offence." In the crime section it adds: "Do not give your passport to others as a guarantee – for example, to motorcycle rental shops or landlords. They can hold your passport against claimed damage." (FCDO)

That last sentence describes the real risk. A deposit is meant to cover damage you actually caused; a passport gives the holder leverage over whatever amount they claim, because without it you cannot complete the residence declaration at your next hotel or leave the country. The FCDO also reports that foreign nationals involved in traffic accidents have been stopped from leaving Vietnam until the police finished their investigations (FCDO), which is exactly the moment you want your documents in your own hands.

Alternatives to propose at the counter

Most shops want two things: to know who has the bike and to have something that covers damage. You can offer both without giving up the original:

  • A cash deposit against a written receipt. The receipt should show the amount, the plate number, the date and what may be deducted. Our deposit guide explains which refund terms to agree in advance.
  • Copies instead of the original. Offer a photocopy of the passport photo page and of your visa or entry stamp, the same printed copy the UK advice says the authorities accept.
  • A written rental contract. It should name you, the bike, the rental period and the deposit, so the shop has a record of who has its vehicle without holding your passport.
  • Photos at pickup. Photograph every side of the bike, the odometer and the fuel gauge before you ride away; that evidence keeps any later disagreement short.
  • Another shop. If the only security a shop will accept is your original passport, consider renting somewhere else.

If a shop already has your passport

If you have already handed it over, limit the exposure. Ask for a signed note that describes the document, with your name and passport number, and the date it will be returned. Photograph the data page before it leaves your hands, and keep the shop's address and phone number with you.

If the shop refuses to give it back, for example to push you into paying a disputed damage charge, do not negotiate alone. Decree 282/2025/NĐ-CP also fines anyone who unlawfully appropriates or holds another person's passport, under Article 21(2)(g), with VND 500,000 to 2,000,000. Call the police on 113 or go to the ward police station, and contact your embassy.

If it is lost or stolen while someone else holds it

A shop is not a safe, and if your passport disappears while someone else has it, the consequences are still yours. The FCDO says to report a lost or stolen passport immediately to the British Embassy in Hanoi or the Consulate General in Ho Chi Minh City (FCDO). Vietnamese law expects a report as well: Decree 282/2025/NĐ-CP fines a failure to notify the competent authorities about the loss of a still-valid passport, under Article 21(2)(a), with VND 500,000 to 2,000,000.

Replacing a passport also takes time on the Vietnamese side. France's foreign ministry warns that obtaining an exit visa from the Vietnamese authorities after losing a passport takes at least five working days, and potentially several weeks if you have stayed beyond your permitted period (France Diplomatie). Weigh that delay before the passport leaves your hands.

Hotels: show it, then take it back

Hotels are a different case. Article 33 of the same law requires accommodation providers to ask foreign guests to present their passport so that the temporary residence declaration can be sent to the police, within 12 hours in most areas and 24 hours in remote ones. The text speaks of presenting the document; once the details are recorded, asking for it back is entirely reasonable.

In short

Your passport is not a deposit. Vietnamese law (Decree 282/2025/NĐ-CP) lists pledging a passport, and accepting one in pledge, among fined acts; the UK government tells travellers not to leave it with rental shops; and you are expected to be able to identify yourself at any time. Offer cash against a receipt, copies and a written contract instead, and keep the original in your hotel safe or on you. Before you collect a bike, run through our pickup checklist.

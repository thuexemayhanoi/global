How to Pay a Traffic Fine in Vietnam

How to pay a traffic penalty in Vietnam: the deadline after you receive the decision, paying online through the National Public Service Portal, late charges and getting your papers back.

If you were stopped at a checkpoint in Vietnam, or a decision reaches you later through your rental shop, a few practical questions follow at once. Where do you pay? By when? And how do you get your licence or the bike's registration card back if the police kept them? On top of that, the forms and the payment portal are in Vietnamese. This guide walks through the deadline set by the Law on Handling Administrative Violations, paying online through the National Public Service Portal, what late payment costs and how held documents come back to you. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

From violation record to decision

At a traffic stop the police usually draw up a record of the violation, and a penalty decision is issued afterwards; the Hà Tĩnh provincial government describes these steps in its explainer on the procedure (hatinh.gov.vn). The decision is the document that matters. It names the violation and the rule it is based on, the amount, the deadline and how to pay. Keep both the record and the decision, and photograph them. Our guide to traffic police stops explains what happens at the roadside.

The deadline: ten days from receipt

Under Article 73(1) of the Law on Handling Administrative Violations, a penalty decision must be complied with within 10 days of receiving it, unless the decision itself gives a longer period (THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT). Filing a complaint or a lawsuit does not pause that duty, except in the cases the law sets out (THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT). The law has been amended several times since 2012, so if in doubt, go by the deadline printed on your decision.

What to check on the decision

Before you pay, read the decision through. It should show at least:

  • your name and the plate number of the vehicle,
  • the date, time and place of the violation,
  • the type of violation and the provision it relies on, which for traffic offences is usually an article of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP,
  • the amount and the deadline,
  • the issuing authority and the decision number.

If something is missing or unclear, ask the issuing office before the deadline runs out.

Paying online through the National Public Service Portal

Since 2020, traffic penalties can also be paid online through the National Public Service Portal (dichvucong.gov.vn). A step-by-step guide from LuatVietnam describes the process like this:

1. Open the portal, go to the online payment section and choose the payment of administrative penalties, then the search for traffic violations (LuatVietnam). 2. Find your case, either by the decision number together with the issuing authority, or by the violation record number with your name, the issuing office and the date of the violation. 3. Choose a bank and pay the amount. 4. Choose whether any held documents are collected from the authority's office or sent to you by post.

According to the same guide, people without a portal account can also pay online, but the details they enter must be complete and exact, because record numbers are not unique (LuatVietnam). The portal's interface may have changed since the guide was written. If you do not read Vietnamese, ask your accommodation or rental shop for help, and only pay through the channels named on the decision.

Getting your documents back

If the police kept documents when they recorded the violation, such as your licence or the rental bike's registration card, you get them back once the amount is paid. When paying online you can choose, as LuatVietnam explains, between collecting the papers from the authority and receiving them by post. On a rental bike, tell the shop straight away if the registration card was kept, because the bike should not be ridden without it. Our guide to which papers to carry lists what you need on the road.

What late payment costs

Missing the deadline costs more than the original amount. Every day of delay adds 0.05 percent of the unpaid sum, and the decision can be enforced, for example by deduction from income or from a bank account; that is how an explainer on the Hà Tĩnh provincial government portal sets it out with reference to the Law on Handling Administrative Violations (hatinh.gov.vn).

If a traffic case stays unresolved, the authority also informs the offices for vehicle registration and driving licences, which then hold back that person's applications to issue or exchange a licence until the case is closed (hatinh.gov.vn, citing Article 47(11) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). For expats with a Vietnamese licence that can be a real problem.

If you have already left Vietnam

Some decisions only reach travellers after they have flown home, often through the rental shop. The deadline still runs. Check the decision as described above and agree with whoever sent it how it can be settled. Online payment through the portal goes through a bank you select in the portal (LuatVietnam); if you have no suitable way to pay, you will need help from someone in Vietnam, such as the shop or your former accommodation. In that case, always ask for the payment receipt. If the shop wants to settle it from your deposit, our guide to rental deposits and refund terms explains what to ask for first.

Don't forget the points

Some violations also cost points on a Vietnamese licence, deducted as soon as the decision takes effect. Our guide to the licence points system explains how the 12 points work and how they come back.

Tips for travellers

  • Pay against a decision only. Pay the body named on the decision, never cash to someone at the roadside who offers to make the problem go away.
  • Keep the paperwork. Save the payment receipt and a copy of the decision, even after you leave.
  • Plan around your departure. If you fly out soon, settle an open decision first so the deadline does not lapse unnoticed.
  • Settle rental cases with the shop. If a decision comes through the shop, ask to see the full documents before paying.
  • Use a translation app. It helps you understand the Vietnamese text, but only pay once the amount, plate, date and place clearly match your case.

In short

A penalty decision must be complied with within 10 days of receipt unless it states a longer period (THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT). You can pay online through the National Public Service Portal and choose how held papers come back (LuatVietnam). Paying late adds 0.05 percent a day and risks enforcement (hatinh.gov.vn).

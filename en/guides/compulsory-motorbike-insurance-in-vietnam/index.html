Compulsory motorbike insurance in Vietnam: what it covers

What Vietnam's compulsory motorbike liability insurance covers under Decree 67/2023, its payout limits and exclusions, the certificate a rider must carry and why it does not protect you or the bike.

Every motorbike on Vietnamese roads must carry compulsory civil liability insurance, and a rental bike is no exception. Many visitors assume this means they are insured. They are not, at least not in the way they might hope: the compulsory policy protects other people from the damage the bike causes, and it comes with limits and exclusions that matter a great deal to a foreign rider. This guide explains what the policy covers under Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, how much it pays, when it pays nothing, what certificate you need to carry and how it fits with travel insurance. For the wider set of rental paperwork, see Rental Documents and Contracts in Hanoi.

Who must buy it

Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP requires motor vehicle owners to buy compulsory civil liability insurance from an insurer licensed to sell it (Article 4). For a rental bike, the owner is the rental business, so the policy is bought in its name and stays with the bike. The decree's definition of a motor vehicle owner also includes a person to whom the owner has lawfully handed possession and use of the vehicle (Article 3(1)), which is how a renter riding with the shop's consent falls within the scheme.

The insurance term is normally at least one year and at most three years under Article 9 of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, so a properly insured rental bike carries a policy that runs well beyond your rental period.

What it covers

Article 5 of the decree defines the insured risk as the owner's civil liability towards third parties and passengers. Article 7(1) spells out what the insurer pays for:

  • damage to the health, life and property of third parties caused by the vehicle, outside any contract,
  • damage to the health and life of passengers carried on the vehicle.

The rider is not a third party. Article 3 of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP excludes the driver, the people on the vehicle and the owner from that definition. In plain terms, the compulsory policy pays for the pedestrian you hit, the car you scrape and the injuries of your passenger. It does not pay for your own injuries or for damage to the rental bike.

How much it pays

The payouts are capped. Under Article 6 of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, the limit for injury or death is VND 150 million per person per accident, and the limit for property damage caused by a two-wheeled or three-wheeled motorbike or a moped, including electric ones, is VND 50 million per accident.

Anything above those limits is the liability of whoever caused the damage. A serious injury claim or damage to an expensive car can easily exceed them, and the excess can then land on the rider.

When it pays nothing

Article 7(2) of the decree lists the cases where the insurer is not liable. Two of them are especially relevant to visitors:

  • No valid licence. The insurer does not pay if the rider has no driving licence, uses an invalid or expired one, or uses a licence that does not match the vehicle. A rider whose licence has been suspended is treated as having no licence at all (Article 7(2)(c), Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP).
  • Alcohol or drugs. Damage to property is excluded if the rider's blood or breath alcohol exceeds the normal level set by the Ministry of Health guidance, or if the rider has used banned drugs or stimulants (Article 7(2)(đ), Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP).

The licence exclusion is the one that catches foreign riders most often. Which foreign licences and permits are valid in Vietnam is covered in our guide to foreign licences and motorbike classes. Without a valid licence, the compulsory policy simply does not respond, and a victim's claim can fall on you in full.

The certificate you must carry

The insurer issues one insurance certificate per vehicle under Article 10(1) of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, and Article 10(3) allows electronic certificates. The certificate shows, among other things, the owner, the plate number, the liability limits and the insurance period.

Riding without a valid certificate is fined. Under Article 18(2)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a rider of a motorbike or moped without a valid compulsory insurance certificate faces a fine of VND 200,000 to 300,000. Before you ride away, ask the shop to show you the certificate, check that the plate number matches the bike and that the dates cover your rental, and keep it with the registration papers; our guide to which papers to carry on a rental bike lists the rest.

If there is an accident

When an accident happens, the person who bought the insurance and the insured person must notify the insurer immediately (Article 12, Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP). On a rental bike, that means calling the shop at once so that it can contact its insurer, after you have dealt with the injured and called the police. Our guide to what to do after a motorbike accident sets out the order of steps at the scene.

Questions to ask the rental shop

A two-minute conversation at pickup tells you where you stand:

  • Is the compulsory certificate valid for my whole rental, and is it a paper or an electronic certificate?
  • Has the bike any voluntary cover on top? Article 4(3) of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP lets the buyer and the insurer agree wider conditions or higher amounts for an additional premium, but nothing obliges a shop to do so.
  • Who pays for damage to the bike itself? The compulsory policy does not, so the answer lies in the rental contract and in the deposit terms.
  • What should I do first if there is an accident? Save the shop's number and agree how you will reach it.

Why travel insurance still matters

Because the compulsory policy protects others, your own medical bills, an evacuation flight and damage to the rental bike all need cover from somewhere else. The UK government advises making sure your travel insurance covers your planned activity (FCDO). Our guide to travel insurance for motorbike riding in Vietnam explains which questions to ask your insurer, including how it treats licences and alcohol.

In short

Compulsory motorbike insurance under Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP covers third parties and passengers, up to VND 150 million per person for injury and VND 50 million per accident for property damage. It does not cover you or the bike, and it pays nothing if you ride without a valid licence. Check the certificate before you ride, carry it, and arrange your own travel insurance.

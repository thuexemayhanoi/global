Emergency Numbers in Vietnam: 113, 114 and 115

Vietnam's emergency numbers for police, fire and rescue and ambulance, what to say on the phone, what the law expects after a crash and when to call your embassy and insurer.

A crash, a fire, a travel companion who suddenly collapses: in moments like these every minute counts, and nobody wants to be searching the internet for the right number. Vietnam has no single emergency number for everything; police, fire and rescue and ambulance each have their own. This guide lists the numbers, explains what to say on the phone, sets out the duties that apply after a road accident and says when to contact your embassy and insurer. If you are riding a rental motorbike, save the numbers in your phone before your first ride. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The numbers to know

According to THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT, the four national hotlines are:

| Number | Service | Call it for | |---|---|---| | 113 | Police | Accidents with injuries or disputes, theft, threats, anything that needs the police | | 114 | Fire and rescue | Fires, and people who need to be freed or rescued | | 115 | Ambulance | Medical emergencies needing an ambulance | | 111 | Child protection | The national child protection line |

Save 113, 114 and 115 under clear names such as "Police VN" or "Ambulance VN" so you can find them instantly under stress.

Which number when

After a motorbike crash with injuries, call the ambulance on 115 and inform the police on 113. The French foreign ministry advises travellers to place themselves under police protection after an accident by dialling 113 and then to contact their embassy (France Diplomatie). If a vehicle is on fire or someone is trapped, 114 is the right number. A breakdown without injuries is not an emergency; your rental shop is the first contact, as our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains.

What to say on the phone

Emergency call centres usually work in Vietnamese, so keep your message short and clear:

1. Where? Street, house number, a landmark or the kilometre marker on a rural road. The location in your map app helps. 2. What? Accident, fire or medical emergency. 3. How many? How many people are hurt and whether anyone is unconscious. 4. Call-back: your phone number.

If you do not speak Vietnamese, ask bystanders to call for you. That is not an imposition: under Article 80(2) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, people present at an accident must help the injured and report the accident immediately to the police, a medical facility or the nearest People's Committee.

Your duties after a crash

If you are involved in an accident, Article 80(1) of the law sets clear duties:

  • Stop immediately, warn of the danger, leave the scene as it is, help the injured and inform the police, a medical facility or the People's Committee.
  • Stay at the scene until the police arrive, unless you or an injured person must get emergency treatment or your life or health is threatened; in that case, report to the nearest police or People's Committee straight away.
  • Give information about yourself, the others involved and the accident when the authorities ask.

Other drivers passing an accident must take injured people for emergency treatment under Article 80(4); priority vehicles and vehicles with diplomatic privileges are exempt. Our guide on what to do after a motorbike accident covers the first steps in more detail.

Moving the injured yourself?

In principle, the scene stays as it is. Article 80(3) allows a vehicle involved in the accident to be used to take an injured person for emergency treatment only if no other vehicle is available, and the positions of the vehicle and the casualty must be marked first, without changing or removing traces. If someone has died, the scene must be left as it is and the body covered.

If you witness a crash

Bystanders have duties too. Under Article 80(2), anyone present at an accident must help and give first aid to the injured, report the accident at once to the police, a medical facility or the nearest People's Committee, help protect the scene and the victims' property, and give information when the authorities ask. Without Vietnamese you can still help: warn approaching traffic, stay with the injured and ask locals to call 115.

Plan for longer distances

The German foreign office notes that medical care outside the big cities is inadequate and that there are no emergency doctors or air rescue flights within the country (Auswärtiges Amt). Call early, especially on trips to remote areas, and expect help to take longer than in a city.

Hospital, embassy and insurer

The UK's travel advice for Vietnam says your insurance should cover local treatment and unexpected medical evacuation, including an air ambulance if you need to be flown home, because medical bills can be expensive and the British Embassy does not pay them; if you are referred to a medical facility, contact your insurer quickly (GOV.UK health advice). Keep your insurer's emergency number to hand and keep every receipt.

After a serious accident, or if the police open an investigation, inform your country's embassy early; France Diplomatie names this step explicitly (France Diplomatie). Our guide to travel insurance and motorbike riding explains what to check in your policy.

Prepare before you ride

  • Save the numbers: 113, 114 and 115, plus the rental shop and your accommodation.
  • Know how to share your location: learn how to show and send your position in your map app.
  • Keep your phone charged: a power bank under the seat keeps it working when it matters.
  • Carry an emergency note: name, blood group, medical conditions and your insurer's contact help rescuers if you cannot speak.

Our rental pickup checklist reminds you to get the shop's emergency number at handover.

Frequently asked question

What if the operator cannot understand me? Stay calm, repeat the place and the word for the problem, and ask bystanders to take over the call. At the same time, call the rental shop or your accommodation; both can help translate on the spot.

In short

113 for the police, 114 for fire and rescue, 115 for an ambulance (THƯ VIỆN PHÁP LUẬT). After a crash, stop, help, secure the scene and report it; bystanders must help and report too (Article 80). After a serious accident, also inform your embassy and your insurer.

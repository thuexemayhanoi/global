Riding a Motorbike at Night in Vietnam

When headlights are compulsory in Vietnam, when you must dip them, what Decree 168/2024 fines for it, why foreign ministries advise against night riding and how to get home safely.

Hanoi does not slow down after dark. Street kitchens open, the lanes around West Lake fill up and many travellers want one more ride on the rental bike after dinner. Night riding comes with extra rules, though, and the risks shift. This guide explains when lights are compulsory and when you must dip them under Vietnamese law, the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, what European foreign ministries advise, and practical ways to get home safely. For the wider picture on safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

When headlights are compulsory

Under Article 20(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, riders must switch on the headlight between 18:00 and 06:00 the next morning, and also in fog, smoke, dust, rain or any other bad weather that limits visibility. The rule applies whether or not the street is lit. That is why it pays to check, when you pick up the rental bike, that the headlight, high beam, tail light and indicators all work.

When you must dip your headlight

Article 20(2) requires you to switch from high beam to low beam:

  • when pedestrians are crossing the road,
  • on stretches through residential areas where the street lighting is working,
  • when you meet oncoming traffic, unless an anti-glare median separates the carriageways,
  • when you turn at an intersection.

In the city this means riding on low beam almost all the time. High beam only makes sense on dark roads without oncoming traffic, such as rural roads between villages, and even there only until another vehicle appears.

What the penalties are

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the same fine of 200,000 to 400,000 VND for both mistakes in Article 7(1): riding without lights between 18:00 and 06:00 or in poor visibility (point g), and using high beam in the situations listed above (point h). The same range applies under point i to sounding the horn between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas and around hospitals, which Article 21(2) of the Law forbids.

Why foreign ministries advise against riding after dark

Several European governments explicitly urge caution. The French foreign ministry states: "Il est déconseillé de conduire la nuit", in other words, driving at night is not recommended. Portugal's foreign ministry advises travellers to avoid travelling at night where possible (Portuguese travel advice). Germany's Federal Foreign Office recommends not using cyclos and motorbike taxis (xe ôm) after dark. The UK travel advice points out that riding a motorbike in Vietnam carries higher risks and that inexperienced riders should not hire one.

The reasons are easy to see. Outside the centres many roads are poorly lit or not lit at all, potholes and roadworks appear late, and long-distance roads carry many lorries and coaches at night. In the northern mountains, tight bends, fog and animals on the road add to the risk. A tired rider on an unfamiliar road sees hazards too late.

Make sure the rental bike can be seen

Whether other road users see you at night is decided when you collect the bike. In a shaded doorway, check that the headlight is bright and switches between high and low beam, that the tail light and brake light come on when you pull either brake lever, and that all four indicators work. Check the mirrors and the horn as well. A tinted visor is pleasant by day but dangerous at night, so ask for a helmet with a clear visor, or ride with the visor open and clear glasses. If anything is missing, have it fixed before you leave or ask for a different bike. Our pickup checklist helps you not to forget anything.

On tour in the north: arrive before dusk

If you plan a trip into the mountains, set each day's distance so that you reach your stop before dark. Depending on the season the sun sets in northern Vietnam roughly between 5 pm and 7 pm, and in deep valleys it gets dark earlier. Allow generous time for breaks, photo stops and slow sections, and leave early in the morning rather than riding the last hour in the dark. In the cool season, fog and drizzle often roll in over the mountains, and then the lighting rule applies during the day as well.

Night-time risks in Hanoi

The city changes at night too. Streets are emptier and some riders go faster. Vehicles without lights appear at junctions, and narrow lanes often have no street lamps. Then there is alcohol: under Article 9(2) of the Law any riding with alcohol in the blood or breath is prohibited, so after an evening with a few beers the rental bike stays parked. Our guide to drinking and riding in Vietnam explains the rule in detail.

Fatigue is often underestimated. After a long day of heat, sightseeing and dense traffic, concentration drops and reaction times grow, especially when the ride back to your accommodation leads through neighbourhoods you do not know. Treat the ride home as a planned trip on a route you know, not a spontaneous detour. If your eyes start to feel heavy, a taxi is the better choice.

Speed matters more after dark, because you can only react to what your headlight shows you. The general limits still apply at night; our guide to motorbike speed limits in Vietnam lists them, but on unlit roads the safe speed is often well below the posted maximum.

How to get home more safely

  • Light and reflection: ride on low beam, wear light-coloured clothing or a reflective vest, and keep your visor and glasses clean so that oncoming lights do not dazzle you.
  • Adjust your speed: ride only as fast as allows you to stop within the distance your headlight shows.
  • Choose familiar routes: at night, ride only roads you have already ridden in daylight, and move long rural legs to the daytime.
  • Change plans in time: if you are still far from your accommodation after sunset, park the bike safely and take a taxi or a ride-hailing car.
  • Stay reachable: charge your phone before you set off and save the rental shop's number; the police emergency number is 113.

If the bike breaks down at night, push it off the carriageway, stay out of the traffic yourself and call the rental shop; our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down covers the next steps. If something more serious happens, after a motorbike accident in Vietnam explains the first steps, and stopped by traffic police on a rental bike explains how roadside checks work.

In short

Lights are compulsory between 18:00 and 06:00 and in poor visibility (Article 20), you dip your headlight in town and for oncoming traffic, and getting either wrong costs 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Several foreign ministries advise against riding after dark. If you do ride in the evening, go slower, stick to routes you know and leave the bike parked after a drink. For more on handling city traffic, read Hanoi traffic for first-time riders.

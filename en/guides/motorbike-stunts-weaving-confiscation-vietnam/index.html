Stunts and weaving on a motorbike in Vietnam: fines, suspension and confiscation

What Vietnamese law says about weaving, wheelies, riding with no hands and constant revving: the fines in Decree 168, licence suspension, impoundment and the tricks that lead to confiscation.

Vietnamese streets have their show-offs: a rider lifting the front wheel at a red light, a passenger filming a friend who rides with both arms in the air, a group weaving through traffic at night with the throttle wide open. Visitors sometimes take this for part of the scenery. The law treats it as some of the most serious behaviour on two wheels. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) bans these tricks outright, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP punishes them with fines of up to VND 10,000,000, many months without a licence and, for the worst of them, confiscation of the motorbike itself. This guide explains which acts are banned, what each one costs and why it matters even more on a bike that is not yours. For the wider safety picture, see our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law bans

Two parts of Law 36/2024/QH15 set out the tricks. Article 9(5) lists among the acts prohibited to every road user illegal racing, organising, inciting, helping or cheering on illegal races, and riding "lạng lách, đánh võng", that is weaving and zigzagging through traffic, or revving the engine continuously. Article 33(3) then names what a motorbike rider must not do, including:

  • letting go of the handlebars with both hands;
  • riding a two-wheeler on one wheel;
  • sitting sideways to ride, or standing or lying on the bike while riding it;
  • swapping riders while the bike is moving;
  • turning round to steer facing backwards, or riding blindfolded;
  • scraping the side stand or another object along the road while moving;
  • carrying a person standing on the seat or the luggage rack, or sitting on the handlebars.

The list ends with a catch-all for "other acts that disturb road traffic order and safety" (Article 33(3)(g)), so a trick that is missing from the list is not automatically allowed.

Weaving, revving and group speeding

Article 7(9) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines a motorbike rider VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 for each of these acts:

  • (a) weaving or zigzagging on the road, or using the side stand or another object to scrape the road surface while moving;
  • (b) riding in a group of two or more bikes above the speed limit;
  • (h) sitting behind the person in front and reaching round them to steer, unless the person in front is a child under six;
  • (i) riding in a convoy that obstructs traffic, unless the competent authority has issued a permit;
  • (k) using the horn or revving the engine continuously in residential areas or near hospitals and clinics, except priority vehicles on duty.

The fine is only the start. Under Article 7(12)(b) of the decree, all five acts also lead to suspension of the right to use the driving licence for 10 to 12 months. Under Article 48(1)(b), the police may also impound the motorbike before the penalty decision is made, to stop the violation at once. What impoundment involves is explained in our guide on when police can impound a motorbike.

Point (b) is the one that catches friends on a road trip. When two or more bikes ride together above the limit, each rider in the group can face this heavier band (Article 7(9)(b)). Our guides to speed limits and riding in a group explain how to plan a pace that stays legal.

Tricks that cost you the motorbike

For some acts the decree does not set a fine at all. It orders confiscation of the vehicle. Under Article 7(11) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, the motorbike is confiscated when the rider:

  • lets go of the handlebars with both hands, steers with the feet, sits sideways, lies on the seat, swaps riders while moving, turns round to steer backwards or rides blindfolded (point (a));
  • rides a two-wheeler on one wheel (point (b));
  • weaves or zigzags again as a repeat violation of point 9(a) (point (c)).

On top of confiscation, Article 7(12)(c) suspends the right to use the driving licence for 22 to 24 months, and Article 48(1)(b) lets the police impound the bike immediately.

Confiscation means the motorbike is taken by the State and does not come back to you. If the bike belongs to someone else, a friend, a host or a shop, losing it becomes your problem to settle with its owner. Read what your agreement says about traffic violations before you set off, and treat the bike as something you have to return.

When a stunt causes a crash

If weaving, scraping the stand, group speeding, steering from behind or constant revving (points 9(a), (b), (h) and (k)) causes a traffic accident, the fine rises to VND 10,000,000–14,000,000 under Article 7(10)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and Article 7(13)(d) deducts 10 points from the licence. How the points work is explained in our guide to Vietnam's licence points system, and what to do at the scene in our guide after a motorbike accident.

Without an accident, none of the acts in this guide carries licence points: Article 7(13) does not list them, and the decree relies on suspension instead.

Racing and cheering on races

Racing has its own article in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP:

  • gathering to cheer on, help or incite speeding, weaving, chasing each other on the road or illegal racing is fined VND 1,000,000–2,000,000 (Article 35(1)), which applies to spectators, not only to riders;
  • organising an illegal race is fined VND 40,000,000–50,000,000 for an individual (Article 35(2));
  • a rider who takes part in an illegal motorbike race loses the vehicle by confiscation (Article 35(3)(b)) and the right to use the driving licence for 22 to 24 months (Article 35(4)).

Standing on the pavement and cheering a late-night race is therefore an offence in itself, as well as a dangerous place to stand.

Passengers who stand or sit on the handlebars

Tricks often involve the passenger. A rider who carries a person standing on the seat or the luggage rack, or sitting on the handlebars, is fined VND 600,000–800,000 under Article 7(3)(e) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and the passenger is fined separately, VND 400,000–600,000, under Article 12(5)(a). How a passenger should sit instead is covered in our guide to riding pillion, and how many people may ride at all in our guide on how many people can ride one motorbike.

The penalties at a glance

Every act in the table also allows on-the-spot impoundment under Article 48(1)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Filming, groups and showing off: practical advice

  • The camera changes nothing. A trick done for a video is judged like any other. If you want footage from a moving bike, let the passenger hold the camera: the rider may not let go with both hands, and holding and using a phone while riding is banned as well (Article 9(6) of Law 36/2024/QH15). Our guide to using a phone while riding explains the limits.
  • Ride a group like a group, not a race. Agree on a pace below the limit, leave space and let the slowest rider set the speed. Riding side by side is banned separately (Article 33(3)(a)).
  • Leave the throttle alone in town. Revving in a residential street sits in the same band as weaving (Article 7(9)(k) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Our guide to using the horn covers the noise rules.
  • Keep the passenger seated. A passenger who stands up for a photo puts both of you in breach (Articles 7(3)(e) and 12(5)(a) of the decree).
  • Remember whose bike it is. On a borrowed or hired motorbike, confiscation means going back without the bike. No clip is worth that.

Frequently asked questions

Is riding with one hand illegal? The specific ban in Article 33(3)(d) of Law 36/2024/QH15 is on letting go with both hands. One-handed riding is not listed as such, but holding a phone in that hand is a separate ban, and you must stay in control of the bike at all times.

What if I do a wheelie on an empty road? The ban in Article 33(3)(d) and the confiscation in Article 7(11)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP do not depend on how busy the road is.

Do these offences cost licence points? Not unless they cause an accident. Article 7(13) of the decree lists no points for them; it uses licence suspension instead, and an accident caused by points 9(a), (b), (h) or (k) costs 10 points.

Can the police take the bike on the spot? Yes. Article 48(1)(b) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP allows impoundment for the acts in Article 7(9)(a), (b), (h), (i) and (k) and in Article 7(11) before the penalty decision.

Summary

Law 36/2024/QH15 bans weaving, zigzagging, constant revving and racing (Article 9(5)) and tricks such as riding with no hands, wheelies and riding sideways (Article 33(3)). Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP punishes the first group with VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 and 10 to 12 months without a licence, and the worst tricks with confiscation of the motorbike and 22 to 24 months without a licence (Article 7(9), (11) and (12)).

Ride predictably, keep both hands on the bars and keep your passenger seated. Before you set off, check the bike with our rental bike checklist.

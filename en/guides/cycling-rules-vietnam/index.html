Cycling in Vietnam: Traffic Rules for Bicycles

The traffic rules for cyclists in Vietnam: passengers, single file and lanes, lights at night, umbrellas and phones, loads, signals and one-way streets, and the bicycle fines in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Many travellers explore Vietnamese towns and the countryside by bicycle, from lakeside loops in the cities to lanes between rice fields. A bicycle feels like the most innocent vehicle on the road, and most visitors assume that the traffic rules are written for motorbikes and cars. They are not: cyclists have their own duties under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), and Article 9 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets out their own fines. This guide covers passengers, where to ride, lights, what you may not do on a bicycle, loads, alcohol and the penalties. For riding safety in general, see our page on riding safety and support.

Bicycles in the law

Article 34(2)(a) of Law 36/2024/QH15 defines a bicycle as a vehicle with at least two wheels, powered by a person through pedals or a hand crank, and lists it among the non-motorised vehicles (xe thô sơ). People who ride non-motorised vehicles are not "drivers" in the legal sense: Article 2(9) reserves that word for people operating motor vehicles, and the licence duty in Article 56(1) applies to drivers. No licence is needed for an ordinary bicycle.

The helmet duty in Article 31(3) applies to e-bikes (xe đạp máy) and their passengers, not to ordinary bicycles. The law therefore does not make a helmet compulsory on a pedal bicycle, but wearing one remains a sensible choice in busy traffic.

Passengers

Article 31(1) allows a cyclist to carry one passenger, or two when the second passenger is a child under seven. Carrying more people than allowed is fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(o) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, except when taking a sick person to emergency care.

Where to ride: single file, keep right, use your lane

Under Article 31(4), riders of non-motorised vehicles must ride in single file and, where a part of the road is provided for non-motorised vehicles, ride in it. Article 13(1) sends slower vehicles to the right of the carriageway, and on roads with marked lanes Article 13(3) puts non-motorised vehicles in the lane furthest to the right, with motor vehicles to the left.

The fines follow these rules. Not keeping to the right or riding in the wrong part of the road is fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and so is riding three or more bicycles abreast under Article 9(1)(g). The law asks for single file; the fine applies from three abreast.

Lights at night and in tunnels

Between 18:00 and 06:00, Article 31(4) requires a light or a reflective signal at the front and back of a non-motorised vehicle. Riding at night without them is fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(i) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. In road tunnels, Article 26(1) requires non-motorised vehicles to use lights or carry a light-emitting signal at any time of day.

What you may not do on a bicycle

Article 31(2) applies the motorbike riding bans in Article 33(3) to cyclists and the passenger bans in Article 33(4) to people riding with them. On a bicycle you may not use an umbrella or audio devices other than hearing aids, enter parts of the road reserved for pedestrians and other vehicles, let go of the handlebars with both hands, hold on to, tow or push other vehicles, or carry bulky items. Article 9(6) also bans holding and using a phone while riding any vehicle that is moving.

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP grades the fines for cyclists:

  • Using an umbrella or holding and using a phone, or carrying a passenger who uses an umbrella: 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(h) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Letting go with both hands, swerving suddenly in front of a moving motor vehicle or steering with your feet: 150,000 to 250,000 VND under Article 9(2)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Holding on to, towing or pushing another vehicle or object, or carrying bulky items: 150,000 to 250,000 VND under Article 9(2)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Weaving or racing, or riding on one wheel: 300,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 9(3)(a) and (b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Signals, one-way streets and giving way

Cyclists follow the same signs, lights and officers as everyone else. Under Article 9 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, stopping suddenly or turning without signalling first, ignoring signs or road markings, and failing to slow down and give way when entering a main road from a side road are each fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND (Article 9(1)(b), (c) and (n)). Ignoring a traffic light or an officer's signal, or failing to give way to a vehicle asking to overtake or to a priority vehicle, costs 150,000 to 250,000 VND (Article 9(2)(b), (d) and (đ)). Riding into a banned area or against the traffic in a one-way street costs 300,000 to 400,000 VND (Article 9(3)(c)).

Signalling a turn on a bicycle means using your arm. Article 15(2) requires non-motorised vehicles without indicators to give a hand signal in the direction of the turn and to keep it up throughout the turn. Our guides to traffic lights and one-way streets and pavements explain the signs and signals that apply to cyclists as well.

Loads

Under Article 31(5), goods on a bicycle must be secure and must not block traffic or the rider's view. They may not exceed one third of the vehicle's length, may stick out no more than 1 metre in front and behind, and no more than 0.4 metres on each side of the wheels. A backpack or a small basket is fine; a long object held across the handlebars is not.

Alcohol

Article 9(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans operating any vehicle on the road with alcohol in the blood or breath, and that includes bicycles. Under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, cyclists are fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND for up to 50 mg per 100 ml of blood or 0.25 mg per litre of breath (Article 9(1)(p)), 300,000 to 400,000 VND above that up to 80 mg or 0.4 mg (Article 9(3)(d)), and 400,000 to 600,000 VND above 80 mg or 0.4 mg, or for refusing a test (Article 9(4)(b) and (c)).

Expressways and parking

Bicycles may not use expressways: Article 25(3) bans non-motorised vehicles from them, and entering one is fined 800,000 to 1,200,000 VND under Article 9(5) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

When you stop, park where it is allowed. Leaving a bicycle on the road or pavement where this is not permitted, or parking it so that it blocks traffic on the road or on a bridge, is fined 100,000 to 200,000 VND under Article 9(1)(k) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

If there is an accident

Cyclists have the same duty to stop and help as other road users. Causing an accident and then not stopping, not keeping the scene as it is, not helping the injured, not staying at the scene or not reporting at once to the nearest police or People's Committee is fined 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 9(4)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Our guide on what to do after an accident explains the first steps, which apply on a bicycle too.

Riding safely among scooters

  • Be predictable. Hold your line, signal with your arm and avoid sudden swerves; scooters will flow around you if they can read you.
  • Keep right, but not in the gutter. Leave yourself room to avoid potholes and drains.
  • Check the bicycle first. Test both brakes, the tyres and the lights before you set off, especially on a borrowed or hired bicycle; the points in our pickup checklist work for bicycles too.
  • Plan around the heat. Ride early or late in the day; our guide to riding in the heat explains the warning signs of heat illness.

City traffic works much the same for cyclists as for scooter riders; our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders explains the flow.

Summary

Cyclists in Vietnam may carry one passenger, or two with a child under seven (Article 31(1)), must ride in single file and keep right, need lights or reflectors between 18:00 and 06:00 and share most of the motorbike riding bans (Article 31). No licence is needed and the helmet duty applies only to e-bikes. Fines under Article 9 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP run from 100,000 VND for small mistakes to 1,200,000 VND for entering an expressway.

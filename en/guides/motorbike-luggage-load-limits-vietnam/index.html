Luggage on a motorbike in Vietnam: what the law allows and how to pack it

Vietnam's load limits for motorbikes: 0.3 m beyond each side of the rack, 0.5 m behind it, 2 m high, the bulky-item ban for rider and passenger, the fines, and how to pack a bag.

Spend an hour in Vietnamese traffic and you will see a scooter carrying a fridge, a tower of plastic crates or a week of family shopping. It is easy to conclude that anything goes. It does not. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets size limits for loads on motorbikes and mopeds and bans both the rider and the passenger from carrying bulky items, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines both of them. For a traveller setting off on a multi-day loop with one backpack each, the rules are easy to meet once you know them. This guide sets out the numbers, the bans and the fines, then a practical way to load a small scooter. For the wider safety picture, see our hub on safer riding and breakdown support.

The three numbers in the law

Article 33(5) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the limits for goods carried on a motorbike or moped. A load may not stick out more than 0.3 metres beyond the width of the luggage rack on either side, or more than 0.5 metres beyond the back of the rack, measured against the rack as the manufacturer designed it. The top of the load may not be higher than 2 metres above the road surface.

| Direction | Limit | Measured from | |---|---|---| | Each side | 0.3 m | the side edge of the luggage rack | | Behind | 0.5 m | the back of the luggage rack | | Height | 2 m | the road surface |

Three things follow from the wording. The reference point is the rack, not the seat or the handlebars, so the first job is to find out how wide and how long the rack on your bike actually is. The numbers are a ceiling, not a target: a bag that reaches 30 cm out on both sides already makes the load about 60 cm wider than the rack, which is plenty to catch a mirror, a gatepost or a pedestrian in a narrow lane. And the rule is about size only. Article 33(5) gives no weight limit for luggage, so for weight you follow the bike's manual and any rating marked on the rack itself.

What the rider may not do

The size limits come with a list of banned behaviour. Under Article 33(3)(đ) of Law 36/2024/QH15, the rider of a motorbike or moped may not:

  • use the bike to tow or push another vehicle or object, or lead an animal;
  • carry bulky items;
  • carry a person standing on the bike or on the luggage rack, or sitting on the handlebars;
  • load goods beyond the prescribed limits.

The law does not put a number on "bulky" (vật cồng kềnh) in these articles. A sensible reading is anything that sticks out well beyond the bike, blocks your view or your mirrors, or needs a hand to hold it. A rider who steers with one hand while balancing a box with the other has no margin left for a pothole, and Article 33(3)(d) separately bans riding with both hands off the handlebars.

What the passenger may not do

The passenger has a list too. Under Article 33(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15, the person on the back may not:

  • carry bulky items;
  • use an umbrella;
  • hold on to, tow or push other vehicles;
  • stand on the seat or the luggage rack, or sit on the handlebars.

For travellers the first point matters most. A passenger with a large suitcase on their lap, or a long bag clamped under one arm, risks being treated as carrying a bulky item. There is also a simpler problem: a passenger who is holding luggage is not holding on. Honda Vietnam's owner's manual for the Wave Alpha tells passengers to hold the rider's hips or the rear grab rail, lean with the rider in turns and keep their feet on the footpegs, even when the bike is stopped (Honda Vietnam, Wave Alpha owner's manual). Our guide to riding pillion covers posture in more detail.

The fines for rider and passenger

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP fines each person on the bike for their own conduct.

  • The rider: holding on to, towing or pushing another vehicle or object, leading an animal, carrying bulky items, or carrying a person standing on the seat or rack or sitting on the handlebars is fined VND 600,000 to 800,000 under Article 7(3)(e) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.
  • The passenger: the same acts by the person on the back, including carrying bulky items, standing on the seat or rack or sitting on the handlebars, are fined VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Article 12(5)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

The second fine is imposed on the passenger personally (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP): riding on the back does not make the bulky-item ban the rider's problem alone. This guide quotes only the fines that name these acts; the size limits of Article 33(5) apply regardless of how a particular case is penalised.

The storage box has its own limit

Inside the bike, the manufacturer's figures are the ones that count. Honda Vietnam's owner's manual for the Wave Alpha gives the storage box a maximum load of 5 kg, and warns against putting flammable or heat-sensitive items, valuables or fragile things in it (Honda Vietnam, Wave Alpha owner's manual). The same manual says to use the helmet holder only when the bike is parked and never to ride with a helmet hanging from it. Other models have different figures, so look up the manual of the bike you are riding before you fill every space. Our guide to Honda Wave, Vision, Air Blade and Lead explains how the common models differ.

How to pack a small scooter for a multi-day trip

None of this needs special equipment, but it does need a few minutes of care before you leave.

1. Travel light. One soft bag per person is plenty for a loop of several days. Soft bags mould to the rack and the seat; hard suitcases do not and are awkward to strap down. If you have a big suitcase, ask your accommodation whether it can store it while you ride. 2. Heavy and low, close to the middle. Put the heaviest things at the bottom of the bag and strap the bag lengthwise along the rack, as close to the seat as possible. Weight high up or far behind the rear wheel makes the front light and the steering vague. 3. Use two straps, then check them. Run at least two straps or cords crosswise, or use a cargo net. Elastic cords stretch, so stop after the first few kilometres and tighten them again. 4. Keep the moving parts clear. No strap ends, bag corners or cords near the rear wheel, the chain or belt, the brake or the exhaust. Keep the rear light, the indicators and the number plate visible. 5. Mind the backpack on your back. A small daypack is easy to wear. A heavy trekking pack pulls your shoulders back, tires you on a long day and hides your indicators from behind; strap it to the rack instead. 6. Leave the passenger's hands free. A small bag on the passenger's back is no problem. Their hands belong on the rider's hips or the grab rail (Honda Vietnam, Wave Alpha owner's manual). 7. Pack for rain. Line each bag with a plastic sack or a dry bag. Our guide to riding in heavy rain covers the rest.

Test the load before the open road

After packing, push the bike a few metres and turn the handlebars from lock to lock. The steering should feel normal and the front wheel firmly planted. Then ride slowly along a quiet street, brake gently and swerve a little. A loaded bike takes longer to stop and leans differently in bends, so leave more distance than usual for the first hour. Our guides to braking on a scooter and riding bends and hairpins explain why.

Two people and luggage: weigh it up

A light bike carries a lot more than its own weight when you ride two-up with bags. Honda lists the Wave Alpha 110 at 96 kg (Honda Vietnam, Wave Alpha); two adults and two bags can easily weigh more than that. The bike still works, but it climbs more slowly, brakes later and tires its riders sooner. If you plan long mountain stages two-up, look at our guide on choosing an engine size, keep the daily distance realistic with our advice on fatigue and breaks, and check how many people may ride one motorbike. On the road, keep the bags closed and in sight; our guide to preventing theft has tips for parking with luggage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ride with a backpack on? A small daypack worn on the back is everyday practice. A large trekking pack is better strapped to the rack, both for balance and because a big load on your back could be seen as a bulky item under Article 33(3)(đ).

Is there a weight limit for the luggage rack? Not in Article 33(5), which sets sizes only (Law 36/2024/QH15). For weight, follow the bike's manual and any rating marked on the rack.

Can my passenger hold a shopping bag? A small bag that does not get in the way is common. A bulky item is banned for passengers under Article 33(4)(a) and fined under Article 12(5)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

Can I tow a friend's broken-down bike with mine? Towing another vehicle is one of the acts banned for riders under Article 33(3)(đ). Our guide to towing or pushing a motorbike explains what to do instead.

Summary

On a motorbike or moped in Vietnam, luggage may stick out no more than 0.3 m beyond each side of the rack and 0.5 m behind it, and may not stand higher than 2 m above the road (Article 33(5)). Riders and passengers may not carry bulky items, and each can be fined for their own breach (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Articles 7 and 12). Pack light, strap the weight low and central, keep the passenger's hands free and test the bike before the open road.

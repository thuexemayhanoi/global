Which Rental Bike Defects the Rider Is Fined For

Why the rider, not the shop, pays when a rental motorbike in Vietnam has a missing mirror, dead indicator, weak brakes or damaged plate, what each costs and how to check at pickup.

The left mirror is missing, one indicator is dead, the horn is feeble: rental motorbikes in Vietnam are regularly handed over with faults like these. Many renters assume that is the shop's problem. At a traffic stop it is not. Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP directs the penalties for a motorbike's technical condition at the rider, which means you. This guide shows what each defect costs, which papers you must carry and how to check at pickup that everything works. For the paperwork side of renting, see our topic page on rental documents and contracts.

Who pays: the rider

Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets the penalties for riders of motorbikes and mopeds who break the rules on a vehicle's condition, and it is explicitly addressed to the person riding. The basis in the law is Article 35(1): motor vehicles on the road must be registered, carry a number plate and meet technical safety and environmental standards. Whether the shop reimburses you afterwards is a matter for your contract; at the roadside, whoever is riding pays first.

What must work

For each of the following defects, Article 14(1) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets 400,000 to 600,000 VND:

  • Horn, number plate light, brake light and left mirror: missing or not working (point a).
  • Indicators: missing or not working (point b).
  • Dipped and main beam headlights: missing, not working or not as designed (point c).
  • Brakes: missing, ineffective or not meeting the technical standard (point d).
  • Rear-facing light: fitting a lamp that shines backwards is penalised too (point đ).

Almost all of these are visible at handover, which makes them easy to avoid.

Why these parts in particular

The list is not arbitrary. The brake light warns traffic behind you, the mirror shows who is overtaking, and the horn is, under Article 21(1) of the law, the tool for warning of danger or signalling that you intend to overtake. Indicators matter just as much: Article 15(2) requires you to signal before turning, and a dead indicator leaves the riders around you guessing. Without dipped headlights you may not ride through a road tunnel under Article 26(1), and at night you need lights anyway to be seen. Our guide to riding at night has more.

Dearer: papers, exhaust and a non-standard horn

Article 14(2) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND, among other cases, for riding without a valid vehicle registration certificate or with an expired one (point a), with a missing silencer or emission control, or above the exhaust and noise limits (point d), and with a horn that does not meet the technical rule for that type of vehicle (point đ). Points a to c also cost 2 licence points under Article 14(8)(a), and a non-standard horn must be replaced or restored under Article 14(6)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Dearest: the number plate

A missing plate, one that does not match the registration or one not issued by the competent authority costs 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND under Article 14(3)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. The same amount applies under point (b) to riding with a plate that is wrongly mounted, illegible, bent, covered or altered. Both cost 6 points under Article 14(8)(b), and in the first case the plate is confiscated under Article 14(5)(a). A bent or dirty plate is not a cosmetic issue.

Which papers you must carry

Papers count as well as condition. Under Article 56(1) of the law, you must carry the vehicle's registration certificate, a licence for the right class and proof of compulsory third-party insurance, among other things. Our guide to which papers to carry lists what the shop should hand you, and our guide to compulsory motorbike insurance explains the insurance certificate.

A five-minute check at pickup

Go through this at handover, ideally with the shop:

1. Lights: dipped and main beam, brake light (with both brake levers), plate light and all four indicators. 2. Horn: one short press. 3. Mirrors: both present, firm and adjusted so you can see behind you; at least the left one is compulsory. 4. Brakes: both bite well before the lever reaches the grip. 5. Plate and papers: plate secure, clean and legible; registration and insurance proof on board, with details matching the bike. 6. Tyres: look at the tread and pressure. A bald or half-flat tyre is a good reason to ask for another bike.

Photograph any defects and have them noted in the contract or fixed before you ride off. Our rental pickup checklist has every item ready to tick.

If something breaks on the road

If the brake light fails or a mirror snaps off during the trip, do not just carry on as if nothing happened. Stop somewhere safe, tell the shop and get it repaired; with lights and brakes it is about your safety, not only about the penalty under Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. If a headlight fails after dark, ride only as far as the nearest safe place where it can be fixed. Our guide on what to do if your rental bike breaks down explains the next steps.

Settle it with the shop

Who ultimately covers a repair, or an amount charged at a stop under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, is up to your contract. Agree at pickup how existing defects are handled, and keep photos and receipts. Our guides to rental deposits and to costs beyond the rental fee help you avoid disputes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay even if the defect was there at handover? At a stop, the penalty under Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP is addressed to the rider. Whether the shop pays you back depends on the contract, which is why documenting and fixing defects before you ride pays off.

Does this apply to electric scooters? Yes. Article 14 covers motorbikes, mopeds and similar vehicles, and under Article 34(1)(g) of the law mopeds include electric ones up to 4 kW. An electric scooter needs working brakes, lights, indicators, horn and mirror too.

In short

For a faulty horn, lights, indicators, left mirror or brakes, the rider pays 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP; riding without valid registration costs 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 VND, and plate defects 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 VND plus points. Check the bike at pickup and have defects recorded or fixed.

How Many People Can Ride One Motorbike in Vietnam?

How many people may ride one motorbike in Vietnam: one passenger as the rule, two only in set cases such as a child under 12, helmets for everyone, luggage limits and the fines.

In Vietnam you see whole families on a single motorbike, along with shopping, gas bottles and potted plants. Visitors soon ask whether three people may ride a rental bike, or whether the children can come along. The law is clearer than street life suggests. This guide explains the rules in Article 33 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the helmet duty for everyone on the bike, the luggage limits and the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

The basic rule: one passenger

Under Article 33(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the rider of a two-wheeled motorbike or moped may carry only one person. Two passengers at most are allowed in four cases only:

  • carrying a sick person to emergency treatment;
  • escorting a person who has broken the law, that is, by police or authorities;
  • when one passenger is a child under 12;
  • when the passengers are elderly, frail or disabled people.

For visitors that means two adults on one bike are allowed and three adults are not. With a child under 12, a rider may carry two passengers if one of them is the child.

Helmets for everyone

Article 33(2) requires rider and passengers to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the chin strap properly fastened. The duty applies to children too. Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP exempts only certain cases from the fine for a passenger without a helmet, including carrying a sick person to emergency treatment and children under 6. If you travel with children, still get each child a helmet that fits: an adult helmet does not sit securely on a child's head. Our guide to what to wear on a motorbike covers protective gear more broadly.

Children on a motorbike: what parents should know

That a child under 12 may ride as a second passenger under Article 33(1)(c) does not make every trip with a child a good idea. A child should be able to reach the footrests, hold on securely and stay awake and calm during the ride. Seat the child where an adult can keep an eye on them, and ride especially slowly and with anticipation. For small children who cannot yet sit securely, a motorbike in dense Hanoi traffic is not a good choice.

The helmet has to fit, too. A helmet that is too big slips at the first hard braking and then gives little protection. Buy or borrow a child's helmet with the child present so that it sits properly and the strap closes, and ask the rental shop whether suitable helmets are available.

What passengers must not do

Under Article 33(4), passengers may not:

  • carry bulky objects;
  • use an umbrella;
  • hold on to, tow or push other vehicles;
  • stand on the seat or the luggage rack, or sit on the handlebars.

Matching rules apply to the rider: under Article 33(3), the rider may not carry people standing on the bike or sitting on the rack or handlebars, and may not carry bulky objects.

Luggage: the limits

Article 33(5) sets clear limits for luggage. The load may extend no more than 0.3 metres beyond the width of the luggage rack on each side and no more than 0.5 metres behind the rack, and it may not be higher than 2 metres measured from the road surface. If you travel as two people with backpacks or suitcases, you will soon see how little room a scooter offers; our comparison of manual and automatic rentals helps you choose a bike for touring with luggage.

What it costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets these fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds:

  • Two passengers outside the permitted cases: 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 7(2)(g) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Three or more passengers: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • A rider without a helmet or with the strap open, and a passenger without a helmet outside the exceptions: 400,000 to 600,000 VND each under Article 7(2)(h) and (i) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Bulky objects, passengers standing or sitting on the rack or handlebars, holding on to other vehicles: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Three of you? The alternatives

If three or more adults travel together, a second bike is the obvious answer, provided both riders hold a licence valid in Vietnam. For short trips in the city, taxis and ride-hailing apps are often easier, especially at night or with luggage. And for excursions, a bus or train combined with a bike rented at the destination can make more sense than three people on two overloaded bikes. Our guide to which papers to carry lists what each rider needs.

Safety first

Even where the law allows two people, a passenger changes how the bike behaves. Braking distances grow, the bike responds more slowly and the weight shifts in corners. Practise starting and braking two-up on a quiet road before heading into city traffic. Passengers should hold on firmly, keep their feet on the footrests and lean with the rider in bends rather than against them. With children, extra care applies: a small child can fall asleep or move suddenly. Consider whether a taxi or ride-hailing car is the safer choice for short city trips.

Before setting off, check that your rental bike is set up for two: the passenger footrests must be there and folded out, and tyre pressure should suit the load. Ask the rental shop if you are unsure. For longer trips two-up, a bike with a little more power keeps hills from becoming a struggle.

A common question

Does the rental shop's permission change anything? No. The limits in Article 33 apply to the rider on the road, whatever the shop says or allows. If you are stopped with too many people on the bike, the rider is the one dealing with the police.

In short

One passenger is the rule; two are allowed only in set cases, such as with a child under 12 (Article 33(1)). Everyone on the bike wears a fastened helmet, luggage has fixed limits, and breaking the rules costs 400,000 to 800,000 VND under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP.

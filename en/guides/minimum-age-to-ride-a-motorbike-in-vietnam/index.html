Minimum Age to Ride a Motorbike or Moped in Vietnam

How old you must be to ride in Vietnam: mopeds up to 50 cm³ or 4 kW from 16, motorbikes with a licence from 18, how e-scooters are classed, the penalties and tips for families.

Families with teenagers and young travellers soon ask the same question in Vietnam: may a 16-year-old rent a scooter, and from what age is a proper motorbike allowed? You will see very young riders on the streets, but the law sets clear age limits, and they depend on whether the vehicle counts as a moped or a motorbike. This guide explains the age rules in the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the difference between the two vehicle classes, the penalties under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP and what all this means for families. For the paperwork side, see our topic page on rental documents and contracts.

Moped or motorbike: the distinction that matters

Vietnamese law distinguishes two kinds of powered two-wheeler. A moped ("xe gắn máy") is, under Article 34(1)(g) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, a two- or three-wheeled vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h; with a combustion engine its capacity may not exceed 50 cm³, with an electric motor its power may not exceed 4 kW. Anything above that is a motorbike ("xe mô tô") under point (e).

That matters to renters because the typical rental scooter in Hanoi is a motorbike in the legal sense: an automatic scooter with 110 or 125 cm³ is well above the moped limit. Genuine 50 cm³ mopeds and low-powered electric scooters are the exception. Our comparison of 50cc and electric scooters explains the options, and our guide to Honda's common rental models gives their engine sizes.

The age limits in the law

Article 59(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety sets two thresholds:

  • From 16 you may ride a moped.
  • From 18 you may obtain licence classes A1, A, B1 and B. Under Article 57(1), class A1 covers motorbikes up to 125 cm³ or 11 kW and class A anything larger.

Below 16, nobody may ride any powered two-wheeler. At 16 or 17, only a moped of up to 50 cm³ or 4 kW is allowed, never a motorbike.

Electric scooters: power is what counts

For electric scooters the deciding figure is motor power, not engine size. An electric scooter of no more than 4 kW with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h is a moped under Article 34(1)(g) and may be ridden from 16. Above that it is a motorbike, so the licence rules and the minimum age of 18 apply; up to 11 kW, class A1 is enough under Article 57(1). Both kinds are on Hanoi's roads and they can look alike, so ask to see the power rating on the registration certificate before a teenager rides off.

A moped needs no licence, but the rules still apply

Moped riders do not need a licence: Article 56(1) expressly exempts them. Under Article 56(4), however, they must know the traffic rules, be able to control the vehicle safely and meet the age and health requirements. A 16-year-old on a moped for the first time in Hanoi traffic will struggle to meet that standard, and the helmet rules apply to mopeds just as they do to motorbikes.

What the penalties are

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets graded penalties in Article 18:

  • Aged 14 or 15: riding a motorbike, a moped or driving a car brings a warning under Article 18(1) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Aged 16 or 17: riding a motorbike of 50 cm³ or more, or with an electric motor of 4 kW or more, costs 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Article 18(4)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Without the right licence: riding a motorbike of up to 125 cm³ without a valid licence costs 2,000,000 to 4,000,000 VND under Article 18(5)(a), and above 125 cm³ 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 VND under Article 18(7)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

The consequences after a crash can be much heavier than any of these amounts. Our guide to what to do after a motorbike accident explains the first steps if something goes wrong.

Health counts as well as age

Age is not the only condition. Article 59(2) requires everyone who drives or rides to be in a state of health suited to the vehicle, and Article 56(4) refers moped riders to the same requirement. Whatever your age, do not ride when you are exhausted, on medication that slows your reactions, or barely alert after a long travel day in the heat with jet lag.

What it means for families

For parents of children under 16 the answer is clear: they may not ride themselves, but they may ride as passengers. Under Article 33(1), a child under 12 may be carried as a second passenger on a motorbike, and everyone on the bike needs a properly fitted helmet.

Teenagers of 16 or 17 can only ride a moped of up to 50 cm³ or a low-powered electric scooter. Ask the rental shop specifically about engine size or motor power and check the figure on the registration certificate. A friendly "no problem" from the shop does not change the legal limits.

From 18, a motorbike may be ridden only with a licence of the right class that is valid in Vietnam. Our guides to foreign driving licences and International Driving Permits explain what that means for visitors.

Tips for young riders

  • Practise before the city. Even a moped takes practice; do your first laps on a quiet street or an empty square.
  • Avoid rush hour at first. Hanoi's evening traffic overwhelms experienced riders from abroad too.
  • Wear the helmet properly. Fasten the strap, keep the visor clean and choose the right size.
  • No passengers at first. A passenger lengthens braking distances and makes the bike slower to respond.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a minimum age for passengers? No minimum age applies to passengers, but the rules on how many people may ride and the helmet requirement in Article 33 do.

May my 17-year-old ride if I ride right next to them? No. The age limits apply whether or not an adult is nearby, and a motorbike above 50 cm³ stays off limits at 17.

In short

From 16 a moped of up to 50 cm³ or 4 kW is allowed in Vietnam, from 18 a motorbike with a licence of the right class (Article 59). Typical 110 or 125 cm³ rental scooters are motorbikes. Riders under 18 on one face a fine of 400,000 to 600,000 VND under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and riding without a licence costs much more.

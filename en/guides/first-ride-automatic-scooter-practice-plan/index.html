First Ride on an Automatic Scooter: A Practice Plan

A practice plan for your first ride on an automatic scooter: learning the controls, the stand, moving off, slow riding, a figure of eight, U-turns and an emergency stop before Hanoi traffic.

An automatic scooter such as the Honda Vision looks harmless: no gears, no clutch, just twist and go. That is exactly what catches beginners out. The throttle responds instantly, a small turn of the wrist sets the scooter moving, and a rider who is startled when pulling away and instinctively grips harder can end up in a wall or a parked car within seconds. This guide sets out a practice plan for your first hours, built on the UK's compulsory basic training syllabus for motorcycle and scooter riders and on the Oregon motorcycle manual. For an overview of choosing a bike, see our topic page on choosing a rental bike.

First: your licence and a place to practise

A practice plan does not replace a licence. Anyone riding a motor vehicle in Vietnam needs a valid licence for the vehicle class under Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15; the only exception is mopeds. A Honda Vision of around 110 cm³ is not a moped but a class A1 motorbike, the class that covers motorbikes up to 125 cm³ under Article 57(1)(a). Our guide to International Driving Permits for motorbikes explains which papers visitors need.

Practise on an empty, level space without traffic, such as a large car park outside business hours, and ask the rental shop where that is possible. The main road outside the shop is not a training ground.

Step 1: get to know the scooter standing still

UK compulsory basic training starts without riding at all. In its first part, novices learn the controls, including the throttle, front and rear brakes, indicators, electric starter, engine cut-out switch, lights, horn and instruments, and the difference between a twist-and-go scooter and a motorcycle with gears (GOV.UK, CBT element B). Go through them with the engine off:

  • Throttle on the right: turn it with the engine off to get a feel for its travel.
  • Brake levers: on most automatic scooters, the front brake is on the right and the rear brake on the left; ask the shop to confirm.
  • Indicators, horn, lights: find the switches without looking.
  • Starting: many scooters start only with a brake lever pulled; the shop will show you how it works on your model.

Step 2: checks and the stand

Basic training also covers simple checks before riding, such as the tyres and electrics, and getting the bike on and off the side stand and centre stand safely (GOV.UK, CBT element B). According to Honda Vietnam, a Honda Vision weighs about 93 kg and has a seat height of 761 mm (Honda Vietnam, Vision). Push it a few metres with the engine off to feel its weight and balance.

Step 3: riding position

The Oregon manual advises sitting with your back straight, keeping your head and eyes up and sitting so that your arms steer rather than prop up your body. Hold the grips firmly and start with your right wrist flat so that you can control the throttle; keep your knees close to the bike and your feet on the footboard while riding (Oregon DMV, Section 4). The flat wrist matters even more on an automatic: with the wrist bent downwards, a startled grip easily turns into an unintended handful of throttle.

Step 4: moving off, riding slowly, stopping

Now you ride. The practical part of basic training includes moving off, riding slowly, riding in a straight line and making a controlled stop using both brakes (GOV.UK, CBT element C). For your first laps that means:

1. Pull a brake, start the engine, keep the brake on. 2. Open the throttle gently while releasing the brake, and ride straight at walking pace. 3. Stop with both brakes, throttle fully closed. 4. Repeat until moving off and stopping feel calm and smooth.

Riding slowly is harder than it looks. Practise riding straight at walking pace without putting your feet down.

Step 5: figure of eight and U-turn

Next, basic training includes riding a figure of eight and a U-turn (GOV.UK, CBT element C). Start with a large figure of eight and make it smaller bit by bit. Look where you want to go, not at the ground in front of the wheel. In slow, tight turns the Oregon manual advises leaning only the bike and keeping your body upright (Oregon DMV).

Step 6: the emergency stop

Stopping as if in an emergency is part of the syllabus too (GOV.UK, CBT element C). Ride in a straight line, slowly at first, and on a signal brake to a halt with both brakes. According to the Oregon manual, the front brake can supply 70 percent or more of the stopping power, so always use both brakes and apply them smoothly rather than grabbing (Oregon DMV). Our guide on how to brake on a scooter goes into more detail.

Step 7: observation and signalling

Before you head out onto the road, looking behind must become routine. Basic training stresses using the mirrors, a shoulder check into the blind spot and a fixed sequence of observation, signal and manoeuvre (GOV.UK, CBT element C). In Vietnam, signalling before a turn is also a legal duty: under Article 15(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15, riders must watch, keep a safe distance, slow down and signal their intention before turning.

Then: quiet streets first, traffic later

Ride your first kilometres on quiet side streets at quiet times, such as early in the morning. Move into denser traffic only once moving off, braking, signalling and looking round happen without thinking. Our guide to Hanoi traffic for first-time riders explains how city traffic flows.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Throttle when startled. Gripping the bar in a fright turns the throttle. Practise opening your hand and braking when surprised.
  • Looking down. Watching the front wheel makes you wobble. Look where you want to go.
  • Feet on the ground. Do not drag your feet while moving; put them down only just before you stop.
  • Rear brake only. The front brake does most of the stopping; practise with both.
  • Too fast into corners. Brake before the bend, not in it.

Frequently asked question

How long should I practise? There is no set time. Practise until moving off, stopping, the figure of eight, U-turns and the emergency stop feel calm and automatic, and repeat the exercises the next day before riding in traffic.

In short

Learn the controls standing still first, then practise moving off, slow riding, stopping, a figure of eight, U-turns and an emergency stop in an empty space (GOV.UK, CBT). Sit upright with a flat right wrist and always use both brakes (Oregon DMV).

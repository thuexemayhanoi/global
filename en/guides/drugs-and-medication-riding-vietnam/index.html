Drugs, Medication and Riding in Vietnam

Why riding with drugs in your system is strictly banned in Vietnam, when riders are tested, what a positive test or a refusal costs and what travellers with medication should know.

Many visitors know that Vietnam has a zero-alcohol rule for motorbike riders. Fewer know that the law treats drugs and banned stimulants the same way, and that the penalties are among the heaviest a rider can face. Then come the questions travellers ask on the road: what happens in a drug test? Can I ride the morning after a joint if cannabis is legal at home? What about the medicines in my travel kit? This guide explains the rules in the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the penalties under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP and what official travel advice says. For an overview of riding safely, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What the law bans

Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists the acts that are strictly prohibited on the road. Clause 3 bans driving or riding with drugs or other stimulants banned by law in your body, and sets no threshold. Next to it, clause 2 bans riding with alcohol in your blood or breath. Both apply to every vehicle, including a rented scooter or electric moped. The same list includes illegal racing, weaving and holding a phone while riding (Article 9(5) and (6)); our guide to using a phone while riding covers the latter.

The key point for travellers: the ban is about the substance being in your body, not about whether you feel impaired.

When you may be tested

Drug tests happen at traffic checks and after crashes. Article 9(4) of the law bans failing to follow officials' instructions and inspection requests. After an accident, the law provides for testing explicitly:

  • Medical facilities giving first aid to people injured in a road accident must inform the police and test the blood of the drivers involved for alcohol, drugs and other stimulants (Article 81(3)).
  • Investigating an accident includes checking the drivers involved for alcohol, drugs and banned stimulants (Article 83(2)(a)).

So if you end up in hospital after a crash, expect your blood to be tested. Our guide on what to do after a motorbike accident explains the other steps.

What it costs

For motorbike and moped riders, Article 7(9) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets two offences:

  • Riding with drugs or banned stimulants in your body: 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND under point (e) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Refusing a drug test: also 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND, under point (g) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Both also bring a riding ban: under Article 7(12)(c), the licence may not be used for 22 to 24 months (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). Refusing the test leaves you no better off than a positive result. The same range and the same ban apply under Article 7(9)(d) and (đ) to the highest alcohol level and to refusing an alcohol test; our guide to drinking and riding explains the alcohol rules.

Drugs are a crime off the bike too

The traffic penalties are only part of the picture. The UK's travel advice for Vietnam says penalties for possessing, using, distributing or manufacturing drugs, including cannabis, are severe regardless of the amount, that you should expect a long jail sentence and heavy fines, including when transiting through the airport, and that the death penalty may be imposed depending on the severity of the crime (GOV.UK, Vietnam safety and security).

The same advice warns that police periodically raid nightlife venues suspected of drug trading and may test people present, and that they routinely test at private residences or parties when responding to a disturbance. A positive result may lead to criminal charges regardless of whether the drugs were taken in Vietnam or before entry (GOV.UK). It also warns that drugs sold in Vietnam may be fake, synthetic or laced with toxic ingredients, including the nitrous oxide balloons widely available in nightlife venues.

Whether cannabis is legal in your home country makes no difference in Vietnam. Turn down offers in the street or in bars.

Medication: what travellers should consider

Many travellers carry medicines for pain, travel sickness, sleep or allergies. This guide cannot judge whether a particular product affects your ability to ride. These principles still help:

  • Read the leaflet. Many patient leaflets include a section on driving. If it says the medicine can cause drowsiness or slow your reactions, do not take it before riding.
  • Ask before you travel. Check with your doctor or pharmacist whether you should ride while taking your regular medication.
  • Keep the packaging and prescription. Carry medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription or a doctor's letter; it makes questions easier to answer, including at a check.
  • If in doubt, don't ride. If you feel drowsy or dizzy after a tablet, leave the bike and take a taxi or ride-hailing car.

Insurance

A crash under the influence of drugs can also be very expensive. Check what your travel insurance says about accidents involving alcohol or drugs; our guide to travel insurance and motorbike riding explains what to look for.

Frequently asked questions

I smoked a joint two days ago. Can I ride today? We advise against it. Article 9(3) bans riding while banned substances are in your body, and you cannot reliably tell whether traces are still detectable. Consumption itself is also a crime in Vietnam.

Can I simply refuse the test? That is no way out. Refusing is an offence in itself and costs the same as a positive result under Article 7(9)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, with the same 22 to 24 month riding ban.

Does the riding ban apply to my International Driving Permit? Yes. Under Article 5(1)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, the right to use an International Driving Permit issued by a country party to the 1968 UN Convention on Road Traffic can be suspended, as well as national licences. Our guide to International Driving Permits for motorbikes explains what you need.

In short

Drugs and banned stimulants in your body are banned when riding (Article 9(3)). A positive test and a refusal each cost 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND and a 22 to 24 month riding ban (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP), and drugs are severely punished off the road too. Check your medicines before you travel and do not ride if you feel drowsy.

Overtaking on a Motorbike in Vietnam

How overtaking works on a motorbike in Vietnam: pass on the left, when passing on the right is allowed, how to signal by day and night, where overtaking is banned and the fines.

In Vietnamese traffic it can look as if motorbikes pass on every side. The law is more orderly than the traffic suggests, and knowing it makes you safer and easier for others to read. That matters most outside the cities, where you will want to get past lorries and buses on two-lane roads full of bends. This guide explains the overtaking rules in Article 14 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, the places where overtaking is banned, how to signal by day and night and the fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. For an overview of safe riding, see our topic page on safer riding and breakdown support.

What counts as overtaking

Article 14(1) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety defines overtaking as the situation on a road with one lane for motor vehicles in each direction, where a vehicle behind moves out to the left to get ahead of the vehicle in front. On roads with two or more marked lanes in the same direction, the lane rules in Article 13 apply instead: you use the appropriate lane and change lanes only where it is allowed, one lane at a time and with your indicator on.

Pass on the left; the right only by exception

The basic rule in Article 14(2) is simple: overtake on the left. Passing on the right is allowed only when the vehicle in front is signalling left or already turning left, or when a special-purpose vehicle is working on the road and there is no room on the left. Squeezing past a slower vehicle on its right, however common it looks in the city, breaks the rule.

When you may overtake

Under Article 14(3) you may overtake only if four conditions are met:

  • there is no obstacle ahead;
  • no vehicle is coming the other way on the stretch you need;
  • the vehicle in front is not itself signalling to overtake;
  • the vehicle in front has signalled right and moved over to the right.

The last point shows how the law sees overtaking: as a cooperation. The rider behind announces the move, and the rider in front makes room.

When someone overtakes you

Article 14(4) sets out the other side. When a vehicle behind wants to pass and it is safe, slow down, signal right and keep to the right until it has gone by, without obstructing it. If it is not safe, for example because of an obstacle or oncoming traffic, signal left to show that overtaking is not yet possible. On mountain roads that left signal is genuinely helpful to the lorry driver behind you.

How to signal

Under Article 14(5), announce the overtake by flashing your headlight or sounding the horn, keep your indicator on throughout the manoeuvre and keep a safe distance from the vehicles ahead and behind. In urban and densely populated areas between 22:00 and 05:00 you may signal only with the light, not with the horn. Our guide to horn rules explains when the horn is allowed at all.

Where overtaking is banned

Article 14(6) lists the situations in which you must not overtake:

1. when the conditions of Article 14(3) are not met; 2. on narrow single-lane bridges; 3. on bends with limited visibility; 4. on two-way roads near the crest of a hill with limited visibility; 5. at junctions and level crossings with railways; 6. when the weather or the road makes overtaking unsafe; 7. when a priority vehicle is coming; 8. on pedestrian crossings; 9. when pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing; 10. in road tunnels.

Any no-overtaking sign adds to this list. On the mountain passes of the north several of these points often come together: tight bends, crests and fog. Patience is the only right tactic there.

What it costs

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP sets these fines for riders of motorbikes and mopeds:

  • No signal before overtaking, or not keeping it on throughout: 200,000 to 400,000 VND under Article 7(1)(b) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Overtaking on the right where it is not allowed: 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(đ) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Overtaking where it is banned, or despite a no-overtaking sign: 800,000 to 1,000,000 VND under Article 7(4)(c) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).
  • Overtaking wrongly and causing an accident: 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 VND under Article 7(10)(a) (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP).

Weaving and riding abreast

In city traffic, overtaking blurs into lane-hopping. The law draws a clear line: Article 9(5) bans weaving and zigzagging through traffic, known in Vietnamese as "lạng lách, đánh võng". Under Article 7(9)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP it costs 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 VND, and Article 7(12)(b) adds a licence suspension of 10 to 12 months.

Riding side by side is banned too: Article 33(3)(a) prohibits motorbike riders from riding abreast, and riding three or more abreast costs 600,000 to 800,000 VND under Article 7(3)(k) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Groups ride in single file with space between bikes.

Practical tips for country roads

  • Hang back before you pull out. Leave enough room behind a lorry to see past it before you move out.
  • Commit, but never blindly. Overtake briskly, never at full throttle into a situation you cannot see.
  • Return only when you see it. Move back in once the vehicle you passed is fully visible in your mirror.
  • Make room for buses. If a coach presses from behind, keep right and let it pass rather than racing it.
  • Check over your shoulder. A quick look before every move protects you from motorbikes already passing you on the left.

Our guides to right of way at junctions and riding at night cover related rules.

A common question

Can I pass a car on the right in a jam? On a road with one lane in each direction, only when the car ahead is turning left, as Article 14(2) says. On roads with several marked lanes in your direction, the lane rules of Article 13 apply instead, and you move through traffic by using the lanes properly rather than by slipping past on the right.

In short

Overtake on the left; pass on the right only when the vehicle ahead is turning left (Article 14(2)). Never overtake on bends or crests without a view, at junctions, level crossings, pedestrian crossings or in tunnels, and at night in town signal only with the light. Fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP range from 200,000 to 1,000,000 VND, and far more after an accident. Before your first ride, our rental pickup checklist helps you check the lights and indicators.

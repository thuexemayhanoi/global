Rental checklist

Hanoi Motorbike Rental Checklist: What to Check Before Pickup

This checklist prioritises safety and clear rental terms. Keep a written record of what you agreed to, especially if the trip involves multiple days.

01

Before booking

Choose a vehicle you can legally and confidently operate. Confirm availability, delivery location, total quoted price and what is included. Ask for cancellation, return and maintenance arrangements in writing.

02

Choosing an automatic scooter or a 50cc bike

Check whether you can put your feet down, reach the controls and move an automatic scooter with the engine off. Confirm luggage space on the actual bike. For a bike advertised as 50cc, ask the provider to identify its engine capacity in the vehicle documents. An advertising label does not establish your driving eligibility or insurance cover. Verify the requirements for the particular vehicle and rider before paying.

03

Monthly rental: agree the end date and renewal process

Record the start and end dates, what the provider means by a month, and when renewal must be requested. Ask who arranges maintenance, who pays for each type of work and what happens while the bike is being repaired. Get early-return, replacement-bike, any usage limits and deposit-refund terms in writing. Compare quotes over the same intended period rather than multiplying an advertised daily rate.

04

Compare itemised quotes on the same basis

Separate the rental charge from a refundable deposit, pickup or delivery, fuel, included equipment and conditional extra charges. Record the agreed pickup and return times, how extensions are charged and how damage is documented. A refundable deposit is money to budget for, but is not automatically a rental expense. If the quote gives only one total, request a written breakdown before committing.

05

Old Quarter pickup: confirm an exact meeting point

Send your accommodation address and ask the provider to confirm a meeting point both parties can reach at the intended time. Establish whether pickup there is offered, whether it costs extra, whom to contact if the location changes and where the bike must be returned. An Old Quarter mention in a listing does not establish a shop location. Confirm any change of meeting point before setting out.

06

Before signing

Read the duration, deposit, identification requirements, insurance clauses and damage responsibilities. Clarify who responds to a breakdown, what counts as late return and how the deposit is refunded.

07

At handover

Check tyres, front and rear brakes, lights, indicators, horn, mirrors and locking system. Record mileage and fuel where relevant. Photograph existing damage and test the bike safely before accepting it.

08

Records to keep until the deposit is back

Keep these in one folder on your phone until the deposit has been refunded: the signed contract or written booking confirmation, the deposit receipt, the photos and video taken at handover (every side of the bike, the dashboard with mileage and fuel level, existing scratches), a photo of the registration card the provider shows you, and the provider's name, address and phone number. Note the agreed return time and place in the same folder. If something is disputed at return, dated photos and written terms settle most disagreements faster than memory.

09

Before riding

Verify current licensing and vehicle rules with an official source. Wear a suitable helmet, plan realistic distances, check the weather and never ride when tired or otherwise unfit.

10

If something goes wrong during the rental

If the bike breaks down, move it off the carriageway and call the provider before paying anyone else for a repair; ask who pays and whether a replacement bike is available. After a collision, help anyone injured and call 115 for an ambulance or 113 for the police: Vietnamese law requires the people involved to stop, give first aid and inform the authorities. Photograph the scene, the vehicles and any documents, and tell the provider and your insurer the same day. If the bike is stolen, report it at the nearest police station and keep a copy of the report for the provider and your insurer.

11

When returning

Follow the agreed time and location. Document the bike’s condition, settle itemised charges, retain receipts and request written confirmation of the deposit settlement.

Frequently asked questions

What should I inspect on a rental motorbike before accepting it?

Check the front and rear brakes, tyres, lights, indicators, horn, mirrors and locking system before you accept the bike. Record the mileage and fuel level where relevant, and photograph any existing damage. Test the bike safely as well, and keep a written record of what you agreed, especially if the trip involves multiple days.

How can I avoid a deposit dispute when I return the bike?

Keep dated photos and written terms until the deposit is refunded, because they settle most disagreements faster than memory. Store the signed contract or written booking confirmation, the deposit receipt and your handover photos and video in one folder on your phone. At return, document the bike's condition, retain receipts and request written confirmation of the deposit settlement.

What should I do if my rental bike breaks down or I have an accident?

Move the bike off the carriageway and call the provider before paying anyone else for a repair, then ask who pays and whether a replacement bike is available. After a collision, help anyone injured and call 115 for an ambulance or 113 for the police. Photograph the scene and tell the provider and your insurer the same day.

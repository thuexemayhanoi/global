Plan your rental

01

Choose the right vehicle

Manual, automatic, 50cc and electric two-wheelers have different handling and operating requirements. Consider your riding experience, route, luggage and applicable licence rules.

02

Compare the total cost

Ask about daily, weekly and monthly pricing, delivery fees, fuel, deposits and the exact rental clock. Request a written quote that includes return conditions and possible extra charges.

03

Understand the agreement

Check identification requirements, insurance, liability for damage, breakdown support and the deposit refund process. Do not assume every foreign licence is sufficient for every vehicle.

04

Inspect before you ride

Photograph existing marks and test both brakes, tyres, lights, indicators, mirrors and locks. Check helmet fit. Do not accept a vehicle with unresolved safety concerns.

How to use this guide before you rent

This English guide is organised around four topic hubs, and each one answers a single question. “Choosing a rental motorbike in Hanoi” explains which type of two-wheeler suits your experience, your route and your luggage. “Hanoi Motorbike Rental Costs and Fees” shows which items make up the total you will be asked to pay and which charges tend to appear only at the end of a rental. “Rental Documents and Contracts in Hanoi” describes what a rental provider will ask to see, what a written agreement should contain and which papers must travel with the bike. “Safer Riding and Breakdown Support in Hanoi” covers protective equipment, local traffic habits and what to do after a flat tyre, a theft or an accident.

If this is your first rental in Vietnam, read the hubs in this order rather than starting with a model you like. Begin with the documents hub, because driving eligibility and paperwork decide which vehicles are open to you at all. Then choose a vehicle class, compare complete rental terms rather than a daily figure, and finish with the rental checklist on the day you collect the bike. Each hub links to its supporting guides, so you can go as deep as your trip requires.

The guide is written for short-term visitors, for long-stay residents who want a vehicle for weeks or months, and for people who have never ridden a scooter before. It deliberately stops short of certain things: it does not take bookings, does not quote prices or deposits, and is not a rental shop. Current rules and commercial terms always come from official sources and from the provider you deal with. Every article lists the sources it relies on and carries a note explaining how it was produced, so you can judge for yourself how far to trust each statement.

  • Read the documents hub first: it tells you which questions about licences and paperwork to settle before anything else.
  • Choose a vehicle class that matches your experience and route, not the photo in the listing.
  • Compare the full set of rental terms in writing, including deposit handling and return conditions.
  • Run the rental checklist at handover and keep your own record of the bike's condition.

FAQ

FAQ

Is a monthly rental always cheaper?

A monthly quote can reduce the average daily cost, but compare the complete contract, maintenance arrangements and early-return terms.

Can I rent without a deposit?

Deposit policies vary by provider and vehicle. Confirm the amount and refund terms directly; do not assume a deposit-free rental.

Can tourists ride any motorbike?

Not automatically. Driving privileges depend on current Vietnamese rules, the vehicle class and your licence. Verify eligibility before riding.

Hanoi Motorbike Rental Costs and Fees Explained

What a motorbike rental in Hanoi really costs: published daily and long-term rates, deposits, late-return rules, fuel, insurance gaps and the 2026 traffic restrictions to budget for.

The daily price on a rental shop's sign is only the first line of what a motorbike costs you in Hanoi. Deposits, the way rental days are counted, late returns, fuel, parking, and above all damage and insurance can move the real total far more than the difference between two shops' daily rates. This guide breaks down each cost so you can compare offers properly and avoid paying twice for the same trip. If you are about to collect a bike, keep the Hanoi motorbike rental checklist open on your phone as well.

What a day of rental typically costs

Rental prices in Hanoi depend mainly on the type of bike, its age and how long you keep it. A public marketplace listing for October 2026 gives a useful reference point: semi-automatic bikes such as the Honda Wave Alpha, Honda Blade or Yamaha Sirius average about 120,000 VND per day, automatic scooters such as the Honda Vision or Air Blade about 180,000 VND per day, and a manual-clutch Honda Winner around 250,000 VND per day, according to Vexere's Hanoi price page. Individual offers on the same page start from 120,000 to 190,000 VND per day depending on the shop and model.

Those figures are a market snapshot, not a guarantee. Newer bikes, delivery to your hotel, included accessories and peak travel periods all push prices up, while older bikes and long rentals bring them down. Treat any quote you find online as the starting point for a conversation, and ask for the final price in writing before you hand over money.

Why longer rentals cost less per day

Most Hanoi shops price in tiers. One operator's published table shows a Honda Vision at 180,000 VND per day for one to five days, falling to 100,000 VND per day once the rental passes 25 days, and a Yamaha Sirius dropping from 130,000 VND to 80,000 VND per day over the same range, as shown in Motogo's Hanoi rate table. The same table lists battery-swap VinFast electric scooters at similar levels to petrol bikes of the same class.

The practical lesson: if you plan to stay for several weeks, ask for a weekly or monthly rate instead of multiplying the daily price. Check what the longer rate includes. A monthly deal that excludes maintenance or punctures can end up costing more than a slightly higher price with servicing included.

How rental days, late returns and one-way trips are counted

A "day" usually means 24 hours from the moment you collect the bike, not a calendar day. Shops listed on the Vexere marketplace commonly charge an extra full day when you return more than 24 hours late, and half a day when you are only a few hours late; the grace period varies between shops, typically three to six hours. If your flight or train leaves early in the morning, agree on the return time before you sign so that a dawn drop-off is not billed as an extra day.

One-way rentals cost extra because the shop has to bring the bike back. For example, collecting a bike in Hanoi and leaving it in Ha Giang carries a 400,000 VND service fee on Motogo's published price list. Some shops do not allow one-way trips at all, and others restrict where you may ride: several listings on Vexere state that their bikes may only be used within the former Hanoi city area.

Deposits: cash, ID or passport?

Almost every shop wants security for a bike worth many times the rental price. Deposits on one public listing page range from 500,000 VND to 3,000,000 VND per bike, sometimes reduced if you can show a return flight or student card, and some shops ask foreign customers either to leave a passport or to pay a 3,000,000 VND cash deposit, according to Vexere's listings. A few operators advertise rentals without a deposit, usually with other conditions attached.

Choose a cash deposit with a written receipt over leaving your passport. The UK government is explicit: do not use your passport as a deposit for hiring vehicles or as a guarantee for a traffic fine. Without it you cannot check into most accommodation, and you are in a weak position if there is a dispute about damage. Before paying a deposit, agree in writing on when it will be returned, in which currency, and which deductions the shop may make.

Fuel, parking and the small costs that add up

Fuel is usually your responsibility. Bikes are often handed over with very little in the tank and should be returned at the same level; ask the shop to note the gauge on the contract. Pump prices in Vietnam are adjusted periodically, so check the current rate on Petrolimex rather than relying on an old blog post. A 110 cc scooter used in the city consumes little, but day trips out of Hanoi add up.

Parking is the cost most visitors forget. Guarded parking lots outside markets, museums, cafés and hotels charge a small fee per visit and give you a ticket that you must keep. Fees are higher at night and at events, and leaving a bike unattended on the pavement invites theft or towing. Smaller extras include a second helmet, a rain poncho, a phone holder and a lock; some shops include a helmet and a little fuel, others charge for each item.

Damage, theft and insurance: the costs that can dwarf the rental

The largest bills in a motorbike rental come from accidents, scratches and theft, not from the daily rate. Before you ride away, photograph and film every side of the bike, including existing scratches, the mirrors, the tyres and the odometer, and send the files to the shop by message so they are time-stamped. Ask how repairs are priced and whether there is a fixed charge for common damage such as a broken mirror or a scratched panel.

Insurance works differently from what many travellers expect. Every motorbike must carry compulsory third-party liability insurance under Decree 67/2023/ND-CP, and it is illegal to ride without it, as the UK travel advice points out. That policy covers damage you cause to other people, not the rental bike and not your own injuries. Ask to see the insurance certificate and the registration document, and ask what happens if the bike is stolen: some shops expect the renter to pay the full value.

Your own travel insurance is the other half of the picture. Many policies only cover motorbike accidents if you hold the right licence for the vehicle. Vietnam is a party to the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, and British riders, for instance, need the 1968 international driving permit together with their UK licence. Permits issued under other conventions may not be recognised, so check your own country's position before you rent, because an invalid licence can leave you paying every medical and repair bill yourself.

Hanoi's 2026 traffic restrictions and your budget

Since 1 July 2026 Hanoi has been piloting a low-emission zone inside Ring Road 1. During the first phase, which runs until 31 December 2026, the area designated Zone 1 in Hoàn Kiếm ward is closed to all motor vehicles, motorbikes included, from 19:00 to 24:00 every Friday, Saturday and Sunday, according to the project published on the government portal. Later phases extend the zone and, from 2028, keep motorbikes that do not meet emission standard level 3 out of the whole Ring Road 1 area.

For a short city visit this mostly affects where you park in the evening. If you are staying in the Old Quarter for a weekend, budget for guarded parking just outside the closed area, or walk and use ride-hailing for evening plans. For long rentals that stretch into 2027 and beyond, ask whether the bike meets current emission standards, or consider an electric model.

A cost checklist before you book

  • Ask for the full price in writing, including how the rental day is counted and the late-return rule.
  • Agree on the deposit form, the refund timing and the deductions the shop may make, and keep the receipt.
  • Check whether helmets, a lock, a rain poncho and any fuel are included.
  • Confirm where you may ride and whether one-way returns are possible and at what fee.
  • See the registration document and the third-party insurance certificate, and ask who pays if the bike is stolen.
  • Record the bike's condition with photos and video before leaving, and repeat the process when you return it.

A clear agreement on these points matters more than saving a few thousand dong on the daily rate. For the step-by-step handover routine, use the rental checklist, and for an overview of renting a motorbike in Hanoi start from the English guide home page.
